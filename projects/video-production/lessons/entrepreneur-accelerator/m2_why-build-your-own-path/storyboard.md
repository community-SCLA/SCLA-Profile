## Design Keys

- **Canvas rhythm is light-heavy in the middle, dark at the turns.** `navy` / `navy-deep` own the title card (1), the AI-acceleration turn (7–9), the "not to avoid the work" turn (12–13), the two releases (18–20) and the close (25); `paper` / `cultured` / `fill-subtle` own the stereotype row (2–3), the experiment field (4–6), the human-elements panel (10–11), the module rail (14–17) and the question (21–24). Alternating the canvas every two to four beats is what keeps twenty-five beats from reading as one long scene.
- **Gold lands in six beats only — 1, 3, 9, 17, 20, 25** — the title rule, the small first step, the completed five-help set, the finished module route, the oversized numeral and the closing rule. That is six of twenty-five, inside the one-in-four ceiling, and this list is exactly the set of beats whose token column cites `gold`. Everywhere else the accent is `blue`, so gold still reads as arrival by the time the close lands.
- **Dimming is canvas-specific.** `muted-video` is a light-canvas token and is used only on light beats (2, 11, 14–17, 21). On the dark canvases there is no muted token in `tokens.yml`, so dimmed content is instead stepped down toward `navy-deep` — the same `paper` type composited toward the canvas colour, no new hue introduced (7, 9, 12, 18, 19). `muted-video` is the nearest named token and is deliberately not used there, because at video scale it sits too close to `navy` to separate.
- **Type roles are fixed per scene class:** `display` only on the title card, `stat` only on the single numeral in beat 20, `quote` for the lines the narration leans on (3, 7, 12, 18–19), `heading` for scene headings and the closing line (25), `body` for card and list copy, `label` for eyebrows, chips, the brandline and the numeral's caption. No role is borrowed across classes, and no beat gives one line two text roles.
- **Two components carry the argument, each learned once.** `chip` is the "one of several" vocabulary — the five experiments (6), the three ways AI helps (13) and the five modules on the rail (14–17), where four sit dimmed and one is lit so the rail still reads as a sequence with an end; `point-marker` is the enumerated-list vocabulary (8–9, 11). Those two are the whole component set for this lesson — no third circle vocabulary is introduced anywhere in the video.
- **The rule-wipe row runs exactly once, at beat 6.** Beat 13's chips lay out on open field and beat 14's rail draws in one continuous pass before any module lights, so neither repeats the device.
- **On-screen copy is the spoken key phrase, never the transcribed sentence** (3, 10, 18, 19), and where copy compresses the script it keeps the hedge that makes the claim honest — "for most people", "does not replace", "you do not need to know whether".
- **Nothing appears before its word.** Every card, marker, chip, caption and line names the onset it is keyed to, read from `audio/narration.words.json`; each item is its own entrance under the stagger cap, and no beat holds static for more than ~2.5s before its boundary.
- **Every scene reserves a column or band for illustration that text never enters** — glyphs sit above their captions (2, 4–5, 13), illustrations hold the opposite half from the text column (3, 10, 18, 22–24), and the rail cards alternate above and below the rail (14–17) so no two ever share a band. The bottom band stays empty for footer chrome throughout, until the brandline takes it at 25.
- **Motion follows the tokens' contract literally:** every entrance settles inside 1.2s on a 0.12–0.18s stagger with the ease varied per element; outgoing content exits before incoming content lands (3, 10, 19, 21) so two illustrations never coexist; each scene resolves with one cued `closing-beat`; the only ambient movement is the finite bloom in beat 7 — nothing repeats indefinitely, and the dark fields use radial falloff, never a linear gradient.
