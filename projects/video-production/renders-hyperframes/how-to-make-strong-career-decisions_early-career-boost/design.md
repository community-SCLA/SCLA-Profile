# design.md — brand truth for `how-to-make-strong-career-decisions_early-career-boost`

Design source of record for this one video. Brand values are LOADED from this
workspace's own `tokens.yml` copy (colors, type floors, spacing bands, the
pinned Oxana voice); nothing here restates a hex value a gate also reads.

Lesson: `lesson-scripts/early-career-boost/ready/how-to-make-strong-career-decisions_early-career-boost.txt`
Lane: freeform (agent-native) — no templates, no `scenes.json`, no compiler.
Judge-selected concept: `_concepts/how-to-make-strong-career-decisions_early-career-boost/CONCEPT.md`
(Pitch B, the peg-and-thread decision board, with Pitch A's outmatched-not-deleted
tally columns grafted in). This file sharpens that contract against the real
timings; it does not replace it.

## Concept angle

> ONE decision board is on screen from the first frame to the last held frame —
> it gains a rule, a scored boundary, pins, punched strips, a thread and a
> cleared window as the process runs, then strips back to five punched holes in
> its left rail; every idea in this lesson is another mark made on, or read off,
> that same board.

**Persistence: beats s01 → s27, i.e. 100% of the runtime.** The board ground and
its left rail are drawn in beat 1 and are never cleared, never rebuilt and never
left. There is no second illustration anywhere in this video.

**The rule, stated out loud:** *if an element cannot be justified as another way
of reading the same board, it does not exist.* Nothing enters this frame except
as a mark ON the board, a hole IN the rail, or a line of copy ABOUT what the
board just did.

## The carrying object, part by part

Each part depicts exactly one thing the script names, so the drawing
instructions are unambiguous:

| Part | Depicts | Drawn as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The board** | the decision itself | full-bleed deep ground with a faint square rule, drawn flat, one light source — no texture, no grain, no paper |
| **The rail** | the five-step process | a narrow vertical strip down the left edge; it gains ONE hole per step, punched on the way past and never remarked on, and at step five one heavy run down its whole length |
| **The step path** | the process actually walked | a stepped polyline off the rail's peg, drawn tread by tread at s06 and struck through at s07, because most people never walk it |
| **A stray post** | a mark anchored to nothing | a tall flat bar with a short foot, set at a wrong angle; four of them are RINGED at s05 — what the process catches that you would otherwise miss |
| **An empty slot** | room for an option not yet considered | a dashed seat ring on the board; it is the board's own room, and it disappears exactly as a pin seats in it |
| **A pin** | one option under consideration | a filled accent circle in a solid seat ring with a short offset shadow; identical and bare when it arrives, because it has not been rated yet |
| **A punched strip** | the criteria a pin was rated against | a small rounded outline under a pin holding SIX square cells, each filled or hollow. Present/absent only — **no number is printed anywhere on frame** |
| **The criteria bar** | the factors that matter | a bar with a gold leading edge holding **exactly six** slots: written down empty at step three, filled one per factor as the narration names them. Six because the voice names six; a bar of five would put a count on screen the narration contradicts |
| **The scored boundary** | the decision, clarified | an inset rectangle with corner ticks; a small notched tag with an **empty** ruled date line clips to its top-right corner |
| **Stipple** | uncertainty | irregular dots at varying opacity in the right third; "clearing" it means replacing stipple with clean rule inside a hard-edged window — a measurable ink change, never a glow |
| **A thread** | the decision made | a stroked path with one soft bend; the committed thread is drawn at double weight, the heaviest line in the video, exactly once |
| **The tally columns** | the pro-and-con list | two short columns of flat marks with ONE mark grossly oversized and a bracket under it; drawn at the start, pushed outside the boundary at step one, and **never erased**. The same shape returns once, as the pair of "mood" marks struck through at s22 — the board showing the opening mistake in its own language |

Second guard from the judge, honoured: nothing on this board weighs, tips,
balances or compares two sides — the sibling lesson already shipped that device.
The strip is a present/absent punch card, and what it produces is spatial
ranking, not a beam finding level.

## Milestone frames — the accumulation contract

Percentages are of the computed runtime; each milestone introduces a **kind of
mark not present at the previous one**, never merely more of the same mark.

- **~25% (beats s06–s07, "most people do not use any process at all").** A large,
  mostly empty ruled board. Left of centre: the two tally columns, one mark at
  twice the weight and height of its neighbours with a bracket under it,
  shouldering the others out of line. Stray posts float at wrong angles, four of
  them ringed by the process at s05. Off the rail's peg, a stepped path is walked
  tread by tread and then struck through, and a loose gut curve is drawn across
  the lower board instead. The right third is grey stipple, densest at its inner
  edge. **No pins, no boundary, no strips, no thread.**
- **~50% (beats s11–s14, step two, "widen your options").** A scored boundary now
  frames the working area with the dated tag clipped to its top-right corner.
  The strays, the step path and the gut curve are swept. The tally columns have
  been pushed OUTSIDE the boundary to the bottom-left and stay there for the rest
  of the video. Inside the boundary, **empty slots open across the whole field**
  and pins seat into them one at a time — three by the end of step two, the rest
  as the narration pushes for more paths. **Two holes** in the rail. Stipple
  unchanged. It reads as: order arrived before material did.
- **~75% (beats s17–s19, step four, the cheap experiment).** The board is crowded.
  Pins in three ranked rows across the working surface, **a punched strip under
  every pin**, the filled cells stepping down row by row so the sort order is
  readable across the room. Three pins sit past the boundary's inner margin — the
  overrun from widening, never tidied away. The criteria bar carries six filled
  slots. One thread arcs from a high row out into a dashed risk band and back;
  inside it, one hard-edged window of grey has resolved into crisp ruled surface
  with a check struck in it. **Four holes** in the rail.
- **100% (beats s23–s25, the closing loop), in two moves.** First the whole board
  at once — boundary, tag, the full overrun population, the ranked strips, the
  filled criteria bar, the cleared window, the struck mood marks, one **heavy
  inked thread** from the rail's start peg to a single pin with a review tag
  hanging off it, and the return arc redrawn at double weight so the loop
  visibly closes on the peg it started from. This is the only frame in the video
  that holds every accumulated layer, and it is unreachable at 0:10 by
  construction. Then the strip-back: the threads lift, the pins withdraw, and
  the held final image is the scored boundary, **five holes in a clean vertical
  column** down the rail, and — still outside the boundary, unchanged since the
  30% mark — the two tally columns with their one oversized mark.

## What accumulates

1. **Population** — 0 pins → empty slots opening across the field → pins seating
   into them in authored waves, the last three overrunning the inner margin and
   staying overrun.
2. **Internal detail** — bare pins gain a punched strip each, and the criteria
   bar those strips are read against is written empty and then filled one slot
   per factor the voice names. The top row's strips print BEFORE the re-sort and
   the rest print after it, so the dense stretch gains detail and population at
   the same time (the judge's guard against "population growth mistaken for
   accumulation").
3. **A wholesale re-sort** — pins travel exactly once, into ranked rows, as one
   authored positional change at a beat boundary. The arrangement a viewer
   memorised at 50% is gone at 75%.
4. **Legibility bought back, partially** — one window of stipple resolves; the
   rest stays grey permanently, because the script says you rarely need certainty.
5. **Structure on the rail** — one hole per step, five by the end, never
   explained while they appear.
6. **Then subtraction** — the final move removes almost everything.

## Frame

- 1920×1080. Every content-bearing mark sits inside x ∈ [130, 1780] and
  y ∈ [130, 950], well inside the safe-area, frame-padding and content-bottom
  bands `tokens.yml` declares and `check_ink.py` grades on real pixels.
- The copy band is y 140→330 (heading, then sub-line). The board's marks live in
  y 360→930. Copy and drawing never share vertical space, so no line of text ever
  sits on top of a mark.
- The rail occupies x 150→196. The scored boundary spans x 430→1740, y 405→930;
  its inner margin is x 464→1706, y 439→896. Ranked rows sit at y 510/615/720,
  columns at x 540/780/1020/1260. The criteria bar is x 1020→1520, y 412→474.
  The stipple field is x 1320→1780. The tally columns end at x 205→421, y 817→925.
- The program eyebrow is the only element in the top keep-out, inside the
  `chrome-regions` rectangle `tokens.yml` declares by name.
- The board ground and its faint rule are drawn in `navy` on `navy-deep`: a
  luma difference of ~5, far below the ink gate's local-contrast threshold, so a
  full-bleed ground creates no ink in any keep-out band.

## Motion

- Entrances only: 0.35–0.6s, `power3.out` / `power2.out`, cued to the word
  timestamp that earns them. Nothing settled re-animates in place; there is no
  `repeat`, no `yoyo` and no idle wobble anywhere in this build.
- **Every beat lands a reveal after its own midpoint** (`build_board.py` prints
  the count and names any flat beat). Revision 1 exists because the first cut
  animated its headline for the first half of each beat and then changed 0.00%
  for the second half.
- **No mark is ever left at a partial value.** The first cut dimmed one pin to
  0.42 opacity and held it there for 3.1s, which reads on real pixels as a node
  stuck mid-fade. Marks are at 0 or at their stated alpha; `build_board.py`
  also reports any reveal whose fade window is sampled by the graded snapshot
  grid, so a still can never catch a prominent mark half-drawn.
- **Marks are sized to be measurable.** A stroke under ~2px at under ~0.25 alpha
  on this ground moves an 8×8 grid cell by less than `check_diversity`'s DELTA
  of 4 — invisible to every pixel gate in the lane, and to a viewer on a phone.
  Structural strokes are 2–3.5px at 0.30–0.9.
- The board is only ever *marked, punched, re-sorted or lifted from* — it is
  never rebuilt and never cleared.
- The one wholesale positional change (the re-sort into ranked rows) lands as a
  single authored move at a beat boundary, not as idle motion on settled content.
