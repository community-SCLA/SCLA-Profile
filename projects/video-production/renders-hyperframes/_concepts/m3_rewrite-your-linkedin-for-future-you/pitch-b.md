# PITCH B — m3_rewrite-your-linkedin-for-future-you

**Lens: accumulation-first.** The build-up was designed before the object was
named. Runtime estimate: ~330 spoken words → **~145s (2:25)** including the
1.8s final hold; at the ≥8 beats/min floor that is ~20 beats. Marks below are
given at 30 / 60 / 90 / 120 / close, and the four milestone frames at
25 / 50 / 75 / 100% land at ~36s / ~72s / ~108s / ~145s.

---

## The accumulation ladder

Two registers gain, and they hand off. The first half accumulates **the object**
(volume, then structure). The second half accumulates **the world under it**
(distance, then detail). Because one register is always climbing, no beat is
allowed to be the previous beat re-labelled.

**By ~30s** — *narration is at "that's your first impression, and it's working
whether you've shaped it or not."*
The frame has gained **volume where there was none**. It opens on a dead flat
sheet lying across the grass — the largest thing in frame and doing nothing.
By 30s the first air is in it: a long low lobe has lifted off the ground, the
mouth is being held open, and the flat sheet is now a rounded mass. It has also
gained an **outside**: three small figures at the field edge are already
looking, before anything has been shaped for them.

**By ~60s** — *"a strong headline and summary does three things" → "for your
headline, you can use a simple structure."*
The frame has gained **structure and density**. The blank fabric now reads as
gores and seams, and three load tapes run its full length, crown ring to mouth
ring, each one landing on its own anchor at the basket rail. Where 30s had one
undifferentiated mass, 60s has a mass with three named lines through it, and
the tapes — not the fabric — visibly carry the weight.

**By ~90s** — *"same person. But now the profile points forward."*
The frame has gained **stance**, and what faces us swaps. Until now the craft
lay on its side with a small hand-tag on the basket rail turned toward us,
naming only the field it is standing in. At 90s the same craft comes upright:
nothing was added or replaced, but the tag is now edge-on and the standing
envelope is what the field sees. First frame in the video with a **horizon
below the object** rather than behind it.

**By ~120s** — *"it connects your past to your future" → "in this module,
you'll draft…"*
The frame has gained **altitude, and therefore ground**. The tether is off. The
launch field is no longer the whole bottom of the frame — it is one patch among
several, and hedgerows, a lane and rooftops have appeared beyond it. The
balloon's own shadow lies across the grass, touching the launch mark behind and
the open field ahead in one unbroken shape: the first thing in the video that
is **newly connected** rather than merely bigger.

**By close (~145s)** — *"you make it easier for new doors to open."*
The frame has gained **reach**. The densest picture in the video: a patchwork
country filling the lower half — many fields, hedgerows, a river, lanes, gates
standing open — with the launch patch still identifiable and the three watchers
now three dots on it. The envelope has stopped gaining; the world has not.

---

## The carrying object

**A hot-air balloon envelope — inflated from a flat sheet of fabric on the
grass into a standing, flying craft.**

Drawn technically, not whimsically: the envelope is built from gores with
visible seam lines and three load tapes in brand accent running crown-to-mouth,
the basket and burner as clean line-work, the crown ring and mouth ring as real
hardware. Flat brand fills, no gradients, no cartoon face on the basket, no
festival bunting. The ground is a flat tinted patchwork — fields as plain
shapes, hedgerows as strokes — so that "more detail below" is achieved by
adding countable elements, never by adding texture noise.

---

## Four frames

**25% (~36s) — horizontal, and dead.**
The envelope lies on its side across the full width of frame, half-filled: a
long low lobe of gores, mouth held open, burner cold, basket tipped on its side
with a small hand-tag lashed to the rail. Ground line low, sky empty. The object
owns most of the canvas and still isn't doing anything. Three figures stand at
the field edge, looking. Nothing is above the horizon.

**50% (~72s) — radial, and structural.**
A three-quarter view up the throat of the now-full envelope toward the crown
ring. The picture is no longer a horizontal mass but a **radial** one: the three
load tapes converge on the crown ring at the centre of frame, each drawn as a
distinct accent line, each traced back down to its own anchor at the rail. Close
enough to see seams. Still on the ground, still tethered — the whole frame is
about how the thing is made, not where it is.

**75% (~108s) — upright, with distance behind it.**
The craft stands. Envelope in the upper two-thirds, basket just clear of the
grass, tether slack. The horizon has dropped, so for the first time a band of
country shows behind the crew: hedgerows, a lane, roofs. The balloon's shadow
lies on the grass, running from the flattened launch mark behind it to the open
field ahead. Three wind layers are indicated as three faint drifts at three
heights, going three ways.

**100% (~145s) — aerial, and dense.**
Aloft. The envelope stays large and high in frame, burner lit; below it the
lower half of the canvas is a full patchwork country — fields, hedgerows, a
river, lanes, gates standing open onto them. The launch patch is still there,
small, with three dots on it. From this height the seams and hardware have gone
soft and the only thing still readable on the envelope is the three load tapes.

Four different pictures by construction: **flat-horizontal → radial-close →
upright-with-horizon → aerial-with-country**. No two share an orientation, a
camera distance, or a subject.

## The payoff beat

The closing ~12s, on *"because people can immediately see how your experience
and strengths match the roles and challenges you're excited about next."*

Two things resolve at once. First, the frame contains **both ends of the story
for the only time in the video** — the launch patch and the far horizon in one
picture, which was physically impossible to draw at 0:10 because neither the
craft nor the altitude existed. Second, the object **re-reads an earlier
element**: the three load tapes introduced at 50% as internal structure are, at
this distance, the only part of the envelope still legible. The thing you built
into it is the thing that carries at range. That is the argument of the lesson
delivered by the drawing, not by a heading.

---

## Why this is not lazy

- **Every beat has a named physical delta**, and they are measurable by a
  builder without judgment: envelope fill state, seam/gore visibility, tape
  count anchored, tether state (held → slack → gone), horizon height in frame,
  and count of ground elements below. A beat that cannot state which of those
  changed is not a beat.
- **Heading swaps against a static prop are impossible here**, because the
  object changes *orientation* three times (horizontal → radial → upright →
  aerial). No milestone frame can be produced by re-labelling the previous one;
  each needs a different drawing.
- **The shuffle test passes by construction.** Both accumulators are monotone —
  the envelope only ever gains fill and structure, the ground only ever gains
  elements — so a contact sheet sorts itself into time order from the pictures
  alone.
- **It fails in neither rejected direction.** Against the *slow* cut: nothing
  re-animates in place; motion is entrance-only and the settled envelope is
  pixel-static once it has arrived — richness comes from the object earning
  more, never from the picture swapping faster. Against the *thin* cut: the
  carrier is never a small mark on an empty frame; it holds the majority of the
  canvas from 0:00, and the closing frame is the densest in the video, not the
  emptiest.
- **The persistence is earned.** The 50% structural beat is set up specifically
  so the 100% beat can cash it. Cut the payoff and the tapes become decoration —
  which is the test that the object is doing argumentative work.

**Fidelity guards.** No third-party interface anywhere: no profile card, no
platform chrome, no avatar photo, no product blue. The two headline strings the
script quotes appear only as the script's own words — the "before" string on the
small hand-tag at the basket rail, the "after" string along the standing
envelope's band — never inside a mocked-up product surface. Every count on
screen traces to the narration: three watchers = recruiters, managers and peers;
three load tapes = the three things a headline does and the three parts of the
structure; three wind layers = a promotion, a lateral move, or a role redesign.
The closing frame deliberately shows an **uncounted** number of open gates,
because the script says "new doors" without a number.
