# PITCH A — m3_rewrite-your-linkedin-for-future-you

**Lens:** metaphor-first

## The carrying object

**A hand-typesetting bench: a full case of metal type sorts, one composing stick, and the flat galley bed the finished block gets locked into.**

Drawn as flat shapes, no texture painting: a sort is a small upright rectangle with a darker face-bar on its top edge; the case is a fixed grid of those rectangles parked along one side of the frame; the composing stick is a shallow U-channel (two long bars and one end stop) that things get slid into; a *rule* is a thin full-width bar with no face; the chase is an open rectangle frame with four wedge-shaped quoins at its corners. The whole bench — case, stick, galley — is on screen and in its final position by the second beat and never moves again; everything after that is sorts migrating out of the case and into the channel. Colour does one job only: an unset sort is flat and faceless, a set sort carries the ink-dark face-bar.

## Four frames

**25% — the worn slug alone.** The bench is fully drawn: the case of sorts sits packed at the left, the composing stick sits empty in the middle of the galley. One single old slug — a low, flat bar with a tired face — lies on the bed by itself. It is the only set thing in a frame full of unset material. Three empty channel divisions are chalked into the stick beside it, waiting.

**50% — three segments and two rules.** The stick is now loaded: three distinct runs of sorts, left to right, with a thin rule bar driven in between each pair so the divisions read at a glance. The old worn slug has been lifted out of the bed and parked below the stick, so both lines sit in one frame at once and you can see one is short and faceless and the other is three-part and dense. The case at the left is visibly gappier than it was.

**75% — the block grows, the proof gets marked.** The headline slug has been lifted from the stick and locked at the top of the galley. Beneath it, a taller block of set lines is building, line by line, in the bed — the summary. Two or three of its sorts carry a bright face instead of the ink-dark one, and they are the only bright things on screen. Lying half over the bed's edge is a pulled proof sheet with hand-marks scored across it, and one sort is out of the block, held above the gap it came from, ready to go back corrected.

**100% — locked, inked, pulled.** The chase frame is down around the whole block with its four quoins wedged tight. A roller bar has crossed the face and every face-bar in the forme now reads ink-solid as one continuous surface. Beside the locked forme lies the pulled impression: one sheet, the three-part line across the top, the block beneath it, legible as a single object from across the frame. The case at the left is now mostly empty sockets.

## What accumulates

Sorts. There is a finite, visible population of them and it only ever moves one direction: case → stick → galley → locked forme. Every beat the case has more empty sockets and the bed has more set material, so the frame's density physically migrates from left to right across the runtime. On top of that migration, three state changes stack and never reverse: the rule bars go in and stay in, the chase comes down and stays down, the ink crosses the face and stays. Nothing re-enters the frame; nothing swaps out. At 25% one flat slug sits on an empty bed beside a full case — at 100% a locked, inked forme fills the bed beside an emptied case. You could restore the beat order from the socket count alone.

## The payoff beat

The final beat: **the impression is pulled.** Every sort that was set one at a time over the whole runtime becomes one flat readable sheet in a single move — the whole object re-read at once, in a form that can be read by anyone at a glance. That frame is literally undrawable at 0:10, because at 0:10 the stick is empty and there is nothing to print. It also lands exactly where the narration lands: what was assembled piece by piece is now something other people can take in immediately.

## Why this is not lazy

A builder cannot satisfy this with headings over a static prop, because the prop is never twice in the same state — empty stick, three loaded runs, rules driven in, block building below, a sort held out for correction, chase locked, ink laid, sheet pulled. Each is a different drawing, and each is caused by the sentence being spoken over it. It also fails in the right direction against the bracket: this is not churn. The bench arrives once, early, and never re-composes itself; the camera never cuts to a new stage; the only motion in the frame is material moving to where it stays. That is the approved pattern — one object built early and thereafter only re-read — not the rejected one, where the picture swapped fast and gained nothing.

It also stays inside the script. Three segments because the narration names three; a rule bar between them because the narration says to separate them with a line; a before line and an after line in one frame because the narration gives both; two or three bright sorts because the narration says two or three themes; a marked proof and a corrected sort because the narration says to use feedback to sharpen it. No count, claim or sequence appears that the narration does not speak. And no product interface is ever drawn — the object is a bench of metal type, not a screen.
