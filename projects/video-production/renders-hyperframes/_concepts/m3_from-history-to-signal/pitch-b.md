# PITCH B — m3_from-history-to-signal

**Lens: accumulation-first.** The build-up was designed before the object was
named. Runtime basis: 373 narration words ≈ **150s** (≈2:30) including the 1.8s
final hold. Quarter marks land at ~37s / ~75s / ~113s / ~150s.

---

## The accumulation ladder

What the frame has physically GAINED at each mark. Nothing here is a heading
swap; every line is more glass, more angle, more light, or more reach.

**By ~30s** — *a heap has arrived, and a light with nowhere to go.*
The lower two-thirds of the frame fills with a dense, uncounted field of loose
glass segments: chipped, mismatched, stacked at every angle, some face-up and
catching a glint, some edge-on and dark. Above it, a bare lamp burns. Its light
leaves in every direction at once — a wide, soft, undirected halo that reaches
nothing. **Gained:** the archive itself (mass), and glare (light that exists but
does not point). This is the script's "long record of everything you've done"
drawn as a physical quantity, and "harder for other people to quickly see where
you're heading" drawn as scatter.

**By ~60s** — *a carrier frame, three graduations, and the first glass that
does nothing.*
An empty brass carrier — the ribbed skeleton that a lens belt seats into — is
set around the lamp: open, obviously unfilled, its courses visible as empty
slots. Three graduation notches are cut into its rim (the three things the
script says a reader actually scans for). Then the first two segments are
lifted out of the heap and seated. They are **flat** — unbevelled — so the
lamp's light passes straight through them and keeps scattering. **Gained:** a
structure with named positions, first occupancy, and a visible contradiction:
glass is in the lens and the light still goes nowhere. That is "responsible
for X, duties included Y" drawn as an object.

**By ~90s** — *one segment, cut twice, and the first ray that leaves.*
The same single piece of glass is shown seated twice, side by side in the same
slot position: once flat, once with a bevel ground into its face. Only the
bevelled one bends its share of the lamp forward — a single thin thread of
light exits the carrier and crosses a short span of frame. **Gained:** the
first directed ray in the whole video, and a second axis of accumulation that
is not "more pieces" — it is *angle*. Same glass, same origin, one version
throws.

**By ~120s** — *the belt substantially filled, a shaft instead of a thread,
and a second readable surface.*
Segments are seated in courses around the lamp, each one bevelled as it goes
in. The thread of light has thickened into a solid shaft crossing half the
frame. Alongside the lens, the tower's flat **daymark band** — the face that is
read at a distance, in daylight, without the lamp — is fitted and painted with
the same forward direction. **Gained:** occupancy across the whole belt, beam
width and reach, and a second channel that says the same thing the beam says.
Two surfaces, matching the two things the script names.

**By ~150s (close)** — *the heap is still there, and the light lands.*
The belt completes. The lens makes one slow one-way turn and stops. The beam
crosses the entire frame and **lands** — a single narrow bright band on the far
edge, the first time in the video that light arrives somewhere instead of
leaking. And the heap has NOT vanished: it still sits below, still large, now
pocked with the clean gaps where segments were taken. **Gained:** completion,
arrival, and the visible evidence of curation rather than rewriting — the
script's own "the goal here is not to rewrite your entire history."

## The carrying object

**A lighthouse lens belt, assembled segment by segment from a heap of old
glass that is never cleared away.**

Drawn as a three-quarter cutaway in flat SCLA-token line-and-fill: a brass
ribbed carrier with individually drawn slots, and inside each slot a distinct
glass segment with its own stepped prism profile, bevel line, chipped corner
and glint — never a smooth concentric bullseye, and never two segments drawn
identically. Below and behind it, the heap is a real drawn pile with depth:
overlapping silhouettes, edge-on slivers, a few pieces face-up catching light,
drawn dense enough that a viewer's eye can pick individual pieces out of it.
The lamp is a small hard core; light is drawn as flat-fill wedges — a wide pale
scatter early, a narrow saturated shaft late — so beam width, beam length and
beam count are all measurable to the eye from frame to frame.

## Four frames

**25% (~37s) — THE HEAP.** Wide, low-key, bottom-heavy. Loose glass fills the
lower frame edge to edge; the bare lamp sits high and small inside a broad pale
halo that fades out before it reaches anything. No carrier yet, or a carrier
just entering, empty. No directed light anywhere in frame.

**50% (~75s) — THE TWIN CUT.** A close-up. The wide shot is abandoned; we are
in tight on one slot of the carrier, showing the same segment seated two ways —
flat on the left, bevelled on the right, the bevel's ground face clearly drawn.
Off the right-hand one, a single hairline ray leaves. The heap is out of shot
or reduced to a blurred lower edge. Composition, scale and subject are all
different from every other frame.

**75% (~113s) — THE BELT WORKING.** Wide again, but unrecognisable against
frame 1: the carrier is mostly seated in courses, the lamp is now hidden behind
its own glass, and a solid shaft of light crosses half the canvas. The daymark
band stands beside it carrying the same direction. The heap is still present at
the bottom but visibly quarried — gaps, not absence.

**100% (~150s) — THE LANDING.** The widest framing in the video. The completed
lens sits left; the beam crosses the full canvas; at the far right it lands as
one narrow bright band. The daymark reads at a glance. The heap sits below,
still big, still there, gaps and all. For the first time the whole object and
the whole reach of its light are visible in a single picture.

## The payoff beat

**The single turn and the landing, on the closing question** ("if someone only
saw my resume and my LinkedIn profile, would they understand the future I'm
aiming for?" → "much closer to a yes"). The lens rotates once, one way, and
stops; the beam extends past the point it has held all video and terminates in
a bright band at the far edge. Three things become simultaneously visible that
have never shared a frame: the assembled belt, the beam's full reach, and the
still-intact heap it was curated from. This frame is undrawable at 0:10 — at
0:10 there is no carrier, no bevel, no beam and nothing for light to land on.

## Why this is not lazy

- **Five independent state variables**, none of which a heading swap can fake:
  heap density and gap pattern; carrier occupancy; per-segment bevel (present /
  absent); beam width and reach; daymark fitted or bare. A builder who leaves
  the prop static fails the picture, not just the taste read.
- **Two different kinds of accumulation.** Count (segments seated) and *angle*
  (bevels), plus their product (light reach). The rejected thin cut had one
  axis and never moved it; the rejected slow cut churned without gaining. Here
  the picture at 120s cannot be reached by re-arranging the picture at 30s.
- **The shuffle test passes on light alone.** Order the frames by beam: none →
  hairline → shaft → landed band. Time has a visible direction even with all
  copy removed.
- **The heap deliberately survives.** Refusing the empty-stockpile ending is
  both faithful to the script ("not to rewrite your entire history… it's to
  curate") and the thing that separates this carrier from every "stack depletes
  as structure builds" pattern in the batch.
- **Batch-distinct.** No bricks, no paper, no pegs or thread, no scrap field
  sorting into columns, no trail, track, plant, tree or span. The carrier is
  glass and light, and its accumulation medium — *beam reach* — is not
  available to any other carrier in the batch.

**Builder notes (contract, not decoration).** On-frame words are the script's
own — headings Title Case, no terminal period; the only numeral that may appear
is the script's own 30%. Seated segments are pixel-static once placed; the only
motion in any beat is the next segment arriving, a bevel being ground, or the
beam extending. The closing rotation is a single one-way move that ends and
holds — never a loop, never idle wobble. One hero illustration per frame: the
lens. No fabricated resume, profile or platform UI anywhere.
