# PITCH A — m3_from-history-to-signal

**Lens:** metaphor-first

## The carrying object

**A museum display case standing in front of a crammed storeroom shelf** — the
shelf is the archive your resume has become, the case is what a decision-maker
actually gets to look at.

It is drawn flat and two-tone, no perspective tricks: the shelf is a grid of
stacked rectangles, each holding a plain solid (a crate, a cylinder, a folded
form) with a small tag-rectangle hanging off it on a two-pixel thread. The case
is one large rounded rectangle with a thin top rail, three low pedestals on its
floor, a blank placard bar across its head, and a blank wall-card rectangle
mounted to its right. Light is drawn, not implied: each pedestal's spotlight is
a pale triangle dropping from a small lamp on the rail, so "lit / unlit" is a
shape on screen, not an opacity mood. Every state change in this video is one of
four visible operations — an object moves shelf → pedestal, a hanging tag is
replaced by an engraved caption bar, a triangle of light turns on, or lettering
lands on the placard and wall card.

## Four frames

**25% — the archive, and nothing to look at.**
The shelf fills most of the frame, packed edge to edge, every object carrying a
small hanging tag. The case in front is empty: three bare pedestals, no light
triangles, placard bar blank, wall card blank. Heading carries the script's own
word — the profile has turned into *an archive*. The picture's whole message is
density with no direction.

**50% — the duty tag under the light.**
Camera is now on the visitor's side of the glass. One object has been pulled
from the shelf onto the centre pedestal and its light triangle is on — and under
that light its hanging tag is legible and wrong: *"responsible for managing
projects."* Three empty label brackets sit along the case's front edge, drawn as
open square brackets waiting to be filled — results, scope and how it lines up
with the role you want next, the things the script says people are scanning for.
The shelf behind is still full but has dropped to a background weight. One
object lit, two pedestals still bare.

**75% — the swap, and the selection.**
The same centre object, unchanged in shape, has had its dangling tag cut away and
an engraved caption bar mounted under it instead: *"led a cross-functional
project that reduced onboarding time by 30%."* The cut tag is drawn falling, still
on screen. Objects two and three have arrived on the flanking pedestals with
their own light triangles up and their own caption bars mounted. The three
brackets along the front edge are now filled. The shelf is still there and still
full — nothing was thrown away, which is the script's point about curating rather
than rewriting your history.

**100% — the exhibit reads as one thing.**
Wide, from the visitor's standing distance. Three lit plinths, three engraved
captions, the placard across the head of the case now lettered, and the wall card
beside it lettered — the headline and the summary, the last of the three moves.
For the first time the whole case is inside one frame with a viewer's sightline
drawn into it, and the closing question sits on the wall card: would they
understand the future I'm aiming for?

## What accumulates

Four things gain, one thing recedes, and every one of them is a countable shape:

- **Light** — zero triangles → one → three → three under a lettered placard.
- **Labels** — hanging duty tags → cut away → engraved caption bars mounted flush
  under each object.
- **Signage** — the placard bar and wall card start blank and stay blank until the
  final act, so the case is literally unnamed for three quarters of the runtime.
- **Occupancy** — the case fills pedestal by pedestal while the *brackets* along
  its front edge fill in parallel.
- **The shelf recedes** — foreground and dominant at 25%, background weight by
  50%, present but quiet at 100%. It never empties. That is the curation claim
  drawn rather than asserted.

Reorder these frames and the sequence breaks immediately: an engraved caption
cannot precede the tag it replaced, and the placard cannot be lettered before the
case has anything in it.

## The payoff beat

**The pull-back to the full case at the close.** Everything before it is drawn
tight — a shelf, a pedestal, one object under one lamp. The final beat is the
first time the case, its three lit plinths, its placard, its wall card and the
viewer's sightline are all in one picture, and the eye is walked placard →
captions → forward. It could not have been drawn at 0:10, when the case was three
bare pedestals in the dark.

There is a second, smaller payoff at ~65%: the tag-for-caption swap on a single
unmoved object. The object's shape does not change at all — only its label does,
which is exactly the script's "same work, but one version signals results."

## Why this is not lazy

A builder cannot satisfy this with heading swaps against a static prop, because
the prop is *empty* for the first quarter and unnamed for the first three
quarters. The four operations are all geometry: an object translates from a shelf
cell to a pedestal, a tag rectangle detaches and falls, a caption bar appears
flush under an object, a triangle of light turns on. None of them is text
substitution, and none can be skipped without the final frame failing to assemble.

It also refuses the obvious cheat of emptying the shelf. The script says the goal
is not to rewrite your entire history — so the shelf stays full and the case stays
selective, and the gap between the two is the whole argument, visible in every
single frame.

Nothing here is invented: the archive, the scanning for results and scope and fit,
the duty-bullet, the 30% rewrite, the three moves, the headline and summary, and
the closing question are all the narration's own. The case is only another way of
reading it.
