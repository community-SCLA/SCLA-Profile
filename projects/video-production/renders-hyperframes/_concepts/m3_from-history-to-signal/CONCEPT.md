# CONCEPT — m3_from-history-to-signal

*Judged 2026-08-05 against the two APPROVED rows of `design-system/docs/taste.md`
(`m1_mini-syllabus` freeform trial; `build-direction-before-you-build-a-plan`
Jul 30–31). The 2026-08-05 `career-building-is-a-repeatable-process` cut is
SHIPPED-NOT-A-REFERENCE and was not used as an anchor. Assigned carrier family:
the lighthouse lens assembled from a heap of glass — binding.*

Runtime basis: 373 narration words ≈ **150s**, quarter marks ≈ 37 / 75 / 113 / 150s.
Percentages below are the contract; seconds are indicative and follow the real TTS.

## Concept angle

**A heap of scattered old glass is seated and bevelled segment by segment into a
lighthouse lens, turning one lamp's directionless halo into a single narrow beam
that lands on a reader's edge** — the heap is on frame from beat 1 and the lens
from roughly beat 3 to the final hold, so the carrier persists across **≈95% of
runtime**, every beat but the title card.

## The carrying object

A **lighthouse lens belt under assembly, drawn as a flat three-quarter cutaway**
in SCLA line-and-fill — no perspective tricks, no rendered glow, nothing that
depends on opacity to read.

- **The heap.** A real drawn pile occupying the lower third of the canvas, edge to
  edge: overlapping segment silhouettes at every angle, edge-on slivers, chipped
  corners, a few faces catching a hard glint. Dense enough that a viewer can pick
  out individual pieces. It is the archive — the "long record of everything you've
  done" — as physical quantity. **It is never cleared.**
- **The carrier.** A ribbed brass skeleton around the lamp with **individually
  drawn empty slots**, arranged in courses. The slot divisions must be visible in
  every frame the carrier appears in — this object is never once an unbroken ring.
- **The segments.** Each seated piece is drawn distinctly: its own stepped prism
  profile, its own chip, its own glint. No two identical. A segment has exactly two
  states, and both are *shapes*, not shading: **flat** (no ground face) and
  **bevelled** (a ground face drawn as a hard diagonal band across its front).
- **Three gauge slots** cut into the carrier rim, drawn as open brackets that fill
  one at a time — the three things the script says a reader is actually scanning
  for: results delivered, scope handled, and fit with the role you want next.
- **The lamp and the light.** The lamp is a small hard core. Light is drawn as
  **flat-fill wedges with hard edges** — a wide pale scatter early, a narrow
  saturated shaft late — so beam width, beam length and beam count are countable to
  the eye from frame to frame. Never a soft gradient, never a bloom.
- **The daymark band.** A flat panel mounted on the same housing (part of the one
  hero object, not a second illustration), the face read at a distance in daylight
  without the lamp. It is the LinkedIn headline and summary. **It stays bare and
  unpainted until the final quarter** — the object is literally unnamed for three
  quarters of the runtime.

## Milestone frames — the builder's ACCUMULATION CONTRACT

Four genuinely different pictures. Each names what must be *drawn*, and each is
undrawable at any earlier mark.

**25% (≈37s) — THE HEAP.** Wide, bottom-heavy. Loose glass fills the lower third
edge to edge, dense and individually drawn. The bare lamp sits high and small
inside a broad pale halo that fades out before it reaches anything on frame. **No
carrier yet or a carrier just entering, empty. Zero bevels. Zero directed rays.
Three gauge slots empty. Daymark bare.** The picture's whole message is mass with
no direction.

**50% (≈75s) — THE TWIN CUT.** Scale change: abandon the wide shot, go tight on
one carrier slot. The **same segment shown seated two ways side by side** — flat on
the left, bevelled on the right, the ground face clearly drawn. Off the bevelled
one and only that one, a **single hairline ray** leaves the carrier and crosses a
short span. The heap is out of shot or a reduced lower edge. Composition, scale and
subject all differ from every other milestone. **First bevel; first directed ray;
first gauge slot filled.**

**75% (≈113s) — THE BELT WORKING.** Wide again but unrecognisable against 25%: the
carrier is mostly seated in courses, every seated piece bevelled, **the lamp now
hidden behind its own glass**, and a solid shaft of light crossing half the canvas.
The daymark band is fitted and painted, carrying the same forward direction as the
beam. The heap is still present and still large at the bottom, now **visibly
quarried — clean gaps, not absence.** Two gauge slots filled, the third filling.

**100% (≈150s) — THE LANDING.** The widest framing in the video. The belt
completes; the lens makes **one slow one-way turn and stops**. The beam crosses the
full canvas and **lands** as one narrow bright band on the far edge — a reader's
viewing edge, carrying the script's closing question. The daymark reads at a
glance. The heap sits below, still big, still there, gaps and all. Three gauge
slots filled. For the first time the assembled belt, the beam's full reach, and the
intact heap it was curated from share a single frame.

## What accumulates

Five independent, countable state variables — no heading swap can fake any of them:

- **Carrier occupancy** — empty slots → two seated → courses seated → belt complete.
- **Bevels (angle, not count)** — zero → one → all seated → all seated, and the
  discarded flat piece drawn falling back into the heap.
- **Beam** — none → hairline → half-canvas shaft → full-canvas shaft terminating in
  a landed band. The shuffle test passes on the beam alone, with all copy removed.
- **Gauge slots** — three empty brackets → one → two → three filled.
- **Naming** — daymark bare for three quarters, then painted.

And one thing **recedes without disappearing**: the heap goes from dominant
foreground at 25% to quarried background at 100%. It never empties. That is the
script's "the goal here is not to rewrite your entire history — it's to curate,"
drawn rather than asserted, and it is the gap between heap and lens that carries the
argument in every single frame.

## The payoff beat

**The single turn and the landing, on the closing question.** The lens rotates once,
one way, and stops; the beam extends past the reach it has held all video and
terminates in a bright band on the far edge, where the script's own question sits:
would they understand the future I'm aiming for? Three things become simultaneously
visible that have never shared a frame — the assembled belt, the beam's full reach,
and the still-intact heap. This frame could not have been drawn at 0:10: at 0:10
there is no carrier, no bevel, no beam and no far edge for light to reach.

A second, smaller payoff sits at **50%**: the twin cut. Same glass, same origin,
same slot — one version throws light forward and one does not. That is the script's
"same work, but one version signals results, and the other just lists duties,"
with no words needed.

## Grafted from the runner-up

Pitch A's strongest move was the **label surgery on an object that never moves** —
the duty tag cut away and drawn *falling*, the shape unchanged. Three grafts:

1. **The discarded flat segment is drawn falling back into the heap**, not deleted.
   The rejected version of your own experience is not thrown away; it returns to the
   archive. This closes B's one soft spot — it made the twin cut a comparison, and A
   makes it a *replacement with visible residue*.
2. **A's empty label brackets become the three gauge slots** cut in the carrier rim,
   filling one at a time. B named three notches but never filled them; A's brackets
   made the "three things they scan for" an accumulation channel instead of decor.
3. **A's blank-signage discipline** — the placard unlettered until the final act —
   becomes the **bare daymark**, held unpainted until 75%+. It is the single cheapest
   guarantee that the closing frame is undrawable early.

## Batch-diversity note

This lesson holds the **glass-and-light** carrier, and its accumulation medium —
**beam reach** — is not available to any sibling. The three sibling M3 carriers are
all *surfaces you place things onto* (a darkroom light table, a gallery wall with a
hanging rail, a lit museum exhibit case); this one is an *instrument you assemble
that changes what light does*. Nothing in this concept places, hangs, pins or mounts
items on a flat display surface. Explicitly absent: exhibit cases, pedestals,
placards, wall cards, hanging rails, light tables, brick walls, skylines, ruled paper
worksheets, peg-and-thread boards, scrap fields feeding a statement strip, trail
markers, career tracks with checkpoint nodes, plant cuttings, potted trees on wires,
and truss spans. Pitch A's museum case is a forbidden carrier here and was scored on
its ideas only, never as a shippable expression.

## Judge's note

**Likeliest failure mode: the lens collapses into the rejected thin cut.** A builder
under time pressure draws the carrier as a smooth concentric ring with a soft glow
on a near-empty frame — which is the 6-dot circle on empty canvas the owner rejected
on 2026-08-05, wearing new vocabulary. Three concrete guards, all checkable on a
contact sheet without reading a word of copy:

1. **No frame ever shows an unbroken ring.** Slot divisions and per-segment profiles
   are visible in every frame the carrier appears in; no two segments drawn alike.
2. **The heap is required ink, not scenery.** It occupies the lower third with
   individually distinguishable pieces from beat 1 to the close. A frame whose lower
   third is empty dark is a defect, not a style choice.
3. **Light is a hard-edged flat-fill wedge, never opacity.** Beam width and length
   must be measurable with a ruler on a still. Any beam that reads as glow, bloom or
   mood is re-authored.

**Second guard — the beam must not claim an outcome the narration does not make.**
The script's promise is about *comprehension*: that a reader would understand the
future you're aiming for, and that you'll be "much closer to a yes" on that question.
So the beam lands on a **reader's viewing edge carrying the script's own closing
question** — never on a harbour, a ship, a shore, a destination, a job title, an
offer, a promotion, or any figure being rescued or arriving. No outcome is depicted
anywhere in this video. Likewise: no fabricated resume, profile page, or platform UI;
the only numeral permitted on frame is the script's own 30%.

**Third guard — motion.** Seated segments are pixel-static once placed. The only
motion in any beat is the next segment arriving, a bevel being ground, the discarded
piece falling, or the beam extending. The closing rotation is one one-way move that
ends and holds — never a loop, never idle wobble on settled glass.

## Scores

**Pitch A — museum display case and storeroom shelf.** Genuinely strong craft. The
object is concrete and drawable (Q1 pass): light is a triangle shape rather than an
opacity mood, and all four state changes are geometry, not text substitution. The
four frames are different pictures (Q2 pass, marginally — 75% and 100% are the same
kind of picture separated by count, lettering and framing distance rather than by
scale or subject). Accumulation is real and countable across five channels, and the
refusal to empty the shelf is exactly right on the script's curation point (Q3
pass). The pull-back payoff is legitimate (Q4 pass), and an empty-then-unnamed prop
resists the lazy build (Q5 pass). But the lit museum exhibit case is assigned to a
sibling lesson in this batch and is forbidden here, and the pitch is inseparable
from it — every operation it describes is a case operation. **Verdict: RUNNER-UP.
Disqualified as an expression by the batch-diversity assignment; three of its ideas
grafted.**

**Pitch B — lighthouse lens belt from a heap of glass.** Lands on the assigned
family and earns it: the object is named down to per-segment chips and glints
(Q1 pass), and the four frames include a real scale change — the 50% close-up
abandons the wide shot entirely, which is the one thing A's four frames never do
(Q2 strongest pass). It accumulates on two independent axes, count *and* angle, plus
their product in beam reach, and the shuffle test survives with all copy stripped
(Q3 pass). The payoff is a frame that is structurally undrawable at 0:10 (Q4 pass),
and five state variables leave no room for a static prop with swapped headings
(Q5 pass). Its two risks — a glow-instead-of-shape lens, and a beam that quietly
promises an outcome the script never claims — are both closed by the guards above.
**Verdict: WINNER.**
