# CONCEPT — mini-syllabus_early-career-boost

**Judged against the two APPROVED rows in `design-system/docs/taste.md` only.**
The 2026-08-05 `career-building-is-a-repeatable-process` cut is a
SHIPPED-NOT-A-REFERENCE row and is not anchored on here in any form.

## Concept angle

**A career foundation wall laid course by course inside a ghosted scaffold, with
one block-sized notch left open in its base from the opening beat until the last** —
on screen from beat 2 through the final beat, roughly **95% of runtime** (the
title beat already carries the empty scaffold and the notch, so the object is
never introduced mid-video).

## The carrying object

A wall of staggered rectangular slabs rising from a solid baseline plinth,
drawn as **flat geometry, never masonry texture**: three slab widths (1u / 2u /
3u), small corner radius, brand ink on the brand dark field, one hairline joint
between neighbours. Above and around the built mass sits the same slab grid at
**outline weight only** — the ghost scaffold — which **fades out toward the top
edge so it can never be counted**. Nothing on screen ever asserts a number of
steps, milestones, or modules; the script names no such number.

Slabs carry **one small glyph on the slab face itself**, never an icon parked
beside a row — the wall is the single hero illustration on frame. Three faces
exist, and only three, because the script names exactly three ways of working:
a **dot face** (click-through activities), an **arc face** (short reflections),
an **up-nib face** (uploads).

Down the right edge of the wall runs a **graduated rail**: a thin vertical bar
with evenly spaced ticks, inked solid up to the height of the built mass and
left faint above it. This is the script's own "your progress bar always shows
where you are and what's ahead," drawn once and never captioned. The rail is
**derived, not decorative** — its inked height must equal the built height in
every single snapshot.

**Layout discipline.** The wall lives in the right two-thirds of the canvas and
grows upward inside that column; the copy column (eyebrow, heading, statement)
holds the left. Vertical growth must never cross into the heading band. At its
tallest the wall's solid mass occupies roughly two-thirds of frame height — it
**is** the frame, not a prop beside empty space.

**Legibility discipline.** Exactly one region carries accent ink per beat —
whatever the narration is naming right now. Everything already placed drops to
quiet brand ink and is **pixel-static thereafter**: no wobble, no keep-alive, no
re-marking. Richness here is the object earning more, never the picture
swapping faster.

**Guard against recolouring the approved reference.** The approved
`m1_mini-syllabus` carrier is a thin **horizontal** spine of five nodes with
annotations hanging off it and a travelling checkpoint. This concept must
contain **no horizontal spine, no node-and-dot track, and no travelling
highlight**. Its picture is vertical solid mass against a baseline. Test: with
every word of text removed, the two objects must be unmistakable for one
another in silhouette.

## Milestone frames — THE ACCUMULATION CONTRACT

Percentages are of real runtime; rescale to the actual `#root data-duration`.
Each frame must be a genuinely different picture *before any text is read*.

**25% — a foundation just started.**
A low **two-course band of solid slabs across the bottom third**, seated on the
plinth. One block-sized **notch in the plinth is clearly empty**. Everything
above is faint outlined lattice, fading out at the top. The rail is inked only
near the floor. *Skyline: bottom third. Element kinds on screen: solid slabs +
ghost outline.*

**50% — a worksite with tools attached to it.**
Mass reaches **mid-frame** across four courses and is visibly **mixed**: the
three slab faces interleave, and slabs laid plain in the first act have **picked
up their own glyphs where they already sit** — material already on screen
re-read, not new material added. A **hinged sight-arm** attaches at the wall's
left edge and fans open over the empty ghost region (the Career Mapping Tool,
aimed at what is not built yet). A **help tether** clips in from the frame
margin to the wall's side and stays clipped for the rest of the video. *Skyline:
mid-frame. New this act: three faces, two permanent appendages that change the
silhouette.*

**75% — a wall with openings, caught mid-conversion.**
Solid mass fills roughly **two-thirds of frame height**. One mid-band is
unmistakably a **different face from its neighbours** — hatched-and-hollow slabs
have been ringed and turned over to solid brand faces in place, positions
unchanged (limiting beliefs → growth mindset). The top course sets **three open
window frames** — the first non-solid elements in the whole piece, because these
are the questions, not the answers — and the third opening gains **three notches
along its sill**. The base notch is still open. *Skyline: two-thirds. New this
act: a second kind of element (voids), and a band that changed face without
moving.*

**100% — a load-bearing structure with room left above it.**
The **commitment slab** — scored on the wall's working face from two feeds at
once (Workbook, AI-Career Commitment tool) — has been **lifted out of the
working course**, leaving **one clean empty socket** high in the wall, and has
travelled down the outside and **seated into the base notch that has been open
since the third second**. The whole wall settles a few pixels onto it. The rail
inks to the top of the built mass. Three faint outlined slabs queue in the ghost
above the build line and stay unfilled. *Skyline: settled full. Unique to this
frame: the notch is closed, and there is exactly one hole where there was a
slab.*

## What accumulates

1. **Mass, monotonically.** Courses only ever increase. Built height is the
   progress indicator and the object are the same drawing, so progress cannot be
   faked with a moving highlight.
2. **Kinds of element.** Solid slabs → three distinguishable slab faces →
   hollow/hatched slabs → open voids. The vocabulary on screen grows.
3. **Silhouette, permanently.** The sight-arm and the help tether arrive once
   each, change the object's outline, and are present in every later frame.
4. **Material already placed, re-read.** Two beats transform slabs that are
   already on screen (plain slabs acquiring glyph faces; the hatched band turning
   over). Neither is achievable by adding an element — both require the earlier
   state to have been authored differently.
5. **One hole opens and one hole closes.** The base notch is open from beat 1
   and closed exactly once, at the end. The upper socket opens exactly once, at
   the end, and stays open.

## The payoff beat

**"You'll submit it as your first step."** The commitment slab leaves the
working face, travels down the outside of the wall, and seats into the base
notch. The wall drops onto it; the rail completes.

The re-read is the whole point: **the piece you write last is the piece that
goes in first.** The gap the viewer has been looking at for the entire runtime
turns out to be the foundation — the wall was never load-bearing until something
was submitted. And the narration's "career development isn't about thinking,
it's about doing" is the same event as the slab physically leaving the working
face. This frame could not have been drawn at 0:10: at 0:10 there is one course
of slabs and a hole.

## Grafted from the runner-up

**Pitch A's "one subtraction, at the end only."** A's workbook earned its
closing frame partly by having the commitment card *tear out and sit apart from
the object* — the single removal in the whole runtime, which is exactly why it
reads. Pitch B's payoff as pitched was purely additive, and a purely additive
payoff is the one a lazy builder can fake by adding one more slab and calling it
the ending.

Grafted in: the commitment slab is **scored on the wall's own working face
first, then lifted out and carried down**, leaving a permanent empty socket
above. The closing frame now carries both a hole that closed and a hole that
opened — a state no earlier frame can be mistaken for, and one that cannot be
produced by continuing to stack. It also reads the narration more honestly:
you write it into what you are building, and then it leaves you and becomes the
first course.

## Judge's note

**The failure mode this concept will slide into: the middle stretch collapsing
into "another slab arrives"** — five or six beats that are the same picture with
new headings, which is the thin-carrier rejection wearing a construction hat.
Beats in the 40–70% range are where it will happen, because that is where the
narration is listing (activities, tools, support, topics) and stacking one more
slab per list item is the cheapest legal move.

**The concrete guard, in two parts.**

1. **The skyline test.** Measure the top edge of the *solid* mass in the four
   milestone stills. It must read bottom-third → mid-frame → two-thirds →
   settled-full. If any two milestone stills show the same skyline, the concept
   has been violated and those beats are re-authored, not re-captioned.
2. **The three transform beats are mandatory and are not arrivals.** Plain slabs
   acquiring glyph faces in place; the hatched band turning over in place; the
   commitment slab lifting out of a course it was already in. Any build where
   every beat is "a new element enters" has dropped the part of this concept that
   makes it rich, and is failing regardless of what the pace numbers say.

The secondary risk is the opposite one — density killing legibility. That is
already fenced by the one-accent-region-per-beat rule and by the ghost scaffold
staying at outline weight. Nothing on frame may assert a count the script does
not speak; the only counts drawn are the three ways of working, the three real
questions, and the three moves, each of which the narration states.

## Scores

**Pitch A — the open workbook.** *(1) Name the object:* strong — a workbook is
as concrete and drawable as it gets, and the script names it, so nothing is
imported. *(2) Four-frame test:* the four descriptions are genuinely different,
but a large share of that difference is **camera** (push in, near-macro, rotate
to three-quarter) rather than the object gaining anything. A camera move is
cheap to fake and, in this flat-vector toolchain, a book seen edge-on with a
stacked-hairline fore-edge is the kind of drawing that degrades into crude
shapes — and the degraded version is a static book with new headings, i.e. the
thin rejection. *(3) What accumulates:* genuinely strong — wedge thickness,
marks that never clear, permanent additions, one subtraction. *(4) Payoff:* very
strong; the tear plus the tip-onto-edge is a real re-read. *(5) Lazy-build test:*
the contract is well written but two of its four clauses (the silhouette break,
the near-macro) are framing instructions, and framing is not accumulation. A
also proposes line art on paper-white, a full departure from the field both
approved cuts sit on, which adds palette risk the concept does not need.

**Pitch B — the foundation wall.** *(1) Name the object:* strong, and it is the
script's own phrase — "each step builds your career foundation." *(2) Four-frame
test:* the strongest of the two, because the four pictures differ by **measured
mass and height with the camera locked**. Nothing depends on a move; the
difference survives a contact sheet read cold. *(3) What accumulates:* the best
answer available to this question — monotonic mass, a growing element
vocabulary, two permanent silhouette changes, and two transforms of material
already on screen. *(4) Payoff:* a hole authored in the opening beat and closed
in the last, which is the "could not have been drawn at 0:10" test passed
structurally rather than asserted. *(5) Lazy-build test:* closes the hole
properly — a heading-swap build produces two frames of identical height and a
rail contradicting its own wall, both visible instantly on the sheet. Its
weaknesses are a listy middle (guarded above) and a payoff that was purely
additive (fixed by the graft).

**Verdict: PITCH B wins.** It is visibly not a recolour of the approved
horizontal-spine reference, its accumulation is measurable in pixels without a
camera move, its payoff is authored into the first frame rather than announced
in the last, and it is drawable in flat geometry the pipeline actually renders
well. Pitch A's single best idea — the one subtraction at the end — is grafted
into B's payoff.
