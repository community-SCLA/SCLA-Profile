# PITCH B — mini-syllabus_early-career-boost

**Lens: accumulation-first.** The gains were designed before the object was named.

**Runtime assumption:** ~245 spoken words → **~110s** (est. 1:50 incl. the 1.8s final
hold). Marks below are **28 / 55 / 83 / 110s**. Rescale proportionally to the real
`#root data-duration` once narration is synthesised — the ladder is expressed as
*state at 25/50/75/100%*, not as absolute seconds.

## The accumulation ladder

What is physically MORE, denser, re-sorted, or newly connected at each mark. Every
gain below is an entrance or a one-time transform — nothing idles, nothing re-marks.

**By ~28s (25%) — mass exists where there was only outline.**
The frame opens as a ghosted lattice (faint outlined cells) sitting on a solid
baseline plinth, so the object owns the canvas from 0:03 with almost no ink in it.
Then ink arrives: a first course of solid slabs seats onto the plinth, then a second
course staggers over the joints. A graduated rail at the right edge inks its ticks up
to the built height and stays faint above it. **Net gain: 0 → ~7 solid slabs, 0 → 2
courses, plus a rail that now reads a height.** One block-sized notch in the base
course is left open, outlined, unexplained.

**By ~55s (50%) — the mass stops being uniform, and the object grows appendages.**
The next courses arrive in three distinguishable slab faces (a dot face, an arc face,
an up-nib face — doing, reflecting, uploading), and the plain slabs already laid pick
up their own marks as they are named: *a re-sort of material that was already on
screen.* A hinged sight-arm attaches at the wall's left edge and fans open over the
empty ghost region — the mapping tool, aimed at what is not built yet. A tether line
clips on from the frame margin and stays clipped. **Net gain: a third and fourth
course, three faces where there was one, one new appendage, one new connection.**

**By ~83s (75%) — the wall roughly doubles and one band changes its face.**
A whole upper band lands. Inside the working course some slabs arrive hatched and
hollow; they are ringed, then turned over to solid brand faces — the band's texture
visibly converts from hatch to solid while the slab positions stay put. Then the top
course sets three **open frames** — window openings, the first non-solid elements in
the whole piece, because these are the questions, not the answers — and the third
opening gains three notches along its sill. **Net gain: height roughly doubles, the
piece gains a second kind of element (voids), and an already-placed band flips face.**

**By ~110s (100%) — the base closes and the whole thing settles.**
A wide slab is composed off-wall to the left, scored from two feeds at once. It
travels down the outside of the wall and seats into the base notch that has been open
since 0:03. Everything above settles ~4px onto it; the rail's fill completes to the
top of the built mass. Three faint outlined slabs then queue in the ghost above the
build line and stay unfilled. **Net gain: the densest state of the run, the base
finally load-bearing, and the ghost above deliberately still open.**

## The carrying object

**A career foundation wall, laid course by course inside a ghosted scaffold, with a
graduated rail down its right edge and one block-sized notch left open in its base.**

Drawn as flat geometry, not masonry texture: staggered rectangular slabs in three
widths (1u / 2u / 3u), small corner radius, brand ink on a light field, each slab
carrying one tiny glyph on its face rather than an icon beside it — the wall is ONE
hero illustration. The ghost scaffold is the same slab grid at outline weight only,
**fading out at the top edge so it is never a countable total** (nothing on screen
claims a number of steps, milestones, or activities the script does not state). The
rail is a thin vertical bar with evenly spaced ticks, inked solid up to the built
height and left faint above it — that is the script's own "where you are and what's
ahead," drawn once and never captioned.

*Not the reference map:* the approved reference builds its object early and then only
ever re-reads it. This one starts nearly empty and gains mass monotonically; its
picture is vertical accumulation against a baseline, not a flat surveyed field, and
its payoff is a piece **seating into a gap**, not a route being traced across
something already complete.

## Four frames

**25% (~28s).** A low two-course band of solid slabs across the bottom third, resting
on a plinth with one clearly empty notch in it. Everything above is faint lattice.
The rail at right is inked only near the floor. Reads: *a foundation just started.*

**50% (~55s).** The mass reaches mid-frame and is visibly mixed — three slab faces
interleaved through four courses. A hinged arm juts out to the left and fans open
over ghost space; a tether line runs in from the frame margin and clips to the wall's
side. Reads: *a worksite with tools attached to it.*

**75% (~83s).** Tall — solid mass fills roughly two-thirds of frame height. One
mid-band is unmistakably a different face from its neighbours (just turned over). The
top course is three open window frames, one of them notched three times along its
sill. The base notch is still open. Reads: *a wall with openings, caught
mid-conversion.*

**100% (~110s).** The full wall, settled. The base notch is closed by the widest slab
in the piece, spanning under two courses. The rail is inked to the top of the built
mass. Three outlined slabs hover queued above the build line. Reads: *a finished,
load-bearing structure with room left above it.*

## The payoff beat

**The final beat of "You'll submit it as your first step."** The slab written off-wall
during the commitment-statement beat travels down and seats into the base notch that
has been visible and empty since the third second of the video; the entire wall drops
a few pixels onto it and the rail's fill completes. The re-read is the whole point:
**the piece you write last is the piece that goes in first** — the gap you have been
looking at for a hundred seconds turns out to be the foundation, and the wall was
never load-bearing until something was submitted. This frame could not have been
drawn at 0:10: at 0:10 there is one course of slabs and a hole.

## Why this is not lazy

- **Mass is the content.** The 25% and 75% frames differ in how much wall exists, not
  in what the heading says. A builder who freezes the object produces two frames of
  identical height, which the contact sheet shows immediately.
- **Two beats transform material that is already on screen** — earlier plain slabs
  acquiring their glyph faces, and the hatched band turning over. Neither is
  achievable by adding an element; both require the earlier state to have been
  authored differently. A static prop has no earlier state to convert.
- **The rail is derived, not decorative.** Its inked height must track the built
  height at every beat. A heading-swap build leaves a rail that contradicts its wall
  in every snapshot.
- **The payoff needs a hole authored in frame one.** The base notch has to exist,
  empty, from the opening beat and be filled by an element composed elsewhere and
  relocated. Nothing about that is a caption change.
- **It survives the shuffle test.** Any two contact sheets can be put in time order
  from mass alone — courses only ever increase, and the base notch is open then
  closed exactly once.
- **It respects the motion rule.** Every gain is an entrance or a single one-shot
  transform (turn-over, seat, settle); once a slab is placed it is pixel-static. No
  wobble, no keep-alive, nothing to answer "boring" with churn — the richness is the
  object earning more.
- **It stays inside the narration.** Nothing on screen counts steps, names a duration,
  or asserts an order the script does not speak; the scaffold is unbounded above and
  the queued slabs stay unlabelled, precisely so the frame never claims a syllabus
  length the lesson never states.
