# PITCH A — mini-syllabus_early-career-boost

**Lens:** metaphor-first.

## The carrying object

**An open workbook, seen large and nearly flat-on, whose left half thickens with marked pages while its right half of blank pages thins away.**

It is drawn as line art on paper-white: two page rectangles meeting at a curved gutter, the outer edges built from a stack of stacked horizontal hairlines so the *thickness* of each half is a visible wedge, not an implied one. Page furniture is all hard shapes — a column of hollow squares for milestones, evenly spaced rule-lines for writing, a taped-in folded rectangle with crease lines for the Career Mapping Tool, a dotted vertical perforation where a card tears out, a narrow ribbon tab threaded into the fore-edge for `#questionsupport`. Ink is brand ink on paper, one accent reserved for whatever the current beat is marking; everything already marked stays marked and stays on screen.

The book fills roughly three-quarters of the canvas at its smallest — it is the frame, never a prop sitting beside empty space.

**Not the reference map.** The approved reference carrier is one map built early and thereafter only *re-read*; nothing about it changes. This carrier is the opposite motion — it never re-reads until the last beat, and every beat physically alters it (a box inks, a page turns, a fold-out breaks the page rectangle, a perforation tears). A map does appear here, but as one taped-in page *inside* the object, not as the object.

## Four frames

**25% — WIDE, mostly white.** The book lies open and flat. The left page carries the lesson's title block; the right page carries a single column of hollow milestone squares running top to bottom, only the topmost one inked. Both fore-edges are thin wedges of nearly equal thickness. The picture reads as *empty room to fill*.

**50% — the silhouette breaks.** Camera has pushed in and off-axis. The Career Mapping Tool page has been taped in and unfolded: a creased rectangle spilling out past both page edges, so the frame is no longer book-shaped at all — the map is the widest object on screen and the page beneath it survives only at the corners. Small marks from the previous beat are still visible where the paper shows: a click-through tick, a short reflection rule-line filled in, an upload corner. The `#questionsupport` ribbon now hangs from the fore-edge and will never leave.

**75% — TIGHT and vertical.** Near-macro on a tear-out card. A run of perforation dots falls vertically down the middle of the frame; the career commitment statement is handwritten across the card. At the cut-off left edge of the frame, the *same* statement is visible again in the ruled column of the workbook page — written twice, both ways, exactly as the narration offers. The book itself is out of frame except that sliver. Nothing here resembles frames 1 or 2.

**100% — WIDEST, and a new angle.** Pull back and rotate to three-quarter: the book is now seen from its edge, so the **fore-edge is the subject**. The left wedge is visibly thick and dense with mark-flecks; the right wedge of blank pages is thin. The milestone column shows through, inked where the first steps landed and hollow where the track continues. The ribbon tab still juts out. And the torn card sits *apart* from the book, off to one side, its perforated edge ragged — gone from the book, submitted. This frame could not have been drawn at 0:10.

## What accumulates

1. **Thickness.** The marked half of the book grows and the blank half shrinks, continuously, in pixels. This *is* the lesson's "progress bar always shows where you are and what's ahead" — the object and the progress indicator are the same drawing, so progress cannot be faked with a moving highlight.
2. **Marks that never clear.** Hollow milestone squares ink in and stay inked. Rule-lines fill and stay filled. Nothing already written is ever wiped to make room.
3. **Permanent additions.** The taped-in map and the `#questionsupport` ribbon arrive once and remain in every subsequent frame, changing the object's outline for good.
4. **One subtraction, at the end only.** The commitment card leaves the book. It is the single removal in the whole runtime, which is why it reads.

No count is ever asserted on frame or in narration — the milestone squares are drawn as a column, never numbered, because the script names milestones without a number.

## The payoff beat

The final line — the submitted statement, then activities, milestones, and skills that move you toward your goals. On that beat the card tears free along the perforation and the book tips onto its edge, and for the first time the *whole* lesson is legible in one picture: everything covered is the thick marked wedge, everything ahead is the thin blank wedge, the help ribbon is still there, and the first thing ever submitted is sitting outside the book. The narration's "career development isn't about thinking, it's about doing" is the same event as the card physically leaving.

## Why this is not lazy

A builder cannot satisfy this with headings against a static prop, because four of its states are geometric and gradable, not textual:

- **Fore-edge thickness is a contract.** CONCEPT.md pins it at each quarter; the left wedge must be measurably thicker in pixels at 100% than at 25%, and the right wedge thinner. A static book fails this on the contact sheet at a glance.
- **The 50% frame must not be book-shaped.** The fold-out has to break the page rectangle and dominate the canvas. A heading swap cannot change a silhouette.
- **The 75% frame is a scale change, not a content change** — near-macro, vertical, book out of frame. Any build that keeps the same wide book composition there has visibly skipped the beat.
- **The perforation must actually tear.** At 100% the card is off the book with a ragged edge. Drawing the dotted line without the tear is a detectable miss.
- **The shuffle test is passed by the object alone.** Order can be restored from wedge thickness and mark density with every word removed — which is the definition of the accumulation the thin rejected cut lacked.

The failure mode this concept has to be watched for is the *opposite* of thin: a page so busy it stops being legible. The guard is that only one region is ever in accent ink per beat, and everything settled goes quiet line-work and does not re-animate.
