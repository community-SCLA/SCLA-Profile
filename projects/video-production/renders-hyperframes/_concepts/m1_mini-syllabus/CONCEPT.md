# CONCEPT — `m1_mini-syllabus`

Program: `mid-career-momentum` · Script: `lesson-scripts/mid-career-momentum/ready/m1_mini-syllabus.txt`
Source: the owner-APPROVED "rich carrier" row in `design-system/docs/taste.md`.

## Concept angle

**The course is ONE career track drawn end to end, and the mid-career checkpoint
is a single marked node on it — every beat is another thing read off that same
track, never a new picture.**

Persistence: **beat 1 → last beat, 100% of runtime** (the approved cut held it
across all 17 beats of 104.8s). The rail is drawn in the opening title beat and
is never cleared, never rebuilt and never left. Well above the ≥60% floor.

## The carrying object

A **horizontal career rail** — one thin hairline running nearly the full frame
width at optical centre, carrying **five evenly spaced nodes**, with the third
(centre) node being the mid-career checkpoint.

What I saw in the approved cut's contact sheets, concretely:

- **The rail.** A single 1–2px blue hairline, ~x200 → ~x1720, sitting at roughly
  y=620 — below the headline band, above the detail band. It is a *line*, not a
  bar or a track graphic; it has no chrome, no ticks, no arrowheads.
- **The nodes.** Five small hollow rings on the line, evenly spaced, blue stroke,
  unfilled. They are quiet at rest — smaller than the type around them.
- **The checkpoint.** The centre node becomes a **larger gold double-ring** with
  a small gold star/asterisk glyph inside it, and a gold uppercase micro-label
  **MID-CAREER CHECKPOINT** set directly beneath it. It is the only node ever
  given a name.
- **Travelled vs ahead.** The segment from the left end through the checkpoint
  thickens and turns **solid gold**, and the nodes it passes turn gold with it.
  The segment to the right of the checkpoint later becomes a **dashed blue
  line** with its two remaining rings ghosted back — the road ahead, uncertain.
- **Hangers.** Detail rows attach *under* the rail as short bulleted lists (small
  square bullets; blue for things that merely exist, gold for things the track is
  pointing at). Fork questions drop from the checkpoint on a **short vertical
  gold connector** so they read as leaving that node, not floating beside it.
- **The window.** A gold **bracket above the rail**, just right of the
  checkpoint, labelled `NEXT 6–12 MONTHS`, spanning a defined stretch of the
  dashed road.
- **The marker.** A small solid gold square planted *on* the rail inside that
  bracketed window — the 90-day commitment, pinned to a place.
- **Ground.** Deep navy full-bleed with one soft radial lift behind the rail's
  centre. Nothing else is on the frame. Gold appears nowhere except on the thing
  the track is pointing at right now.

**The rule the approved cut stated out loud, and the builder inherits:** *if an
element cannot be justified as another way of reading the same track, it does
not exist.* Every element is a mark ON the rail, a label ABOVE it, or a detail
HANGING UNDER it. No second illustration, no decorative object.

## Milestone frames — the ACCUMULATION CONTRACT

Runtime is whatever the real narration comes to (~105s in the approved cut).
These four frames are read off that cut's own contact sheets and are binding
regardless of final beat count.

| % | Approved-cut evidence | What the frame must show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **25%** | sheet 1, frames 4–5 (21.1s / 29.5s) | The rail is fully drawn and one node has been **named**: the centre node is a gold double-ring with the `MID-CAREER CHECKPOINT` label under it. The rail either still reads all-blue, or has just begun filling gold from the left end. Nothing hangs under it yet. |
| **50%** | sheet 1, frame 8 (52.1s) | The rail is now **two-toned**: solid gold from the left end through the checkpoint, **dashed blue** from the checkpoint rightward. The road ahead has visibly gone uncertain. Small detail rows have started hanging under the travelled half. |
| **75%** | sheet 2, frames 4–5 (74.0s / 82.3s) | The three fork questions have been asked off the checkpoint and **cleared**, and the **`NEXT 6–12 MONTHS` bracket has arrived above the rail**, just right of the checkpoint, narrowing the dashed road to one defined window. Detail rows under the rail now sit inside that window's reach. |
| **100%** | sheet 2, frame 8 (103.6s) | The whole track reads at once: gold travelled segment → named gold checkpoint → dashed road ahead → the bracketed 6–12 month window → a **gold marker planted inside it** → and the **far-right end node lit gold** as the CTA. |

The 100% frame is **impossible to draw at 0:10** — at 0:10 there is a plain blue
line with five hollow rings and no named node.

## What accumulates

Six distinct, permanent additions to one object — nothing is a highlight moving
around:

1. **A name** — the centre node gains a gold treatment and a label it keeps.
2. **Travel** — the rail behind the checkpoint fills solid gold and stays filled.
3. **Attachments** — tools, support and constraints hang under the rail as rows.
4. **Uncertainty** — the road ahead converts from solid to dashed.
5. **A bracketed window** — `NEXT 6–12 MONTHS` narrows the dashed road.
6. **A planted marker** — the 90-day commitment lands *inside* that window.

The forks are the one deliberate subtraction: three questions arrive off the
checkpoint one per beat, are held together for one beat, then clear — and what
replaces them is the bracket that answers them. That subtraction is what earns
the bracket's arrival; it is not churn.

## The payoff beat

**The final beat.** The far-right end node lights gold — the same double-ring
treatment the checkpoint got — so the track's destination becomes the CTA, and
for the first time the viewer can read the whole argument in one sweep:
*where you've been (gold) → where you are (named checkpoint) → what's unknown
(dashed) → the window you'll work in (bracket) → the commitment you planted
(marker) → where this goes (lit end node).*

The re-read is the payoff. No new object appears; the object finally completes.

## Provenance

This concept is **not a fresh pitch**. It is the design intent of the
**owner-approved 2026-08-05 freeform trial cut of this same lesson**, sited at
`projects/video-production/experiments/m1-mini-syllabus-freeform-trial/`, which
is the "APPROVED — rich carrier" row in `design-system/docs/taste.md`. No concept
competition was run, by instruction.

The builder must **re-author this fresh** against the real, newly synthesised
narration timings in its own workspace. Do **not** copy the trial's HTML,
compositions, coordinates or beat boundaries; the trial is a design reference,
not a source tree. Beat count and beat starts belong to the new narration and
must satisfy the live pace gates on their own terms. What is inherited is the
**object, its behaviour and the accumulation contract above** — not its markup.

## Judge's note

**The failure mode a re-build slides into: heading swaps against a static prop.**
The rail is so easy to draw once and leave alone that a builder can produce
seventeen beats of new headings over an unchanged blue line, pass every gate
(the pace numbers are met by the heading changes), and ship the *thin* cut — the
6-dot-circle failure in a nicer costume.

**The guard:** the milestone table above is a contract, not a description. Before
the precheck vision review, lay the contact sheets side by side and run the
shuffle test — if the sheets can be reordered without anything looking wrong, the
rail has not accumulated and the build must be re-authored, not re-snapshotted.
Concretely, every one of the six accumulations must be visible as a *state
change on the rail itself*, and no stretch of three consecutive beats may leave
the rail in an identical state.

Second, smaller trap: answering any perceived flatness with motion. The rail is
only ever **extended, marked or re-lit** — never rebuilt, never re-animated once
settled (`check_motion.py` blocks it, and the owner's rejected-slow cut is proof
that churn is not richness).
