# CONCEPT — using-the-career-map-tool_early-career-boost

**Winner: Pitch B (accumulation-first), with Pitch A's lettering discipline grafted in.**

## Concept angle

One squared worksheet sheet, clipped to a board and filling the frame, that
gains every mark the lesson describes into four fixed regions and is finally
ruled into four named zones that re-sort the marks already on it — **persisting
from beat 1 through the final beat, 100% of runtime.**

## The carrying object

**A single large sheet of squared worksheet paper, seen flat from directly
above, clipped to a drafting board.** Not a screenshot: no browser chrome, no
window frame, no cursor, no scrollbar. This is the worksheet the lesson hands
you, drawn in ink.

- **The sheet.** Warm off-white rectangle occupying ~80% of the frame inside the
  safe area. Faint square grid across the whole surface, present from beat one
  and never re-drawn. Flat metal clip bar across the top; a rail of punched
  holes down the left edge.
- **Four fixed regions, in place from the first beat, never moved or resized:**
  a **header band** along the top (the step, hand-lettered), a **left intake
  margin** (a narrow column for profile lines, control blocks and the resume
  slip), a **right shelf** (a 3x2 block that holds the approach tiles), and the
  **centre working field** (the large open grid — the sheet's whole middle and
  lower two-thirds).
- **The marks, five shapes only.** *Approach tiles* — wide rounded rectangles,
  2px ink outline, a pin dot at the top-left corner; outline when shelved,
  filled when pinned. *Job chips* — narrower rectangles with one rule-line
  inside; they grow taller as detail lines are added. *Control blocks* — small
  solid ink blocks in the left margin carrying only button words the narration
  speaks. *The resume slip* — a rectangle with one folded corner, clipped over
  the margin's lower edge. *Ruled strokes* — the path lanes, the time-frame
  band, and the two heavy axis strokes, all drawn along grid lines that already
  exist.
- **Motion discipline.** Marks land and then hold. The sheet gains by new ink
  entering, never by settled ink drifting, pulsing or re-marking. The one
  permitted re-placement is the final re-sort, and it is a one-way move.

## Milestone frames — the ACCUMULATION CONTRACT

Each frame must be a genuinely different picture because the sheet has gained,
lost, or re-arranged something countable. The countable state is named in bold;
a beat that does not change one of these numbers is not a beat.

**25% — Started.** Header band lettered with the landing step. Right shelf holds
**six** labelled approach tiles (Traditional path, Fast track, Entrepreneurial,
Work-life balance, Location independent, Social impact) in a 3x2 block, with one
gap where a tile has been lifted out. Centre field: **one** pinned tile, alone,
surrounded by open grid. Left margin: **one** ticked control block. The whole
lower two-thirds is bare grid.
*Countables: shelf 5 of 6 · pinned 1 · margin marks 1 · lower field empty.*

**50% — Fed.** Same sheet, same regions, different weight. The shelf is visibly
gapped. The pinned tiles in the upper field have **not multiplied — they have
thickened**: each carries a short stack of ruled detail lines beneath it (target
industries, specific goals), so each chip is two to three times its 25% height.
The left margin has filled into an intake column — three ruled profile lines
plus the dog-eared resume slip clipped over its lower corner. **The centre lower
two-thirds is still provably bare grid** — a reserved emptiness the viewer can
feel is waiting.
*Countables: shelf gapped · pinned chips carry 3–4x their 0:45 ink · margin
marks 5 · lower field still empty.*

**75% — Sorted.** The lower two-thirds is now the loudest part of the frame.
It is ruled into horizontal **path lanes** with job chips strung along each in
sequence; each lane's left end carries a quick-stats strip (roles, industry,
focus); one lane is bracketed as the selected path; along the bottom edge a
ruled time-frame band runs left to right, and the selected lane's job chips step
upward across it in a staircase, each chip taller than the one before. The upper
pinned chips are still present but no longer the focus.
*Countables: lanes ruled · job chips in the lower field, many · one lane
bracketed · staircase climbing.*

**100% — Judged.** Two heavy ruled strokes cross the centre field, horizontal
and vertical, landing on grid lines that have been there since beat one, and
labelled along the edges with the script's own wording — personal fit and energy
across, long-term value and career capital up. Four zones named in their
corners: Prioritize, Explore, Enjoy or Side, and Park. **Every chip that arrived
over the whole video is unpinned and re-placed as a plotted mark inside one of
the four zones.** The lane rules survive only as faint ghosts. The shelf is
empty. The margin's control stack is complete.
*Countables: shelf 0 · lanes ghosted · every chip at a different coordinate than
at 75% · four zones named.*

Bare, fed, sorted, judged. Strip every heading and the four still restore to
order from the pictures alone: the shelf only ever empties, the field only ever
fills.

## What accumulates

1. **Population moves one way between regions.** The six approach tiles arrive
   on the shelf once and only ever leave it. The shelf count is monotonically
   decreasing across the whole runtime and hits zero at the payoff. Nothing is
   ever restocked.
2. **The same chips gain interior ink.** A pinned chip at 50% is the chip from
   25% with detail lines added, so it is physically taller. This is not a new
   prop swapped in — it is the approved-reference signature: marks arrive once,
   then are only ever re-grouped.
3. **The empty two-thirds is scheduled to disappear.** The lower field is
   provably bare at 50% and provably the densest region at 75%. That single fact
   makes a static background impossible.
4. **One honest subtraction.** On the regenerate beat, the confirmation overlays
   the sheet and the pinned approaches and job chips dim to bare outline — the
   script says plainly that the old career approaches and jobs will be removed.
   Two drawn controls: Regenerate, Cancel. A field that can visibly LOSE is a
   field the viewer believes was really holding something.
5. **The sheet's organising logic flips once, permanently.** For the first half
   it is a form read top to bottom; from the axis beat onward it is a field read
   by position. It loses clutter exactly as it gains meaning.

## The payoff beat

**The closing beat, when the two heavy strokes are ruled straight across the
marks already on the sheet.** Up to that instant the sheet has been a list of
things you told the tool; the moment the axes land, the faint grid that has sat
quietly under the entire lesson re-reads as a coordinate field, and every loose
mark the viewer watched arrive one at a time re-reads as one comparable set.
The camera holds the whole sheet for the first time since the opening.

The frame at 0:10 was a blank ruled sheet with a full shelf. This frame is that
same sheet — and it could not have been drawn then, not by adding a heading,
only by everything that happened in between.

A secondary, quieter re-read lands at the generation beat, when loose pinned
chips become connected lanes. It earns the final one by teaching the viewer that
this sheet re-sorts.

## Grafted from the runner-up

**Pitch A's lettering discipline — the sheet is the worksheet, never a
screenshot of the tool.** Carried across in full, because it is the guard that
keeps a tool walkthrough from drifting into fabrication:

- **Every word lettered on the sheet must be a word the narration speaks.** The
  two axis labels, the four zone names, the six approach names, the profile
  fields, and the button words — nothing else gets lettered.
- **Never letter a count, total or figure.** The script's only path and job
  numbers, and its date range, appear inside "for example" phrasing — they are
  illustrations of what the tool might return, never content to draw. Show a
  selection without asserting how many; the summary mark is a ruled band with no
  numerals; the time-frame band is ruled with plain ticks. Quantity is shown by
  the number of chips actually drawn, never written down.
- **No screen feature the script does not describe.** No chrome, no cursor, no
  invented panel, no invented control.

Pitch B's accumulation ladder is what makes the video move; Pitch A's guard is
what keeps it true. Without the graft, B's "summary line in the script's own
example wording" would have lettered a fabricated result onto the frame.

## Judge's note

**The failure mode this will slide into: the intake half flattens.** The script
gives eight walkthrough parts, and Parts 1 to 4 are all "fill something in."
A builder working through them faithfully will produce eight near-identical
pictures of a sheet with a slightly fuller margin and a new heading each time —
which is the thin cut's exact failure wearing a nicer prop, and it will still
pass every numeric gate.

**The guard:** every beat in the intake half must own one specific countable
delta from the contract above — a *named* tile leaves the shelf, a chip grows a
detail line, the margin gains one profile line, the resume slip clips on — and
**no two consecutive beats may change the same countable.** If a script step has
no delta left to claim, it does not get its own beat; merge it into its
neighbour. Check it on the contact sheet, not in the HTML: three sheets apart in
time must differ in a number you can count with your eyes.

## Scores

**Pitch A** names a genuinely drawable object (a worked-on worksheet, not an
abstraction) and has the sharpest single insight in either pitch — the axes
landing across ink that is already there, so the sheet's organising logic flips
from form to field in one stroke. Its four frames are real pictures, but the
weakest pair is 75 and 100: both are "axes plus cards," separated only by how
many cards have been placed, and its 25-to-50 step is largely "more ink in the
same place." Accumulation is stated honestly but not made countable, so the
lazy-build test is answered by three good invariants rather than by numbers a
reviewer can check by eye. Its fabrication guard is the best-written paragraph
across both documents. **Strong, and the runner-up.**

**Pitch B** wins on the two questions that decide this bracket. Its four frames
are four categorically different pictures — bare, fed, sorted, judged — because
it schedules the lower two-thirds of the sheet to be empty for half the runtime
and then become the densest region, a whole-frame change no heading swap can
imitate. Its accumulation is countable per region (tiles on shelf, lines per
chip, chips in the lower field, chips per zone), which turns the milestone
frames into a contract a builder's own contact-sheet review can fail, and it is
the only pitch with a subtraction beat — the regenerate confirmation dimming the
sheet — which is what makes the viewer believe the field is really holding
something. Its payoff plants the grid at beat one and cashes it at the end. Its
one real risk was lettering the script's "for example" figures onto the frame,
which the graft closes. **Winner.**

**Verdict: Pitch B, with Pitch A's lettering discipline grafted in whole.**
