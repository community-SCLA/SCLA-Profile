# PITCH A — using-the-career-map-tool_early-career-boost

**Lens: metaphor-first.** One drawable object, seen the whole runtime, that gains
real content and then gets re-read.

---

## The carrying object

**One large sheet of squared worksheet paper, seen flat from directly above,
that fills with hand-inked notes and then gets ruled into four quadrants that
re-sort the notes already on it.**

It is drawn as a warm off-white rectangle filling roughly 80% of the frame, with
a faint square grid, a single ruled left margin line, and a hand-lettered title
strip along the top edge. Everything that arrives on it is made of four shapes
only: small rounded-rectangle **approach tiles** (outline when unchosen, filled
when chosen), short **ruled note lines** that ink in left to right, small
**option cards** (an index-card rectangle with one rule across the top and a
short label), and two heavy **ruled axis lines** with arrowheads and edge labels.
No browser chrome, no window frames, no cursor — this is the worksheet the
lesson actually hands you, not a screenshot of the tool.

## Four frames

**25% — the sheet is mostly bare paper.** Title strip lettered at the top. Along
the upper third, a single row of six outline approach tiles carrying the exact
six names the script lists (Traditional path, Fast track, Entrepreneurial,
Work-life balance, Location independent, Social impact). One or two are filled
solid; the rest stay outline. Below the row: empty grid, three-quarters of the
sheet untouched. Reads as: *a form barely started.*

**50% — the upper half is dense and worked-in.** The chosen tiles now have short
ruled note lines hanging under them (target industry, a goal line) and a profile
block on the left margin has inked in — name line, industry line, background
line. A small clipped rectangle sits in the top-right corner for the resume. The
lower half of the sheet is still bare grid. Reads as: *you have told it about
you; the answer hasn't come back yet.* Genuinely different picture from 25% —
half the sheet is now ink where it was blank.

**75% — the sheet has been ruled, and the notes are in motion.** Two heavy axis
lines are now drawn across the lower field, labelled along their edges with the
script's own words: horizontal *Personal fit and energy*, vertical *Long-term
value / career capital*. The option cards that came out of the worked-in upper
half have been cut loose and sit in a loose stack at the sheet's right edge,
with the first few already set down out on the field, off-centre and uneven. Two
or three of the four zone names are lettered in faint hand in their corners; the
rest are still blank corners. Reads as: *mid-sort.* Nothing about this frame
could be mistaken for 50% — the axes did not exist there.

**100% — the whole sheet is one plot.** Every card is placed. All four corners
carry their lettered zone names (Prioritize, Explore, Enjoy or Side, Park) and
the four quadrants carry four faint tints. The worked-in upper half has thinned
to a small folded-back block in the top-left corner — its content became the
cards, so it no longer needs the room. The eye can take the entire sheet in at
once and see the spread. Reads as: *side by side, one criteria set applied to
all of them* — which is the closing line the narration lands on.

## What accumulates

Three things, all measurable frame to frame:

1. **Ink on the sheet goes up monotonically through the first half.** Bare grid →
   six tiles → tiles plus filled profile block, note lines and a clipped resume.
   The sheet at 50% has visibly more marks than at 25%, not different marks.
2. **The marks made in the first half BECOME the cards placed in the second
   half.** They are the same objects, cut loose and re-arranged — not a new set
   of props swapped in. This is the approved-reference signature: a field of
   marks arrives once, then is only ever re-grouped.
3. **The sheet's organising structure changes once, permanently.** For the first
   half the sheet is a *form* read top-to-bottom; from the axis beat onward it is
   a *field* read by position. The upper-half working notes then shrink, so the
   sheet loses clutter exactly as it gains meaning.

Nothing here is a moving highlight. If you deleted every label, you could still
put the four frames back in order from the pictures alone.

## The payoff beat

**The beat where the two axes are ruled straight across the notes that are
already on the sheet.** Up to that moment the sheet has been a list of things you
told the tool; the instant the axes land, the same ink re-reads as coordinates,
and the cards begin to settle by position rather than by order. That is the
"turn those career criteria into a side-by-side view" line, drawn.

The second, quieter payoff is the final frame itself: the whole sheet visible at
once with all four zones named and every card sitting somewhere on purpose. It
could not have been drawn at 0:10 — at 0:10 there were no cards, no axes, and no
zones, only bare grid and a title.

## Why this is not lazy

A builder cannot satisfy this with heading swaps against a static prop, because
the contract is not the sheet — it is **what is on the sheet, and where**. Three
things must actually change beat to beat, and each is checkable from a contact
sheet by eye:

- **Mark count only goes up in the first half.** A frame in the back half of the
  walkthrough that has no more ink than the frame before it is a failed beat. A
  static prop with a new heading has identical ink and fails immediately.
- **The axes are absent for the entire first half and present for the entire
  second half.** That is a one-way structural change to the prop itself; you
  cannot fake it with copy.
- **The same card labels must persist across the migration.** A card that exists
  in the stack at the axis beat must be findable, same label, in a zone at the
  end. Re-generating fresh generic cards for the plot would break the through-
  line and would be visible in the shuffle test.

**Fabrication guard for the builder.** Do not put a count on screen. The script
gives exactly six approach names and says you may pick one, two, or three — six
tiles is script-backed, a selected count is not, so show a selection without
asserting how many. The number of option cards on the plot is whatever the
narration names; if the narration names none, use a small handful and never
letter a total anywhere on the sheet. The script's only path/job figures appear
inside "for example" phrasing — treat them as illustrations, never as content to
draw. Every word lettered on the sheet must be a word the narration says: the two
axis labels, the four zone names, the six approach names, and the profile fields
the script lists. Nothing else gets lettered, and no tool screen feature the
script does not describe gets drawn.
