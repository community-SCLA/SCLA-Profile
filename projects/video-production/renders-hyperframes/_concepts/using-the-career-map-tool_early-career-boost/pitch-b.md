# PITCH B — using-the-career-map-tool_early-career-boost

**Lens: accumulation-first.** The build-up was designed before the object was
named. Runtime assumed ~3:00 (this script carries an 8-part walkthrough plus a
two-axis model); the marks below scale proportionally if the refined narration
lands shorter or longer.

Every mark, label and control named below is spoken or written in the script.
Nothing on the frame is a screen feature the script does not describe, and no
count, order or outcome is asserted that the narration does not state.

---

## The accumulation ladder

What the frame has physically GAINED at each mark. The sheet's four regions
(header band, left intake margin, right shelf, centre working field) are in
place from the first beat and never move — everything below is new marks
landing inside fixed geography, not the picture being swapped.

**By ~0:45 — the field gains its first held thing, and the shelf appears full.**
The centre field is still bare grid. What is new: a header band carrying the
step in the sheet's own hand ("Map your career"), one drawn control block ticked
in the left margin, and — the big arrival — the right shelf now holds the six
approach tiles the script lists by name (Traditional path, Fast track,
Entrepreneurial, Work-life balance, Location independent, Social impact). One
tile has been lifted out of the shelf and pinned into the centre field. Net:
**+6 labelled tiles on the shelf, −1 from it, +1 pinned chip, +1 ticked control.**
The frame is now populated where it was empty, and the shelf has its first gap.

**By ~1:30 — density, not more objects.** The pinned chips (up to three, the
script's own limit) have not multiplied — they have THICKENED. Each pinned chip
has grown a small stack of ruled detail lines beneath it: target industries,
specific goals. The left margin has filled with the profile block (three short
ruled lines — name, preferred industry, general background) and a dog-eared
resume slip clipped over its lower corner. The shelf now shows its gaps.
Net: **the same chips carry three to four times the ink they carried at 0:45,
the margin goes from one control to a filled intake column, and the shelf is
visibly emptier.** Denser, not busier.

**By ~2:15 — re-sorted, and the empty two-thirds finally claimed.** The
generation beat lands: the bare lower centre field is ruled into horizontal
path lanes, and job chips sit along each lane in sequence. A summary line is
written above them in the sheet's hand, carrying the script's own example
wording. Each lane's left end gains a quick-stats strip (roles, industry,
focus). One lane is bracketed as the selected path; along the bottom edge a
time-frame band is ruled (2026–2030) and the selected lane's job chips step
upward across it, each chip taller than the one before — responsibilities and
expectations growing over time. Net: **the region that had been empty for two
minutes is now the densest part of the sheet, and the material that arrived as
loose chips is now connected into lanes.**

**By ~3:00 — newly connected, and everything re-placed.** Two heavy ruled
strokes cross the centre field — horizontal and vertical — landing on the grid
that has been there since beat one. Every chip that arrived over the video is
unpinned from its lane and re-placed as a plotted, labelled mark inside one of
four named zones. The lanes are erased down to faint ghost rules. The shelf is
empty. The margin's control stack is complete. Net: **nothing new is added — the
whole accumulated population is re-sorted into a single readable field.**

**One honest subtraction, ~2:30.** On the regenerate beat the confirmation
overlays the sheet and the pinned approaches and job chips dim to outline —
the script says plainly that the old career approaches and jobs will be
removed. Two drawn buttons: Regenerate, Cancel. A field that can visibly LOSE
is a field the viewer believes is really holding something.

## The carrying object

**A single large sheet of squared worksheet paper clipped to a drafting board —
the career map worksheet — that fills the frame from the first beat to the last
and gains every mark of the lesson on its own surface.**

It is drawn as a warm off-white rectangle occupying roughly 80% of the frame
inside the safe area, ruled with a faint square grid, held by a flat metal clip
bar across the top and a rail of punched holes down the left edge. Content
arrives as flat outlined shapes: approach tiles are wide rounded rectangles with
a 2px ink outline and a pin dot; job chips are narrower rectangles with one
rule-line inside; the resume is a rectangle with a folded corner; the tool's
controls are small solid blocks in the left margin carrying the script's own
button words. The two axes at the end are simply two heavy strokes drawn along
existing grid lines — they need no new space, they claim space that was already
ruled, which is what lets the final picture re-read rather than replace.

Marks land and then hold. The sheet gains by new ink entering, never by settled
ink moving — no drift, pulse or re-mark of anything already placed.

---

## Four frames

**25% (~0:45).** Wide sheet, mostly bare grid. Header band reads the landing
section's title. Right shelf: six labelled approach tiles in a 3×2 block, one
gap where a tile has been lifted. Centre field: one pinned chip, alone, with a
lot of empty grid around it. Left margin: one ticked control block. *Reads as:
an intake sheet just started.*

**50% (~1:30).** Same sheet, same regions, completely different weight. Right
shelf now visibly gapped. Centre upper field: up to three pinned chips, each
carrying a stack of ruled detail lines. Left margin: a filled profile block plus
a dog-eared resume slip. Centre LOWER two-thirds: still bare grid — a deliberate
reserved emptiness the viewer can feel is waiting. *Reads as: a form that has
been fed, waiting on something.*

**75% (~2:15).** The lower two-thirds is now the loudest part of the frame:
ruled path lanes with job chips strung along them, a quick-stats strip at each
lane's head, a summary line above, one lane bracketed, and along the bottom a
time-frame band with that lane's job chips climbing in a staircase. The upper
pinned chips are still there but no longer the focus. *Reads as: output, sorted
into routes.*

**100% (~3:00).** Two heavy axis strokes cross the field, labelled along the
edges with the script's own wording — personal fit and energy across, long-term
value and career capital up. Four zones named in their corners: Prioritize,
Explore, Enjoy or Side, Park. Every chip from the whole video sits plotted in a
zone. Lane rules survive only as faint ghosts; the shelf is empty; the margin
stack is complete. *Reads as: a decision, seen all at once.*

Four different pictures: bare, fed, sorted, judged. No two could be mistaken for
each other on a contact sheet, and their order can be restored from the
pictures alone — the shelf only ever empties, the field only ever fills.

## The payoff beat

The closing beat, on the line about seeing your options mapped out doing two
things at once — widening the frame and applying consistent criteria to all of
them. The camera holds the whole sheet for the first time since the opening
beat, and it re-reads twice in one picture: the grid that has been quietly
underneath the entire lesson is revealed to have been a coordinate field all
along, and the loose material the viewer watched arrive one piece at a time is
revealed to have been one comparable set all along. The frame at 0:10 was a
blank ruled sheet with a shelf; this frame is that same sheet, and it could not
have been drawn then — not by adding a heading, only by everything that
happened in between.

A secondary re-read lands at the generation beat (~2:00), when loose pinned
chips become connected lanes; it earns the final one by teaching the viewer
that this sheet re-sorts.

---

## Why this is not lazy

A builder cannot satisfy this with heading swaps against a static prop, because
the contract is a **population and a position**, not a caption:

- The shelf must hold six named tiles and then hold fewer. A static prop cannot
  show a gap where a tile was.
- The pinned chips must gain interior ruled lines between 0:45 and 1:30 — the
  same chips, more ink. Swapping the heading above them changes nothing
  measurable; the chip's own height changes.
- The lower two-thirds of the sheet must be provably empty at 1:30 and provably
  the densest region at 2:15. That single fact makes a static background
  impossible, and it is trivially checkable on a contact sheet.
- The regenerate beat requires the sheet to lose ink and then be intact again.
  A prop that never loses anything fails it outright.
- The final beat requires every chip to be at a DIFFERENT coordinate than it
  was at 2:15, on axes that did not exist at 2:15. Re-placement is not a
  heading swap by any construction.

Each of those is a milestone frame with a countable state (tiles on shelf,
lines per chip, chips in the lower field, chips per zone), so CONCEPT.md can
pin them and the builder's own contact-sheet review can fail them. The failure
mode the thin cut had — a small motif on near-empty frames that never gains
detail — is structurally unavailable here: the object is the frame, and the
frame's emptiness is scheduled to disappear.
