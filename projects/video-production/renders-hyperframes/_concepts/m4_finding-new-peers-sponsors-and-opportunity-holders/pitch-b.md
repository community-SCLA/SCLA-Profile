# PITCH B — m4_finding-new-peers-sponsors-and-opportunity-holders

**Lens:** accumulation-first. The build-up was designed before the object was named.
**Runtime assumption:** 487 script words → **~215s (3:35)**. Quarter marks ≈ 0:54 / 1:48 / 2:38 / 3:35.

---

## The accumulation ladder

What the frame has physically GAINED at each mark. Every rung is irreversible — nothing that arrives ever leaves, so the picture can always be ordered in time.

**By 0:30 — it gains TENSION and DIRECTION.**
Opens on slack: a loose heap of thread and an empty wooden frame, nothing holding anything. By 0:30 the first dozen threads have been lifted out of the heap and anchored end to end across the frame — taut, parallel, all running the same way. Same material as the opening shot; it is now under load instead of in a pile.

**By 1:05 — it gains DENSITY, then its FIRST CROSSING.**
The heap is spent into a full-width sheet of parallel threads spanning the whole frame — the field arrives once, and is never re-supplied. Then the first thread that runs *across* them is laid in and bound at both edges, and a strip of the parallels stops being parallels and becomes one bound band. The frame gains its first square inch of actual fabric.

**By 1:45 — it gains a PASSAGE, and a SELECTION.**
A lifting bar drops into the sheet and raises alternate threads, splitting the flat field into two layers with a wedge-shaped opening through the middle — a gap wide enough for the crossing thread to pass. Nothing crosses without that gap. At the same mark the field narrows in importance: three to five threads are drawn forward and marked as the ones the pattern will actually ride on; the rest stay drawn, but recede.

**By 2:38 — it gains BODY, and the source visibly SPENDS.**
Rows stack. The bound band grows from a strip to a third of the frame and shows three structurally different weave sections, not three colours of the same stitch. Mass migrates downward: the fabric deepens, the unwoven sheet above it visibly shortens, the heap beside the frame is nearly gone.

**By 3:35 — it gains LOAD.**
The finished band comes off the frame, unrolls to full length so the whole pattern reads in one look, and is looped so that it is carrying something. The frame behind it is bare. The leftover heap is still on the bench, untouched, holding nothing.

## The carrying object

**A table loom weaving a single carrying strap** — a wooden frame loom seen three-quarter-on, with a sheet of tensioned threads, a lifting bar, a shuttle, and a band of cloth growing row by row into a strap that can take weight.

It is drawn as a real object with real parts, in the brand ink line: two turned beams top and bottom, a side frame with visible peg holes and grain, the **warp** (the up-and-down threads, drawn as a dense ruled sheet, individually visible), the **heddle** (a slotted bar lying across them that lifts every other thread and opens a wedge-shaped gap called the shed), a wound **shuttle** with a real body and taper, and the **cloth** — an interlaced over-under texture that is genuinely denser and darker than the open warp above it. The three relationship kinds are three physically different parts doing three different jobs: **peers are the warp** (alongside you, same tension, same direction, holding the length), **sponsors are the weft** (the thread that crosses your direction and binds you into a structure you were not in), **opportunity holders are the heddle** (they open the gap the crossing thread has to pass through — closed heddle, no pass). A second, smaller object persists the whole runtime: the heap of loose thread on the bench.

## Four frames

**Frame 1 — 25% (≈0:54, "Sponsors are more senior leaders who advocate for you").**
The loom fills the canvas. The warp is dressed full width: a tall, dense field of parallel verticals from top beam to bottom, edge to edge. At the very base, ONE crossing thread lies in and is bound around the outer threads on both sides — a single horizontal against a hundred verticals. The heap on the bench is visibly smaller than it was at 0:10. *Signature: a tall striped field, one horizontal at its foot.*

**Frame 2 — 50% (≈1:48, "not to meet everyone — a small, strategic map").**
The geometry breaks. The heddle sits across the warp and is lifted, so the flat field has split into an upper and a lower layer with a triangular opening driven through the middle of the frame; the shuttle sits inside that opening, mid-pass. Below it, a hand-deep band of finished cloth. Three to five warp threads are drawn forward, heavier in the line weight, running up through both layers. *Signature: a wedge of open space cutting the field in two — nothing like Frame 1.*

**Frame 3 — 75% (≈2:38, searching your future field / spotting sponsors above you).**
The mass has inverted. Cloth now occupies the lower third and has begun rolling onto the bottom beam; the unwoven warp above it is markedly shorter than in Frame 1, the top beam closer. The band shows three stacked, structurally distinct sections — plain over-under rows, a twisted paired row, a wide-banded row — so the fabric reads as a record of what has already been argued. The bench heap is down to a few strands. *Signature: bottom-heavy, textured, the loom running out of raw material.*

**Frame 4 — 100% (≈3:35, the closing question).**
The loom is no longer the subject. The finished strap lies unrolled on the diagonal across the frame at full length, every section of its pattern legible at once, and its far end is looped through and pulled taut around a load it is holding. Behind it, the loom stands bare and slack. On the bench, the leftover heap — still loose, still holding nothing. *Signature: an object off the machine, under load.*

## The payoff beat

**At "networking is not about collecting business cards… it's about building real relationships" (≈3:12–3:27).** The strap is cut free of the loom, drops out of the vertical grid it has lived in for three minutes, and is looped so it takes weight — beside the untouched heap of loose thread, which cannot. That is the whole lesson in one frame: loose threads accumulate and carry nothing; interlaced ones carry. It is unfakeable at 0:10, because at 0:10 there is no cloth in the frame at all — only slack. The closing question then re-reads the finished strap end to end, one last pass over ground already earned.

## Why this is not lazy

- **Heading swaps against a static prop are impossible.** The row count is a monotone clock. Every beat has to physically deepen the cloth and shorten the warp above it, so a builder who reuses a frame is caught by looking, not by measuring — the shuffle test is trivial to pass here and trivial to fail.
- **It is not a network diagram.** There are no nodes, no dots, no lines drawn between people. The three script categories are three *mechanically different parts* of one machine — a tensioned thread, a crossing thread, and a bar that opens a gap — each of which does a visibly different job on screen.
- **It is not the batch's peg-and-thread board.** No pins, no spans between points, no board, no map. The subject is a growing textile body on a wooden frame, and the composition inverts across the runtime as mass migrates from warp to cloth to a strap that has left the loom — four different geometries, not four labels on one picture.
- **The field arrives once, then is only re-read** — the pattern the two APPROVED reference cuts share. After ~1:05 no new material enters the frame; everything later is that same material bound, structured and spent.
- **The "not everyone" line has a drawn answer.** The heap that never gets used is on screen from the first frame to the last, so the closing argument is made by an object the viewer has been watching the whole time rather than by a caption.
- **Fidelity:** no counts, names or sequence claims beyond the script's own — three kinds of relationship, three to five strategic ones. No people are depicted.
