# Pitch A — m4_finding-new-peers-sponsors-and-opportunity-holders

**Lens: metaphor-first.**

## The carrying object

**A landscape modeller's sand tray on trestles — a shallow timber-framed box of
damp sand that a pair of hands shapes into the small piece of ground the viewer
actually needs, with a few carved wooden seats pressed into it.**

Drawn as: a rectangle of oak framing with visible corner joints and end-grain,
sitting on two trestle legs; inside it a bed of sand rendered as parallel rake
furrows in flat brand-token fills, with shaped relief read by hard-edged
shadow shapes rather than gradients — a plateau is a flat plane with a stepped
shadow on its downhill side, a hollow is a rimmed oval with a dark crescent
inside it. The moving parts are three drawn tools and three carved blanks: a
wooden rake, a card scraper, a hand; and tiny turned seats in three silhouettes
— a low flat bench, a squat stool, a taller chair with a straight back. Camera
sits at a low three-quarter angle for most of the runtime, so relief is legible,
and rises to straight-down exactly once, at the end.

**Explicitly not:** no route, trail, track or line is ever raked into the sand;
no seat is ever arranged in a ring; nothing connects to anything with a stroke.
Relationship is read as *height and adjacency in the ground*, never as an edge.

## Four frames

**25% — one patch of ground, cleared (peers).**
The tray is still 90% dead-flat sand under unbroken rake furrows. One shallow
plateau has been brushed clear at the near edge, its surface scraped smooth, and
three low bench-seats sit flush in it at exactly the same height, shoulder to
shoulder, irregularly spaced. The rake lies across the frame rail; a hand is
still on it. Nothing has height yet — the whole tray reads as one level.

**50% — the ground gains height and a far side (sponsors, opportunity holders).**
Now the tray has real relief. Behind the near plateau the sand rises in three
stepped terraces, each step's shadow visible, and into the top terrace a broad
rectangular hollow has been pressed flat — a table-shaped depression, the place
the ground is worked hardest. Beyond a low saddle in the ridge, a second ground
sits higher and completely bare: raked, untouched, no seats on it at all. A hand
is brushing loose sand *off* the near plateau, uncovering two more bench-seats
that were already down there, half-buried.

**75% — the far ground is entered, and the sand is packed.**
Seats now sit at three distinct heights: benches on the near plateau, one chair
on the terraces beside the table-hollow, one on the far ground across the saddle.
A hand is pressing damp sand up around the base of each seat so it holds — the
banked collar around each one drawn as a thickened rim with its own shadow. In
the same frame, a seat that was simply set down on dry loose sand has tipped, its
hollow collapsed into a slumped cone. Two states of the same act, side by side in
one tray. The rest of the tray is untouched.

**100% — straight down, the whole tray at once.**
The camera is directly overhead for the first time. Three-quarters of the surface
is unbroken rake furrow, edge to edge. In one corner sits the entire worked model
— five seats in all, at three heights, the terraces reading as concentric
step-shadows, the saddle a notch, the table-hollow a dark rectangle. One last
thumb-pressed hollow sits empty beside the highest seat, rim sharp, nothing in it
yet. The rake is set down on the rail, out of the sand.

## What accumulates

Three things, all visible, none of them a moving highlight:

1. **Relief.** Flat → one cleared plateau → three terraces and a table-hollow →
   a saddle and a far ground. The tray goes from a single plane to a shaped
   landscape, and the shadow shapes are drawn, so height is countable.
2. **Seats.** 0 → 3 → 5, and they stop climbing. They also spread across three
   heights rather than clustering, so the *distribution* changes as well as the
   count.
3. **Packed ground.** Early seats sit in scraped sand; late ones sit in banked,
   damped collars. The same object gains a detail the earlier version doesn't
   have, which is what makes the final overhead a re-read rather than a recap.

And the fourth, which is the argument: **the untouched sand stays untouched.**
The share of the tray that has never been worked is the picture of "your goal is
not to meet everyone." It is only readable because the rest accumulated.

## The payoff beat

**The overhead lift on the closing question.** For the entire runtime the tray has
been seen at a low angle, one worked region at a time, so the viewer has never
seen how much sand was left alone. The camera rises to straight-down and the
whole tray resolves at once: a small, deliberate, five-seat model in one corner of
a large flat expanse — which is exactly "a small, strategic map of the people who
are most relevant." The final thumb-hollow, pressed and empty next to the highest
seat, lands on "how can I begin a genuine, respectful relationship with them?"
This frame is undrawable at 0:10, when the tray is blank sand and there is no
relief to look down on and nothing to leave untouched.

## Why this is not lazy

- **It cannot be satisfied with heading swaps against a static prop.** The prop's
  geometry is the content: the sand is flat at 25%, three-stepped at 50%, banked
  and packed at 75%, and viewed from a new camera at 100%. Milestone frames can
  fix the exact relief state and seat count at each beat, so a builder who leaves
  the tray unchanged fails the contract visibly, not subtly.
- **It sidesteps the node-and-line trap on purpose.** A lesson about people wants
  to become circles joined by strokes. Here nothing is ever joined. The three
  kinds of relationship are read as three *heights of ground* — level with you,
  stepped above you, across the saddle — which is a direct reading of the script's
  own "one to three levels above you" and "who already sits near the
  opportunities." The seats are carved objects with silhouettes and shadows, not
  tokens on a plane.
- **The anti-pattern gets a picture, not a caption.** "Not collecting business
  cards or asking for favors at random" is drawn as a seat dropped on loose dry
  sand tipping over beside one bedded into packed, damped ground. That is the
  script's own contrast rendered as a physical failure, in the same object.
- **Every count comes from the narration.** Three kinds of relationship, one to
  three levels above, three-to-five relationships in the map — the model ends at
  three heights and five seats, and no other number is asserted anywhere. No
  names, no faces, no nameplates: the seats are unmarked wooden blanks.
- **Batch-distinct.** No bricks, no wall, no skyline, no paper or ruled sheet, no
  pegs or thread, no field feeding a strip, no trail marker, no track or
  checkpoint, no plant, no truss, no light table, no canvas rack, no lens, no
  upright core. It is a hand-worked tray of shaped ground with carved seats in it,
  and the hands, rake and shadows are always on screen doing work.
