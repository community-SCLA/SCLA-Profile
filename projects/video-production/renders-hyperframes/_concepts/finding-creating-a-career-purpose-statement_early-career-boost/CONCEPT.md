# CONCEPT — finding-creating-a-career-purpose-statement_early-career-boost

**Judged:** 2026-08-05, concept-judge stage (B2). Bracket: the two APPROVED rows
in `design-system/docs/taste.md` only. Winner: **Pitch B**, with one idea
grafted from Pitch A.

---

## Concept angle

A field of blank note-scraps re-sorts into three feeder columns and empties
upward into a single ruled statement strip, which fills, tightens, is copied
three times, and is finally mounted with a loop pointing back at it — the board
is on screen from the first content beat to the last (≈beat 2 through the final
beat, ~95% of runtime).

---

## The carrying object

**One board, drawn as a single hero illustration occupying the middle and lower
bands of the frame. The heading sits in the normal top-left heading zone; the
board is the only illustration on the frame.**

- **The strip** — one wide rounded rectangle across the upper part of the board
  area. Border is a 3px *dashed* brand-blue outline, not solid: dashed is how
  "it is not permanent" is drawn, and it stays dashed for the whole video. Two
  hairline rules inside at the one-third and two-thirds heights — that is "one
  or two sentences", drawn as capacity rather than stated. A square crop bracket
  clamps its right edge. Three faint vertical ticks divide its interior into
  three slots (they arrive at the 40% mark, not at the start).
- **The scraps** — roughly two dozen small rounded rectangles, ~90×56px, in
  three brand tints, each rotated a few degrees off square, each carrying two
  short grey graphic rules inside. **They carry no type at all**, so nothing on
  the board sits under the 40px floor. A scrap only becomes readable copy when
  it is promoted into a slot and enlarges. The field is never counted, numbered,
  or labelled on frame — same discipline as the approved 48-mark cut.
- **The columns** — the same scraps, later, standing in three tight vertical
  stacks with aligned edges, each parked directly beneath one slot, each with a
  left-edge label taken from the script's own three sources.
- **The chips** — five small pills that clip one at a time to the strip's lower
  rail, one per quality the script names. Exactly five; no synthesized sixth.
- **The drafts** — three flatter, rougher repeats of the strip, stacked below
  it. Drawn deliberately *imperfect* (see Judge's note).
- **The mount** — a plain bracket with one up-tick at the head of the board.
- **The loop** — one closed rounded-rect arrow ruled around the board's lower
  margin, arrowhead landing on the mounted line. It exists only in the final
  beat.
- **The margin tags** — two small struck-through tags outside the crop bracket,
  right margin. They arrive and are never removed.

Everything that arrives, stays. Nothing on this board is ever cleared.

---

## Milestone frames — the accumulation contract

The builder scales these to the synthesized wav; they are fractions of runtime,
not fixed seconds (the script runs ~375 narrated words, so expect ~2:20–2:40).
**Each milestone must be a different picture because the object changed, not
because the heading changed.** A snapshot grid that cannot show all four states
has not built this concept.

**25% — Full mess, empty container.**
The dashed two-line strip is ruled across the top of the board with its crop
bracket and a small question tag hanging beneath it. Below it, ~24 blank scraps
sit in an unsorted drift — tilted, overlapping, spread across the lower
two-thirds. Nothing is aligned to anything. The strip is conspicuously,
deliberately empty. *Countable state: 0 slots ticked, 0 filled, 0 chips, 0 tags,
0 column holes.*

**50% — Order below, still empty above.**
The drift is gone as a drift. The same scraps now stand in three tight vertical
columns, edges aligned, each labelled at its left and parked under one third of
the strip. The strip has gained its three division ticks. The picture is now a
grid feeding a container, and the container has still not been filled. Same
marks as 25%, completely different geometry. *Countable state: 3 slots ticked,
0 filled, 0 chips, 0 tags, 0 holes.*

**75% — Filled, labelled twice, hung with chips.**
Each slot carries one enlarged, legible line with the connective words inked
between them, so the strip reads as one continuous sentence. A second label row
sits beneath the three slots — direction / contribution / a reason — added
without any new content entering. Four or five chips are clipped along the lower
rail, and on the last one the strip's right edge has pulled in one notch. Two
struck-through tags sit in the right margin, outside the bracket. The columns
below are visibly gap-toothed. Dense at the top, thinning at the bottom — the
inverse of 25%. *Countable state: 3 filled, 5 chips, 2 tags, 1 dimensional
change, ≥3 holes.*

**100% — The whole board at once.**
One line is mounted under the bracket at the head of the board. Three rough
drafts sit below the strip's old position, one of them circled. A closed loop
arrow runs the lower margin with its arrowhead touching the mounted line. The
columns are sparse and holed. The five chips, two margin tags and crop bracket
are all still present. The eye reads the entire run bottom-to-top: scraps →
columns → strip → drafts → the one line, with the loop pointing back at it.
*Countable state: everything above, plus 1 selection mark, 1 mount, 1 closed
loop, ~12 holes.*

## What accumulates

Five independent counters, every one of them monotone — nothing is ever cleared,
so any two snapshots can be put back in order by counting alone (the shuffle
test, passed by construction):

1. **Column holes.** 0 → 0 → 3 → ~12. Scraps leave their columns and never
   return; the gap they left stays visible.
2. **The strip's interior state.** Empty → ticked into three slots → three slots
   filled with enlarged legible copy → filled plus a second label row. Four
   structural values, none of them a heading swap.
3. **Attached chips.** 0 → 0 → 5, arriving one at a time, never removed.
4. **Margin tags.** 0 → 0 → 2, arriving at the beat that names what is *not* in
   the statement, and kept on the board as evidence rather than deleted.
5. **The object's own geometry.** The right edge tightens one notch on "brief";
   the strip relocates onto a mount on the last beat; a loop that never existed
   is ruled around the board.

The scrap field also re-sorts once — drift → three aligned columns — which is
the approved-reference move: a field that arrives once and is then only ever
re-grouped.

---

## The payoff beat

**The final beat: the mount and the closed loop.**

Two things resolve on the same beat. The object **completes** — the strip that
was ruled empty and dashed at the top of the run is now filled, chosen,
tightened and lifted onto a mount at the head of the board, and the loop's
arrowhead lands on it. That is the script's "you can revise it every single time
you run your decision loop" and "a purpose, in your own words, to point it at",
drawn as one connection rather than said twice. And the board **re-reads**: once
the loop closes, the emptied columns, the drafts and the discarded tags stop
being leftovers and become the visible method — which is what the script says
confidence actually comes from.

A second, smaller re-read happens mid-run: the three filled slots gain the
direction / contribution / reason label row with no new content entering — the
same three objects, read a second way.

This closing frame is not drawable at 0:10, where the board holds one empty
dashed rectangle and two dozen wordless marks.

## Grafted from the runner-up

**The third ingredient arrives from off-board, not from a column.**

Pitch A's sharpest single observation: the script's three ingredients are not
symmetric. The first two are retrospective — "the kind of work you *noticed*
yourself coming alive in", and a criterion you already wrote — so they are
promoted up out of the columns, leaving holes. The third is prospective: "the
strengths and skills you are **growing toward**". It is not in the work already
done, so it must not be drawn coming out of a column. It arrives into slot 3
from off the board's edge, drawn faint on arrival, and column three loses no
scrap at that moment.

Carried across because it fixes Pitch B's one mechanical stretch — three
identical promotions in a row — with an asymmetry the narration itself states,
introduces no fact the script does not say, and gives the third column a
different, later reason to lose marks (the draft-writing beat).

## Judge's note

**Failure mode this concept will slide into: the last third becomes three
near-identical strips.** Once the drafts appear, the board risks reading as the
same picture three times with new headings — the monotony read, and exactly the
stretch a viewer checks out on.

**Guard, concretely:** the three drafts must be individually distinguishable at
contact-sheet scale and must be drawn *rougher* than the strip they copy — one
with a line that overruns its slot, one with a line that falls visibly short,
one with an uneven baseline. That is the script's "honest and rough over
polished and empty" drawn rather than captioned. Second guard: **the column-hole
count must differ in every snapshot after the 60% mark** — each draft pulls its
marks from the columns, so no two frames in the final third can show the same
column geometry. If a builder produces two consecutive snapshots with identical
column geometry in that window, the concept has been broken, not merely
under-decorated.

**Standing constraints for the builder:** the board is the ONE hero illustration
per frame — no icons beside label rows. Scraps carry no type, so the 40px floor
applies only to promoted, enlarged copy. Every gain is a single one-time
entrance or move, after which the element is pixel-static; the right-edge notch
is one declared move, not a keep-alive. No count, sequence or claim on frame that
the narration does not say — the scrap field is never counted, and there are
exactly five quality chips.

## Scores

**Pitch A — index card on a crowded desk.** Strongest on question 1: "an index
card on a desk" is the most nameable object either pitch offered, and the
stateful-prop argument closes the lazy-build test cleanly (question 5). Its four
frames are genuinely four pictures (question 2), and the payoff — the slot
dividers dropping so three docked pieces butt into one continuous line — is the
more elegant of the two completions (question 4). It fails on question 3, and it
fails at the worst possible place. A's accumulation runs in reverse at the end:
the closing frame clears the piles, the residue, the discard margin and the two
losing drafts, leaving a single small card drawn "the smallest it has ever been"
on an empty desk. That is the thin-carrier picture — a small prop beside large
empty space — landing on the one frame a viewer stares at longest, and it is a
frame that *could* plausibly have been drawn at 0:10. Both APPROVED reference
cuts end on their fullest frame, with every segment, bracket and station still
on screen. A ends on its emptiest.

**Pitch B — scraps, columns, statement strip.** Slightly weaker on question 1 —
"a ruled strip fed by a field of scraps" is a board, not a household object, and
it needs the builder to keep it from reading as a diagram. It wins everywhere
else. Question 2: four states that differ structurally, including a 25→50
transition where the same marks produce completely different geometry. Question
3: five monotone counters and nothing ever cleared, which is precisely the taste
target of visibly MORE at the end, and it reproduces both approved signatures at
once — a field that arrives once and is only re-grouped, and a persistent rail
that gains segments and clipped attachments. Question 4: the mount plus closed
loop both completes the object and re-reads the whole board. Question 5: its
milestone contract is falsifiable off the snapshot grid by counting holes,
chips and tags, so a heading-swap build fails visibly rather than arguably.

**Verdict: Pitch B wins.** Pitch A's terminal clearing is disqualifying against
the bracket, and it is not a fixable detail — the emptying *is* A's argument. B
carries A's asymmetric third ingredient and A's rough-draft discipline as the
guard on its one weak stretch.
