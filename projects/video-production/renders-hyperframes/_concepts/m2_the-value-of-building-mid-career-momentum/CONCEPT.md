# CONCEPT — m2_the-value-of-building-mid-career-momentum

**Judged against the two APPROVED rows of `design-system/docs/taste.md` only.**
The 2026-08-05 `career-building-is-a-repeatable-process` ship is
SHIPPED-NOT-A-REFERENCE and was not used as an anchor.

**Runtime basis.** 349 narration words at the pinned Oxana voice ≈ **2:25
(~145s)** plus the 1.8s final hold. Percentages below resolve to roughly
~36s / ~73s / ~109s / ~145s. Reconcile against real word timings at build time.

## Concept angle

**A half-built truss span in elevation, growing out over a gap from three
standing footings while the heap of loose members on the near bank empties into
it** — the object is on screen from beat 2 to the final frame (~95% of runtime,
well past the 60% floor) and is the only hero illustration in the video.

## The carrying object

One flat-on **elevation** — no perspective, no photographic texture, flat brand
vector throughout — divided by a single ruled **ground line** across the lower
third of the canvas.

- **Left of frame, on the near bank: three solid inked footings**, stacked
  vertical masses standing on the ground line, already built at 0:00. Labelled
  in the script's own words: "Projects Delivered" / "Teams Supported" /
  "Stakeholders Managed".
- **Beside them, on the same bank: the stockpile** — a heap of **loose
  members**: short tapered bars lying at unaligned angles, unattached, no
  joints, overlapping each other. This is the only loose-texture register in
  the video and it lives only on the bank.
- **Right of the last footing: the gap** — open, empty canvas above the ground
  line, with the ground line itself dropping away and resuming far right as the
  **further ground**, drawn with a visibly different hatch so crossing to it
  reads as crossing into different territory.
- **Standing in the gap, drawn from the start but unbuilt-to: two marker
  piers** — a near one and a far one, thin uprights with a bracket ruled along
  the ground beneath each. Short bracket = "Ninety-Day Goal" at the near pier;
  long bracket = "Six To Twelve Months" running past it to the far pier.
- **The span itself**, once it starts: never a single stroke. It is always an
  **assembly of discrete pinned members** — top chord, bottom chord and visible
  internal diagonals forming triangulated bays, each bay a separately drawn
  unit with a dot at every joint. Deck thickness is real, not a hairline.

Two textures exist and never mix: **loose** (tapered, angled, unjointed, on the
bank) and **pinned** (ruled to the grid, jointed, over the gap). A member's
register is readable at thumbnail size, which is what makes the transfer visible
without reading a word.

## Milestone frames — the builder's ACCUMULATION CONTRACT

These four are a contract, not a mood board. A build that cannot show all four
as distinct pictures has not built this concept.

**25% (~36s) — left-heavy, nothing over the gap.**
The stockpile dominates: loose members heaped so densely on the bank that the
ground line barely shows through, and the heap has visibly **sorted itself into
two postures** — one slumped low and flat against the bank ("Quiet
Frustration"), one stacked high and leaning outward off the pile's crest
("Growing Ambition"). Right of the footings: nothing but open air over the gap.
The two marker piers stand alone in it, unconnected to anything. A viewer reads:
enormous unsorted supply, nothing committed. This is the script's "a lot of
noise in your head and very little on paper."

**50% (~73s) — the first structure over the gap, and the first transfer.**
Four things exist that did not at 25%. (1) An **anchor plate** ruled onto the
face of the last footing — the stop, where building begins. (2) From it, a
**dotted unsupported line** running out over the gap with **no pier under it**
and no joints, fading off — drift, building out over nothing. (3) Beside it, a
**ruled span of two or three pinned bays**, triangulated, jointed, starting at
the anchor plate and stopping in mid-air short of the near pier. (4) One member
already placed in that span has gone **ghost-grey outline**, on its own beat,
because nothing touched it — momentum decaying. The stockpile is **measurably
lower** than at 25%: the two postures still read, but the heap no longer
occludes the ground line. Nothing has forked yet.

**75% (~109s) — three continuations, three different geometries.**
At the leading edge of the built span, three candidate continuations are drawn
at once, and no two are the same shape:
1. **"Promotion"** — the footing under the span builds **upward**: a taller,
   heavier column on the same footprint, its bays packed tighter and its chords
   thicker, gaining height without leaving its own ground.
2. **"Lateral Move"** — a span that **departs the current alignment sideways**,
   angling off the line and landing on the differently-hatched further ground.
3. **"Role Redesign"** — the **already-built deck is re-trussed**: same two end
   points, same footprint, the internal diagonals re-cut into a different
   pattern of fewer, wider bays.
Laid across all three at once is **one level bar** — a single ruled instrument
resting on all three candidates so they are measured against the same line, its
three notches labelled "Values" / "Energizes You" / "Life Constraints". No
candidate is crossed out, greyed or drawn heavier than the others. The stockpile
is down to roughly half its 25% mass; for the first time in the video **more
drawn material sits over the gap than on the bank** — the crossover.

**100% (~145s) — one continuous span reaching the near pier, bank empty.**
The stockpile holds nothing; the bank is quiet ground under three footings. The
frame reads left to right as one object: three footings → anchor plate → the
pinned span → the junction with all three candidates still standing at equal ink
weight → one of them carried on as a **run of bays that visibly thickens**, each
bay drawn heavier than the one before it, until the deck lands on and caps the
**near marker pier**, inside the short "Ninety-Day Goal" bracket. The long
"Six To Twelve Months" bracket runs on past it to the far pier, and **the far
ground is not reached** — the span stops where the script stops. Closing the
frame: a **hand-drawn ring**, the only stroke in the video not ruled to the
grid, closed around the near pier's **blank name plate**. The dotted drift line
is still there, still unsupported, never built on.

## What accumulates

A **conserved two-register budget**: material only ever leaves the bank and
lands over the gap. Nothing is created and nothing is erased.

1. **Pinned structure goes up.** Open gap → anchor plate → two or three bays →
   a junction with three candidate geometries → a thickening run landing on and
   capping the near pier. Every beat's picture contains every element of the
   previous beat plus at least one newly drawn member, so the shuffle test
   resolves in exactly one direction.
2. **The loose stockpile goes down, monotonically.** Heap occluding the ground
   line → half mass → stragglers → empty bank. It is spent, never deleted.
3. **The chosen run compounds.** Bays along it are drawn progressively heavier,
   so the deck's stroke weight at the near pier is visibly greater than at the
   anchor plate. A row of identical bays reads as a bullet list and defeats the
   closing argument.

At any two beats a viewer can count more pinned joints and less loose material.
Nothing here is a highlight moving around — that was the thin cut.

## The payoff beat

**The final beat — "one small, consistent, and compounding step at a time" —
where the hand-drawn ring closes around the near pier's blank name plate and the
bank goes empty on the same beat.**

It completes the object twice. It completes the **structure**: the span is only
continuous once the near pier is capped, so the whole object first reads as one
thing at this beat and at no earlier beat. And it completes the **budget**: the
bank emptying on the same beat makes the conservation rule visible in
retrospect — the span did not gain material from nowhere, it absorbed the heap.
The ring is the one hand-drawn edge on an otherwise ruled object, so the
finished thing reads as authored by a person rather than printed.

This frame could not have been drawn at 0:10. At 0:10 there are three footings,
a full heap and open air; the closing frame requires the anchor plate, the
junction, three surveyed geometries, a compounding run and an exhausted bank to
already exist behind it.

**The name plate stays blank.** The script says the viewer names the move; the
plate is a ruled empty field the ring closes around. No invented move name.

## Grafted from the runner-up

**Pitch A's straightedge — one instrument laid across all three routes at
once.** Pitch B measured the three paths with three separate gauge marks;
A squared a single straightedge onto the grid and read all three against the
same ruling. A's version is better on three counts: it is **one object rather
than three widgets**, which respects the one-hero-illustration preference; it
makes "none of these paths is better in general" a **single physical act**
instead of a scoreboard that invites a winner; and it gives the 75% frame its
own distinct silhouette — a bar lying across a fan — so the milestone frame
cannot be confused with any other. It is carried in as the **level bar** with
three notches at the junction.

A's ghost-routes idea is carried in the same spirit: the two unchosen
geometries stay standing at **equal ink weight** to the last frame, evidence
that a choice was actually made rather than a default taken.

## Batch-diversity note

Both pitches landed on a **drafting board / gridded plan sheet**, and that
carrier is committed elsewhere in this batch — twice. This concept keeps the
winner's accumulation logic (conserved two-register budget, three distinct
lane geometries, a compounding run, a final enclosing stroke) and **re-casts it
from a flat sheet taking ink into a standing structure being assembled over a
gap.**

Against the committed list, item by item:

- **Not a drafting sheet or worksheet ruled into zones** — there is no sheet,
  no page grid and no ruled zones; the surface is a ground line and open air,
  and material is *pinned*, not inked.
- **Not bricks** (doorway, foundation wall, or skyline-into-wave) — nothing is
  stacked course by course into a mass; the span is triangulated open framing
  with visible joints and empty air showing through every bay, and it grows
  **horizontally out over a void**, not upward into a solid.
- **Not a peg-and-thread board** — no board, no pins-and-string; members are
  jointed structural bars with thickness, not threads between pegs.
- **Not a scrap field re-sorting into columns** — the stockpile never sorts
  into columns and never feeds a statement strip; it is *spent* into a
  structure and ends empty.
- **Not a trail marker on stacked experience** — the three footings are the
  starting ground, not the summit; nothing is stacked to make a marker.
- **Not a career track with a checkpoint node** — the m1 approved cut is a
  **thin horizontal rule with dot nodes**. The explicit guard against
  collapsing into it is in the Judge's note below: this object has vertical
  footing mass, triangulated internal structure, real deck thickness and a
  visible gap of open air beneath it, and is never drawn as a single unbroken
  stroke.

At thumbnail size the contact sheet reads as an elevation with mass on the left
migrating to structure on the right — nothing like a filling sheet, a wall, or
a rule with nodes.

## Judge's note

**The one failure mode this will slide into: the span degenerating into a
line — a thin horizontal rule with dots on it, plus labels.** That is the
easiest thing to draw, it is the m1 approved cut's silhouette rather than this
one's, and at that point the concept has become the thin carrier: a small prop
on a mostly empty canvas whose only change is where the highlight sits.

**The concrete guard, checkable from a contact sheet alone:**

1. **The span is never a single stroke.** Every built segment shows top chord,
   bottom chord and at least one internal diagonal, with a drawn joint dot at
   every member end. A segment with no visible internal diagonal is a defect.
2. **Mass, not hairlines.** Each footing's vertical height is at least three
   times the deck's thickness, and the deck's own thickness is legible at
   thumbnail size.
3. **The object earns its frame.** By the 50% mark the built structure plus the
   footings span at least 60% of canvas width; by 100% the span plus footings
   occupy the full width between the frame's safe margins. The gap of open air
   under the deck must stay visible — it is what makes this an elevation and
   not a rule.
4. **The inverse budget is per-beat.** At each of 25 / 50 / 75% the stockpile
   is *measurably* smaller and the pinned structure *measurably* larger than at
   the previous mark. A beat that changes neither is wrong at thumbnail size,
   and a heading swap changes neither.

**Two standing-rule notes for the builder.** The stockpile is **not** a drifting
animation — it is pixel-static within every beat and re-composed only at beat
boundaries as members depart; members land once and never re-animate
(`check_motion.py`). The one greying member is a single beat-scoped state
change, declared inline with `/* motion-allow: … */` if the motion gate reads it
as a re-mark.

**Fidelity guard — every element traces to a narration phrase.** Years of
experience → the three footings. Two feelings → the heap's two postures.
Noise vs. paper → loose heap vs. pinned structure. Drifting as the default →
the dotted unsupported line with no pier under it. Pause, reassess, choose →
the anchor plate. Momentum decays when untouched → the greyed member. Six to
twelve months and one ninety-day goal → the long and short brackets and their
two piers. Three paths → three different structural geometries. Values,
energizes you, life constraints → the level bar's three notches. None is better
in general → all three stay at equal ink weight, none crossed out. Not solving
everything at once → the span stops at the near pier and the far ground is
never reached. Small, consistent, compounding steps → the thickening run of
bays. A named move you can build toward → the blank name plate the hand-drawn
ring closes around. No count, date, statistic or ranking appears that the
script does not speak.

## Scores

**Pitch A — metaphor-first (drafting board, sheet, scraps).** Names a genuinely
concrete object and passes Q1 easily; the dual counter-moving accumulation (ink
up, loose paper down) is real accumulation, not a moving highlight, and the
three route *characters* — thicken in place, cross the boundary, redraw the
plot's own outline — are the single best structural idea in either pitch. It
weakens on Q2 and Q5. The 100% frame's differentiator is partly a **camera
pull-back** rather than the object earning more, which is exactly the move a
lazy builder reaches for when the sheet has stopped changing; the finished-sheet
stack beside the board is inert scenery that never participates; and between
50% and 75% the only committed changes are ink weight and a straightedge
rotation, which is thin material for a quarter of the runtime. Payoff (Q4) is
named and genuine. **Verdict: strong runner-up — best individual idea in the
round, weakest final quarter.**

**Pitch B — accumulation-first (gridded sheet, band of loose marks).** Wins on
the questions that actually predict a rejection. Q3 is answered by a
**conserved budget** rather than a list of additions — material can only move
between two registers, so "how far in are we" is legible from mass alone and
the shuffle test resolves in one direction by construction. Q2's four frames are
four structurally different pictures (top-heavy → two registers arguing →
branched and right-weighted → single-register and full), with the centre of mass
migrating across the canvas as a thumbnail-visible clock. Q5 is closed hardest:
five load-bearing, contact-sheet-checkable requirements, including the three
lanes being three geometries rather than one component instanced thrice — the
precise failure the thin-carrier rejection was. Q4's payoff completes drawing
and budget on the same beat. Its real risk is **over-labelling**: fifteen-plus
named elements crowding one frame, which the re-cast trims. **Verdict: WINNER,
re-cast per the batch-diversity constraint from a gridded sheet into a truss
span assembled over a gap, with Pitch A's single straightedge grafted in as the
level bar.**
