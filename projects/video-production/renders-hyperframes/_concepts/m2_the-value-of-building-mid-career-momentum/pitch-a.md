# PITCH A — metaphor-first · m2_the-value-of-building-mid-career-momentum

## The carrying object

**A drafting board, seen flat-on and filling the frame, holding one wide sheet of plan paper that starts blank under a drift of loose paper scraps and ends as a single drawn route.**

It is drawn as three stacked layers and nothing else: the board (a flat rectangle with a taped-down edge and a ruled straightedge resting across it), the sheet (a large pale rectangle with a faint square grid, the only surface that ever takes ink), and the scraps (small torn quadrilaterals of varying size, tumbled at angles, lying *on top of* the sheet and spilling past its edges). Off to one side of the board sits a short, squared-off stack of already-finished sheets, drawn edge-on as stacked horizontal bars — closed, done, not part of the drawing surface. Every mark on the sheet is one of three vocabularies: pencil line (thin, light), inked line (thick, brand accent), or a laid step (a short cross-tick sitting on a line). No third dimension, no photographic texture — flat brand vector throughout, so the sheet's fill state is legible at a glance from across a room.

## Four frames

**25% — "a lot of noise in your head and very little on paper."**
The board is buried. Scraps lie thick across the whole sheet at every angle, some overlapping the board's edges, so the grid only shows through in gaps. The stack of finished sheets beside the board is tall and squared — the years already delivered, closed and stacked, not the working surface. The sheet itself carries exactly one mark: a single small point near the lower-left, the spot you're standing on. The straightedge lies unused, off to the side, at an angle to the grid.

**50% — the three paths, just named.**
Scraps have been swept from the sheet's face into one tidy corner block, and the grid is fully visible for the first time. From that single point, three pencil routes now leave — and they are three genuinely different shapes, not three arrows with labels: one **turns back into the ground it started on and thickens there**, drawing tighter and heavier loops inside the same plot (deepening influence and scope right where you are); one **crosses the plot's boundary line** and continues onto the adjacent, differently-gridded ground (a new department, organisation, or function); one **stays inside the plot but redraws the plot's own outline**, its border re-cut into a new shape around the same point (reshaping responsibilities, focus, or schedule without leaving).

**75% — weighing, not choosing yet.**
The straightedge has been squared onto the grid and laid across all three routes at once, so the three are measured against the same ruling. Two routes have gone light — held in thin pencil, ghosted but never erased. The third is being inked: the ink runs partway along it and stops mid-stroke, the pen line visibly thicker than the pencil beneath it. The scrap block in the corner has not grown; the sheet's face stays clear.

**100% — the sheet read whole.**
The framing pulls back to show the entire board at once. One inked route runs unbroken from the original point to a ruled cross-marker on the sheet, with the far edge of the sheet beyond it as the further horizon; the ghost routes remain as faint pencil, still there, not chosen. Along the inked route sit the laid steps — short cross-ticks, evenly spaced, each one small, drawn in the order they were laid. At the head of the route, a single short named label sits where the point used to be alone. The scraps are one small squared block in the corner; the finished-sheet stack beside the board is unchanged.

## What accumulates

Two counter-moving quantities on the same object, both visible without reading a word:

1. **Ink goes up.** The sheet's face goes blank → one point → three pencil routes → one route half-inked → one route fully inked with steps laid along it. Marks are only ever *added*; nothing already drawn is deleted or moved.
2. **Loose paper goes down.** The scrap drift shrinks monotonically from covering the whole sheet, to a corner block, to a small squared block — swept, never vanished. The noise is not erased, it is *filed*.

The chosen route's steps are laid one at a time and each one stays, so the lane gets denser as the argument advances — the compounding is a picture, not a claim. Nothing here is a highlight moving around: at any two beats you can count more ink and less loose paper.

## The payoff beat

The final beat, on "you replace the noise with a named move you can actually build toward." The framing pulls back for the first time in the video and the whole board reads at once: the same single point that sat alone on an empty sheet at 25% is now the head of one continuous inked route, carrying a name, ticked with the steps that were laid one by one, with the two unchosen routes still faintly present as evidence that a choice was actually made. This frame is physically undrawable at 0:10 — at 0:10 the sheet was blank and covered.

## Why this is not lazy

The prop is the content, so there is no beat where the picture is legal without new drawing. A heading swap against a static board fails on sight: the scrap-drift extent, the number and weight of routes, the ink/pencil split, and the presence of laid steps are all different at every act, and each is a shape a builder must actually author. Three of the four milestone frames are *structurally* different pictures — a covered sheet, a three-route fan, a single inked lane — not one picture with new text.

The concept also refuses the two rejected failure modes by construction. It cannot go **thin**: the object occupies the full canvas from the first frame and gains permanent marks at essentially every beat, so the shuffle test is trivially passable — sheet fill order *is* time order. It cannot go **slow-and-churny**: nothing that has been drawn is ever allowed to move again (each beat adds ink or sweeps scraps, then holds), so richness comes from the sheet earning more, never from the picture swapping faster.

**Fidelity note.** Everything on screen is a re-reading of the narration's own words — years already delivered (the closed stack), noise vs. paper (scraps vs. grid), drift vs. a chosen direction (unruled scatter vs. a ruled route), three paths and their three described characters, values/energy/constraints as the one straightedge all three are measured against, the ninety-day goal as the ruled cross-marker and the six-to-twelve-month move as the sheet's far edge, and small consistent compounding steps as the laid ticks. No count, date, statistic, or ranking appears that the script does not speak; the scraps and the steps are deliberately **uncounted and unnumbered**, and no path is drawn as better than another — two stay visible as pencil to the last frame.
