# PITCH B — m2_the-value-of-building-mid-career-momentum

**Lens: accumulation-first.** The ladder below was written before the object was
named. Every rung is a physical statement about the frame — more, denser,
re-sorted, newly connected — not a topic label.

**Runtime basis.** 349 narration words at the pinned Oxana voice ≈ **2:25
(~145s)** plus the 1.8s final hold. Marks below use 30 / 60 / 90 / 120 / close;
the four-frame section uses the true 25 / 50 / 75 / 100% of ~145s
(~36s / ~73s / ~109s / ~145s). Reconcile against real word timings at build time.

## The accumulation ladder

The whole concept runs on a **conserved two-register budget**: there is a stock
of loose marks and a stock of drawn ink, and the only way ink appears is for
loose marks to leave. Every beat moves material from one register to the other,
so "how far in are we" is legible from mass alone.

**Start state (0–10s).** Two registers. An upper band, thin and quiet. A lower
field, empty except for three solid inked blocks stacked hard against its left
edge — the only thing already drawn.

**By ~30s — gained: overwhelming mass in the wrong register, and internal
grouping inside it.** The upper band has filled edge to edge with loose graphite
ticks, far denser than anything below it. Those ticks have also sorted themselves
into two clusters with different postures: one that hangs low and heavy along the
band's floor, one that streams up toward the band's ceiling. The lower field has
gained nothing since 0:10 — still three blocks and empty space. The frame's whole
weight sits above the line. That imbalance is the picture, and it is directly the
script's "a lot of noise in your head and very little on paper."

**By ~60s — gained: the first structure below the line, the first transfer, and
a measured span.** Three new things exist that did not at 30s. (1) A wandering,
faint, dotted trail leaving the last inked block and drifting off with no
destination. (2) Beside it a ruled, straight, solidly inked line that starts at a
drawn stop-mark — and on that line, hardened dots, each one a clump of ticks that
visibly left the band and landed. (3) Two brackets ruled along the bottom edge of
the field: a short one nested inside a long one. The upper band is measurably
thinner than at 30s — the first material has been spent. One already-placed dot
has greyed out, on its own beat, because nothing touched it.

**By ~90s — gained: a junction and three lanes of genuinely different geometry.**
The ruled line reaches mid-field and forks. Three lanes now occupy territory that
was blank at 60s, and no two are the same shape: one climbs in place inside the
same corridor, getting taller and thicker without leaving it; one crosses the
field's ruled gutter into the neighbouring column; one turns back and re-draws the
block it came from with different internal divisions. The upper band is down to
roughly half its 30s density. Net: the field is now busier than the band for the
first time in the video — the crossover point.

**By ~120s — gained: a scoring row, a thickening run, and near-exhaustion of the
band.** Three small gauge marks are ruled under the junction. Along one lane a run
of steps has begun, each drawn slightly larger and darker than the one before, so
the lane visibly thickens toward the far bracket. The other two lanes stay fully
surveyed at equal ink weight — nothing is crossed out or greyed as a loser. The
upper band is down to stragglers.

**By close (~145s) — gained: emptiness above, completion below.** The upper band
holds nothing. Every mark that was noise is now a drawn element. The field reads
as one continuous route: stacked blocks → stop-mark → ruled line → junction →
one thickening lane → the near flag → the long bracket. The last mark drawn is a
hand-ruled box around the near flag, the only element on the sheet with a visibly
hand-drawn edge.

## The carrying object

**A single sheet of gridded drafting paper filling the frame, with a band of
loose graphite marks hanging above its top edge — the sheet fills with ink at
exactly the rate the band empties.**

It is drawn as flat-on architecture, not perspective: a pale sheet with a faint
square grid occupying the lower ~72% of the canvas, its top edge a hard ruled
line, and above that line a band of small tapered ticks at varying angles on the
darker surround. On-sheet elements are all ruler-drawn primitives — filled
rectangles for the experience blocks, a dotted polyline for drift, a solid stroked
polyline for the momentum line, filled circles for placed steps, a thin arc for
each lane fork, short vertical tick-and-square marks for flags, and square-bracket
rules along the bottom edge for spans. Only two textures exist and they never
mix: **loose** (tapered, angled, unaligned, above the line) and **ruled** (aligned
to the grid, below the line), so a mark's register is readable at thumbnail size.

## Four frames

**25% — ~36s.** Top-heavy and lopsided. The upper third is a solid weather of
graphite ticks, visibly clustered into a low-hanging group and an upward-streaming
group. Below the ruled line: an almost empty grid with three filled blocks
stacked at the far left and a great deal of untouched paper to their right. A
viewer reads: enormous unsorted supply, almost nothing committed.

**50% — ~73s.** Two registers now argue. The band has thinned and its two clusters
are distinct enough to read separately. The sheet carries two lines leaving the
last block — one dotted and wandering off the field's edge, one ruled and straight
from a stop-mark, carrying a short row of hardened dots, one of them greyed. Two
nested brackets rule the bottom edge. Nothing has forked yet. A viewer reads:
material is moving down, and paper now has a direction.

**75% — ~109s.** Branched and right-weighted. The junction sits mid-sheet with
three lanes fanning into the right half, each a different drawn shape — a
climbing stack, a lane crossing the gutter, a lane looping back onto its own
block. The band above is sparse and patchy. The picture's centre of mass has moved
from the top band to the sheet's right side. A viewer reads: the supply became
structure, and the structure has options.

**100% — ~145s.** Resolved and single-register. The band is empty; the top of the
frame is quiet dark surround. The sheet is full and reads left to right as one
route: three blocks, stop-mark, ruled line, junction, three surveyed lanes with
one carrying a run of steps that grow heavier as they go, a near flag inside the
short bracket, the long bracket running past it, and a hand-ruled box closing
around the near flag. A viewer reads: everything that was overhead is now on
paper, and it is one thing.

**On-frame copy is the script's own language, nothing added.** Block labels:
"Projects Delivered" / "Teams Supported" / "Stakeholders Managed". Band cluster
labels: "Quiet Frustration" / "Growing Ambition". Line labels: "Drift" /
"Momentum". Lane labels: "Promotion" / "Lateral Move" / "Role Redesign". Gauge
labels: "Values" / "Energy" / "Life Constraints". Span labels: "Ninety-Day Goal"
(short bracket) / "Six To Twelve Months" (long bracket). No counts appear on
frame except the three paths and the two feelings, both of which the narration
states. **The near flag carries no invented move name** — the script says the
viewer names it, so the flag's label field is a ruled blank the hand-drawn box
closes around.

## The payoff beat

**The final beat of the closing paragraph — "one small, consistent, and
compounding step at a time" — where the hand-ruled box closes around the near
flag and the upper band goes empty on the same beat.**

That single beat completes the object twice over. It completes the *drawing*: the
box is the last stroke, and it is the only stroke on the sheet that is not ruled
to the grid, so the finished route reads as authored by a person rather than
printed. And it completes the *budget*: the band emptying at the same moment
makes the conservation rule visible in retrospect — the sheet did not gain new
material from nowhere, it absorbed the noise. The whole sheet is legible in one
look at this beat and at no earlier beat, because the route only becomes
continuous once the near flag is enclosed.

**It could not have been drawn at 0:10.** At 0:10 the sheet holds three blocks
and the band holds a handful of ticks; the closing frame requires the junction,
three lanes, a compounding run and an exhausted band to already exist behind it.

## Why this is not lazy

A builder cannot satisfy this concept by swapping headings against a static prop,
because five things are load-bearing and all five are checkable from the contact
sheet alone:

1. **The budget is a two-number state that must move every beat.** Band density
   and sheet ink are inversely coupled. A beat that leaves both unchanged is
   visibly wrong at thumbnail size, and a heading swap changes neither. This is
   the milestone contract: at 30% / 60% / 90% of runtime the band must be
   *measurably* emptier and the sheet *measurably* denser than the previous mark.
2. **The sheet is strictly cumulative and never erased.** Beat N's picture
   contains every element of beat N−1 plus at least one newly drawn element. Two
   beats therefore cannot share a picture, and the shuffle test resolves in one
   direction only.
3. **The three lanes are three different geometries, not one component times
   three.** Climb-in-place, cross-the-gutter, loop-back-and-redivide. A builder
   who instantiates one card thrice has not built this concept — the lanes would
   be distinguishable only by their labels, which is precisely the failure the
   thin-carrier rejection was.
4. **The compounding run has to actually compound.** Each step is drawn larger and
   heavier than its predecessor; a row of identical dots reads as a bullet list and
   defeats the closing argument. The lane's stroke weight at the far bracket must
   be visibly greater than at the junction.
5. **The final stroke depends on everything before it.** A hand-ruled box around a
   flag is meaningless without the flag, the flag is meaningless without the lane,
   the lane without the junction, the junction without the line. The payoff cannot
   be faked forward.

**Motion discipline, so this does not become churn.** The band is *not* a
perpetual drift animation — it is pixel-static within every beat and re-composed
only at beat boundaries as marks depart. Marks land once and never re-animate.
The single greying dot is a one-time, beat-scoped state change (declared inline if
the motion gate reads it as a re-mark), not a loop. Richness here comes entirely
from the object earning more, never from the picture swapping faster — the frame
at the close is dense because 145 seconds of material has landed on it.

**Fidelity guard.** Every element traces to a narration phrase: accumulated
experience → the three blocks; two feelings → two clusters; noise vs paper → the
two registers; drift as default → the dotted wandering trail; pause, reassess,
choose → the stop-mark; decay when untouched → the greyed dot; six-to-twelve
months and one ninety-day goal → the nested brackets; three paths → three lanes;
values, energy, life constraints → three gauges; none is better in general → all
three lanes keep equal ink weight and none is crossed out; small consistent
compounding steps → the thickening run; a named move you can build toward → the
blank ruled label the hand-drawn box closes around.
