# CONCEPT — m2_mid-career-mindsets-and-limiting-beliefs

Judged against the two APPROVED rows in `design-system/docs/taste.md` only
(m1 mini-syllabus track; build-direction 48-mark field). The 2026-08-05
`career-building-is-a-repeatable-process` cut is not a reference and nothing
here is anchored on it.

Runtime estimate: ~232 narration words → **~105s**. All marks below use 105s;
a builder rescales proportionally.

## Concept angle

**A mature tree kept in a nursery pot is unpotted to show roots it already
had, bound with three small trial limbs, and finally trained onto four
labelled wires** — the drawing is on frame from beat 2 through the final beat,
roughly 95% of runtime, in a fixed frame that never zooms or pans.

## The carrying object

One tree, drawn flat and geometric — no texture, no gradient, no foliage
detail, no perspective. The trunk is a single thick tapering stroke; five
branches are shorter tapering strokes off it; there are no leaves at any
point. A horizon rule sets ground level. Below it, roots are drawn as three
thick strokes fanning wider than the canopy. The nursery pot is a plain
trapezoid. Belief tags are small rounded rectangles tied to the trunk by a
short hooked stroke. A graft is a short **dashed** limb bound to an existing
branch by two crossing strokes. Training wires are thin horizontal rules
running the full frame width with their label set in the margin.

Weights carry the whole argument and must stay separable: **solid stroke =
already built**, **dashed = proposed**, **faint ghost = drawn and withdrawn**.
Brand token roles only — ink for anything built, the muted tint for anything
proposed, and the accent used exactly twice in the entire runtime (the strike
through the withdrawn pivot, and the final bud).

Two build constraints that are part of the concept, not decoration:

- **All lettering is horizontal and at or above the body floor.** Nothing is
  set along a curve, a branch, or a rotated edge. Labels sit beside the thing
  they name.
- **The frame is fixed.** The drawing grows outward into the page; the camera
  never moves. Settled ink is never re-animated once it has entered.
- **Every word on the drawing is a word the script says.** No numbers, no
  dates, no counts beyond the script's own enumerations.

## Milestone frames

The builder's ACCUMULATION CONTRACT. Four genuinely different pictures; none
is reachable from another by swapping text.

**~25% (~26s) — "Grown, and potted."**
A full-grown tree in outline weight, occupying only the middle third of the
frame. Its canopy is visibly clipped flat where it meets a limit that is not
drawn. It stands in a small trapezoid pot on a horizon rule that runs no wider
than the pot itself. Three tags are wired to the trunk, stacked, each lettered
with one belief the script quotes verbatim: *It's too late to change* / *If I
speak up, I'll seem ungrateful* / *If I don't grab the first opportunity, I'll
miss my chance*. Nothing on the page is filled. This is the only milestone
where empty space dominates.

**~50% (~53s) — "The roots were always there."**
The pot is cut away, its outline left as a faint ghost. The horizon rule now
runs edge to edge, and beneath it **three roots are drawn solid and thick,
spreading wider than the canopy**, labelled skills, credibility and
relationships. Above ground, nothing has been added — same trunk, same five
branches, same three tags — but the tags now hang slack on loosened wires.
The picture reads: the support was always larger than the container.

**~75% (~79s) — "One big idea, withdrawn."**
Two line weights fight for the eye. Sketched across the whole upper frame and
struck through with the accent: **one enormous dashed outline of an entirely
different canopy** — the all-or-nothing pivot — reduced to a faint ghost the
page keeps. In its place, **three small dashed limbs are bound onto three
existing branches**, each tagged with one experiment the script names: a
leadership role on a project, a hybrid responsibilities arrangement, an
adjacent function tried with a small assignment. The solid tree has not grown;
it has acquired three provisional additions.

**~100% (~105s) — "Trained, and readable at once."**
**Four thin wires now run edge to edge**, labelled in the margin: projects,
boundaries, responsibilities, skills. The branches — including the three
grafted limbs, now inked solid — are trained along them, so the silhouette
runs from the left frame edge to the right instead of sitting in a central
column. The three belief tags have been **untied from the trunk and re-tied
one to each grafted limb**, thinned to dashed outline. One grafted limb
carries a single solid accent bud: the one new path. One original branch keeps
a small stuck marker. Both ghosts — the withdrawn pivot and the pot — are
still faintly on the page.

## What accumulates

1. **Ink below the line.** Roots go from absent at 25% to three solid labelled
   strokes at 50% and stay for the rest of the runtime.
2. **Provisional geometry above it.** Three dashed limbs attach to three
   *specific* named branches at 75% and are inked solid by 100% — a dependency
   on earlier ink, so no beat can be authored in isolation.
3. **Occupied width.** A central third at 25% → full-width ground at 50% →
   full-width canopy on four wires at 100%. The drawing's footprint is
   monotonic, which is what makes the sheet orderable from pictures alone.
4. **History the page refuses to discard.** The ghost pot and the struck pivot
   are never cleaned up; the three beliefs are never deleted, only de-weighted
   and re-sited.

Nothing here is a highlight travelling around a fixed drawing — the failure
that sank the thin cut.

## The payoff beat

**The training wires arrive and the canopy is laid onto them**, on the closing
turn — "acknowledge where you feel stuck, and commit to exploring one new path
rather than staying in neutral." For the first and only time the whole drawing
is read at once: wide roots, four labelled wires, three grafts now solid, the
beliefs hanging demoted off the very limbs that answered them, one accent bud,
one stuck marker, and the two closing questions beneath. This frame is
undrawable at 0:10 — at 0:10 the same tree is in a pot with its canopy clipped
and every one of those elements is absent.

## Grafted from the runner-up

**Pitch A's plank remount: the beliefs are kept and re-purposed, never
erased.** In A, the three lettered planks come off the pegboard, lean against
the bench legs through the middle stretch, and return at the close mounted
horizontally as the shelf that carries the finished work. That single move is
the sharpest idea in either pitch, because it is the script's own claim —
these thoughts *feel protective* — made structural instead of stated. It
enters here as the tags' journey: wired tight to the trunk at 25%, slack at
50%, and at 100% untied and re-tied one to each grafted limb, thinned. B's
draft only thinned its walls in place; A's version gives the beliefs somewhere
to go.

## Batch-diversity note

The stronger pitch (B) carried an architect's floor plan — hatched **walls** on
a ruled **sheet**, a near-neighbour of three committed carriers at once
(task-bricks re-laid into a doorway, a foundation wall laid course by course,
and the paper worksheet ruled into zones, already used twice). Its accumulation
ladder is kept intact and re-cast onto a tree, which touches none of the nine:
no masonry or brick, no doorway, no skyline or wave, no paper sheet gaining
rules, no pegs and no thread routed between them, no field of scraps re-sorted
into columns, no stacked cairn or trail marker, no end-to-end track with a
checkpoint node, no bench and no tool rack. The one adjacency worth naming is
the four training wires against the committed peg-and-thread board: they are
four plain horizontal rules that arrive only in the final quarter as the
payoff, not a grid of pegs with thread routed between them, and nothing is
ever threaded, pinned or connected point-to-point on them.

## Judge's note

**The failure mode this will slide into: the middle goes static and the tree
sits small and central, gaining labels instead of ink** — precisely the thin
cut, whose carrier never grew and whose sheet cannot be re-ordered from its
pictures. The tempting shortcut is to draw the root reveal once at 50% and
then run headings over a fixed tree to the close.

The guard is the milestone contract read as a footprint test, and it should be
applied at the builder's own contact-sheet self-review: **any two stills a
quarter apart must differ in the drawing's silhouette, not only in its text.**
Concretely — the 50% still must contain solid labelled roots and a full-width
horizon that the 25% still does not; the 75% still must contain both the struck
ghost canopy and three dashed limbs, neither of which exists at 50%; the 100%
still must show four full-width wires and a canopy that reaches both frame
edges, which nothing before it does. A still that fails its quarter's addition
is re-authored, not re-captioned.

Second-order trap, worth stating because a tree invites it: do not answer any
flatness with movement. No sway, drift, rustle or pulse on settled ink — the
rejected *slow* cut had the most inter-beat change of the four references.
Richness here is the object earning more, never the picture moving more.

## Scores

**Pitch A — the boarded-up workbench.** Names a genuinely concrete object and
passes the lazy-build test convincingly: its milestones are geometry moves
(planks off the nails, tools outline→fill, vise takes and keeps a piece, bench
top zoned), so heading swaps could not fake them. Its payoff — the planks
returning as the shelf — is the single best idea in the competition and is
grafted above. It loses on two counts. Its 75% frame drops the composition to
the bench alone, so the accumulation built in the first half leaves the frame
at exactly the point the viewer should be able to see it compounding; the
approved m1 cut never once changes what is on screen, it only ever adds to it.
And its carrier is *a workbench with tool racks* — a committed batch carrier
verbatim — so it could not have run as pitched regardless of score.

**Pitch B — the architect's floor plan.** Wins on the two questions that
separate the approved cuts from the thin one. Its four frames are four
different pictures at a constant framing, and its accumulation is a dependency
graph rather than a sequence: each annex hangs off a doorway cut into a
particular wall and points at a particular existing structure, so no beat is
authorable alone — the same property that lets the m1 track's brackets, ticks
and endpoint read as elapsed time. It also keeps its own history (the erased
pivot's ghost, the original room surviving as a core), which is the shuffle
test passed by construction. Two defects were repaired in this document: its
lettering ran *along* the walls, which the fit gate and the 40px floor would
have fought, and its payoff called for a camera pull-back, which would move
settled ink; the frame is now fixed and all lettering horizontal. Its carrier
sat on top of three committed batch carriers, so the accumulation ladder is
kept and the object re-cast.

**Verdict: B wins**, re-cast from floor plan to the potted-then-trained tree
per the batch-diversity constraint, with A's re-purposed beliefs grafted in.
