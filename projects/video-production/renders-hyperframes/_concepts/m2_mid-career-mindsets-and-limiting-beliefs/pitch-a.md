# PITCH A — m2_mid-career-mindsets-and-limiting-beliefs

**Lens:** metaphor-first — one drawable object that carries the whole lesson and visibly accumulates.

## The carrying object

**A boarded-up workbench** — one bench against a pegboard wall, its tool wall barred shut by three nailed planks, which are pried off during the lesson and re-fastened at the end as the bench's own shelf.

It is drawn as a flat side-on elevation, near full-bleed: the bench top is one long slab rectangle with a thin shadow lip beneath it, standing on two straight legs and one stretcher bar; a vise sits at the left end as two square jaws with a round handle. Behind and above it, the pegboard is a regular grid of small dots filling the upper half of the frame, with tool silhouettes (a wrench outline, a square, a plane) hung on it — outlined and dimmed at the start, filled solid once revealed. The three planks are long thin rounded-rect bars crossing the pegboard at a slight tilt, each carrying its belief lettered along its length; a pry bar is a single angled stroke with a hooked end. Everything is line-and-fill in the brand ink/accent/muted token roles — no photography, no texture, no gradients.

## Four frames

**~25% — "The barred wall."** The bench sits mostly in outline. Three tilted planks cross the pegboard, each lettered with one belief the script actually quotes: *It's too late to change* / *If I speak up, I'll seem ungrateful* / *If I don't grab the first opportunity, I'll miss my chance*. Behind them the tool silhouettes are visible but dim and cut by the planks. Bench top empty. The picture reads: everything you own is right there, and barred.

**~50% — "The wall opens."** The pry bar has been set under the first plank; the planks are lifted off their nails and now rest, still lettered, leaning against the bench legs at the frame's lower edge — kept, not deleted. The pegboard is unobstructed and three tools are now drawn solid and labelled with what the script names: skills, credibility, relationships. The picture reads: the same wall, now reachable. Nothing is on the bench top yet — the frame's weight has moved from the bars to the wall.

**~75% — "The vise."** The composition drops to the bench itself: the vise jaws are open and hold small offcuts one at a time — a leadership role on a project, a hybrid responsibilities arrangement, an adjacent function tried on a small assignment. Each is a short stub piece, deliberately a fraction of the length of the one long uncut board lying untouched at the back of the bench (the all-or-nothing pivot the script says we are *not* doing). The picture reads: small pieces in the clamp, big board still whole.

**~100% — "The bench, re-laid."** Full bench in view again. The three planks are back — now horizontal, mounted as a shelf rail above the bench, their belief lettering turned so the shelf reads as structure rather than barrier. The bench top is laid out in four working zones the script names — projects, boundaries, responsibilities, skills — with the finished offcuts set into them. The tool wall is full and solid. One vise jaw stands open and empty on a blank, unlettered piece: the viewer's own next experiment, matched to the closing questions.

## What accumulates

Three things gain, and none of them reset:

1. **The tool wall fills.** It starts as dim outlines behind bars and ends solid, labelled, and unobstructed — a monotonic gain that a single still can be ordered by.
2. **The vise collects finished pieces.** The three experiments enter one at a time and stay on the bench afterwards; by the close, the bench top holds work that did not exist at frame one.
3. **The planks change job without leaving.** They start as three tilted bars blocking the wall, spend the middle stretch stacked against the legs, and end mounted flat as the shelf that holds the finished pieces. The beliefs are not erased — they are re-used, which is the lesson's own claim that these thoughts *feel protective*.

The bench also gains its zoning: an empty slab at 25% becomes a four-zone surface at 100%. At no point does the concept rely on a highlight travelling around a fixed drawing.

## The payoff beat

**The plank remount, on the closing task** — the beat where the narration turns from teaching to "acknowledge where you feel stuck, and commit to exploring one new path." The three planks swing from tilted bars to one horizontal shelf rail and take the finished offcuts onto them, and for the first time the whole bench is legible in one look: full tool wall, four zones, the beliefs re-purposed as shelf, and one open vise on a blank piece under the two closing questions. This frame is undrawable at 0:10 — at 0:10 the same planks are across the wall and the bench top is bare.

## Why this is not lazy

The concept's milestones are **geometry changes, not copy changes**. A builder cannot satisfy it with heading swaps because each milestone moves a named element to a named new position: the planks must physically leave the pegboard and later remount horizontally; the pegboard tools must go from outline to fill; the vise must open, take a piece, and retain it; the bench top must go from bare slab to four labelled zones. Any two beats pulled from different quarters are structurally different pictures, so the shuffle test resolves — and a static bench with new text on top would visibly fail on the *first* milestone, when the planks are supposed to come off.

It also spends its whole canvas: the bench and pegboard fill the frame, so there is no small prop sitting beside empty space, and detail arrives at the object rather than at a swapped background.

**Fidelity note.** Every count and label traces to the narration: three planks = the three quoted beliefs; three tools = skills, credibility, relationships; three offcuts = the three experiments named; four zones = projects, boundaries, responsibilities, skills. Nothing is added, numbered, or ordered beyond what the script says.
