# PITCH B — m3_using-the-resume-builder-tool-pt2

Lens: **accumulation-first**. Runtime estimate ~4:30 (670 words at the pinned
voice), so the ladder below runs on six rungs rather than four, and the
four-frame test is taken at 1:07 / 2:15 / 3:22 / 4:30.

## The accumulation ladder

What the frame has physically GAINED at each mark. Nothing here is a new
object arriving; the room is established whole in the first ten seconds — a
low workbench, a wheel at the front, and a shelf behind it already holding
your existing plain, unmarked pieces. Everything after is that room gaining.

- **~0:30** — the frame has gained a **measure**. A flat rib is cut with four
  steps in it (responsibility, action, outcome, scope). It did not exist at
  0:10; from here it is in almost every frame.
- **~1:10** — the frame has gained **contact points and gaps**. The rib is
  held against one plain piece lifted off the shelf; one step touches, three
  stand off it with daylight underneath. The failure is now visible, not
  asserted.
- **~2:05** — the piece has gained **height and four bands**. The same clay,
  re-raised on the wheel against that same rib, now carries four distinct
  pressed registers with the example's own figures cut into them. Same
  material, far more form.
- **~2:55** — the frame has gained **trim and finish**. Curls of pared clay lie
  on the bench where a band claimed more than it could hold; the numbers on
  one band are smoothed back to the softer wording; a quiet matte surface is
  chosen over the loud gloss standing beside it. The rib now touches at all
  four steps.
- **~3:45** — the shelf has gained **population and an order**. It is crowded,
  and most pieces on it now carry four bands where at 0:10 none did. Hands
  push them into three groups, and a short front row is drawn to the shelf's
  front edge.
- **~4:30** — the frame has gained an **outward face**. The shelf is seen from
  the street side for the first time, lit, with one piece turned fully
  face-out.

The single strongest accumulator is countable by a viewer: **how many pieces
on that shelf carry four bands.** At 0:10, none. At 4:30, all but the one
kept plain on the end.

## The carrying object

**A kiln shelf of stoneware jars, re-thrown one at a time on the wheel in
front of it — plus the four-step rib that both shapes them and tests them.**

Drawn in flat brand vector: the jars as filled silhouettes with the four
bands as pressed horizontal registers, so a re-made piece is legible from
across the room and a plain one is unmistakably plain. The rib is a small
hard-edged tool with four notched steps — the same object mounted on the
wheel while a piece rises, then lifted off and held against the result, so
the thing that makes and the thing that checks are one object. Motion is
strictly earned: the wheel turns ONLY while a piece is being raised and is
drawn at rest in every other beat, because settled content never re-animates
in place.

## Four frames

**1 — 1:07 (25%), the lens beat.** Wide on the bench. The four-step rib is
finished and held upright against one squat plain jar taken off the shelf;
three steps stand clear with visible gaps. The shelf behind is half full of
plain pieces. Heading: *What The Lens Measures*.

**2 — 2:15 (50%), the accuracy beat.** Tight on the trimming bench, no shelf
in frame. The jar is now tall and four-banded, its bands cut with the
example's own words and figures. A curl of clay falls from the frequency band
where the claim ran past what happened; the percentage band's number is being
smoothed to the softer phrasing. The rib, held against it, touches at all
four steps. The wheel sits empty and still behind.

**3 — 3:22 (75%), the three paths.** Back on the shelf, now crowded and mostly
four-banded, pushed into three labelled groups — a promotion in your lane, a
lateral move, a redesign of your role. Our jar is findable by its mark. A
short front row (four pieces, inside the script's own three-to-five range) is
pulled to the shelf's front edge.

**4 — 4:30 (100%), the headline.** Exterior. The workshop window at night, the
shelf read from the outside, light falling onto the street. The front row is
against the glass; one piece is turned fully face-out with its outward face
freshly cut, and the version not chosen stands set back in shadow. The
original plain jar is still on the far end of the shelf, untouched.

Four different rooms, four different distances, one continuous object.

## The payoff beat

Frame 4, on "that final judgment call is yours." Two things resolve at once:
the shelf is re-read from the *other side* — the side other people stand on —
and the plain jar left on the end makes the whole runtime measurable in a
single picture. This frame is undrawable at 0:10: at 0:10 there is no rib, no
band on anything, no sorted groups, no lit face. The last gesture in the video
is a hand choosing between two faces, not a machine finishing.

## Why this is not lazy

A builder cannot satisfy this with heading swaps against a static prop. The
contract is a **count** — banded pieces on the shelf must go 0 → 1 → many →
all-but-one — plus a rib whose gaps must visibly close between frames 1 and 2,
plus a sort that must actually re-position pieces in frame 3, plus a camera
that leaves the shelf entirely for frame 2 and steps outside the building for
frame 4. Each of those is checkable on a contact sheet, and the sheet's order
is restorable from the pictures alone.

It also stays inside the script. No screen, no browser chrome, no invented
tool feature: uploading is a piece carried from shelf to wheel, the rewrite is
the wheel raising it against the four-step rib, the checks are trim and
finish, the selection is a front row, and the public headline is the one face
turned out to the street. Every number on frame is one the narration speaks,
and the part-number suffix reaches neither frame nor narration.

Against the batch: this is not a wall, a sheet, a board, a strip, a track, a
plant, a span, a canvas, or a core. The nearest neighbours are the display
concepts, and the difference is that nothing here is merely stored — each item
on this shelf is individually taken down, re-made, and put back, which is what
makes the shelf a record of the work rather than a rack.
