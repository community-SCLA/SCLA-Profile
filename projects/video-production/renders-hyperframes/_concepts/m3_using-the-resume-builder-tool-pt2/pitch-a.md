# Pitch A — metaphor-first

## The carrying object

**A key blank held in a bench vise, cut step by step into a working key.**

Drawn flat in profile, no perspective: the bench is one heavy horizontal rule
across the lower third, the vise is two thick blocks and a screw handle, and
the key itself is drawn large — a rounded-rectangle bow, a long straight
shank, and a blade whose **top edge is the only thing in the frame that ever
changes shape**. Four thin vertical guide lines drop from a gauge rail onto
that blade and divide it into four labelled stations; the depth of each step
is the whole picture. Cutting tools (a file, a depth gauge, the bench
machine's head) are short angled strokes that appear at the point of contact
and leave once the cut is made — nothing hovers, nothing idles.

## Four frames

**25% — the blank and the gauge.** The key is clamped in the vise, blade edge
still perfectly smooth and unbitten. Above it the gauge rail carries four
stations dropped onto the blade as thin verticals, each named by the
narration's own four questions: responsibility, action, measurable outcome,
scope. A smooth blade is a blade that turns nothing — the picture is a shape
that has not been asked the four questions yet.

**50% — the blade is bitten, and one cut is wrong.** The bench machine's head
has passed over the blade and lifted away; the top edge is now a stepped
silhouette, four deep cuts where four flat stations were. At one station a
depth gauge sits in the notch and a short file stroke brings that one step
back up to a shallower, true depth — a single visible correction, the rest of
the profile untouched. Second read of the same frame: the tone of the blade's
finish is redrawn from a hard machined edge to a smoother, quieter one.

**75% — the bench, not the key.** The vise is empty. Across the bench sit
three lock cylinders in a row, one for each path the narration names —
promotion in the current lane, a lateral move, a redesign of the current
role — and beside them a split ring that has gained a small handful of
finished keys, each carrying its own stepped profile. One key is lifted off
the ring and held toward the cylinder that matches it. Wholly different
picture: plural objects, bench-wide, the single key no longer the subject.

**100% — the tag, and the bench read at once.** The ring hangs on a hook with
an engraved tag turned outward, its lettering the largest thing in the frame
— the line that has to be read from a distance by someone who is looking for
you. Two candidate tags were drawn; one is hung and the other set down. Under
it, the whole bench is visible in one sweep: the original smooth blank still
sitting at the left where it started, the corrected key, the ring at the
right. The frame is a before-and-after inside one picture.

## What accumulates

Three layers, in order, and nothing is ever swapped out to make room:

1. **Steps on one blade** — a smooth edge becomes a four-step profile, then
   one step is filed back to a truer depth. The blade never returns to smooth.
2. **Keys on a ring** — the first finished key is joined by the others as the
   narration talks about bringing bullets forward. No count ever appears on
   frame; the narration owns the number.
3. **Lettering on the tag** — the last thing to arrive, and the only element
   drawn to be read at a distance rather than inspected up close.

The earlier layers stay on the bench in their finished state, so the closing
frame contains every stage of the lesson simultaneously. The uncut blank at
0:10 is still there at the end, and it is the reason the end lands.

## The payoff beat

The final beat: the tag is chosen, hung, and lettered, and the camera pulls to
the full bench so the smooth blank, the corrected key, the loaded ring and the
readable tag are all in one frame. That picture is physically undrawable at
25% — there is one object on the bench then, and it has no cuts in it. It is
also the beat where the object stops being a thing you make and becomes a
thing that is read by someone else, which is exactly the turn the narration
takes at the end.

## Why this is not lazy

A heading swap against a static prop cannot produce this. Every frame requires
a *different silhouette* of the same drawn object: the blade profile is
authored geometry, not a caption, so beat 2 cannot be beat 1 with new words on
top. The correction beat is the tell — filing one step back to a shallower
depth is a specific, visible, single-element change that no generic template
offers, and it is the honest reading of the narration's "check accuracy, soften
what you cannot support." And the 75% frame changes the bench's *population*,
not its labels: one key becomes several, plus three cylinders that were not
there before. A builder trying to cheat this concept would have to draw the
real thing anyway.

Fidelity notes: the three lock cylinders exist because the narration names
three paths — no fourth is invented. The keys on the ring are never counted on
frame. The tool is drawn as a bench machine, never as a screen, so no interface
feature is depicted that the script does not describe. No part number appears
anywhere on frame or in narration.
