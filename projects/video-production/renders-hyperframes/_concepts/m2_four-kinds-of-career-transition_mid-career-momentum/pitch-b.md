# Pitch B — accumulation-first lens

**Lesson:** `m2_four-kinds-of-career-transition_mid-career-momentum`
**Script length:** ~580 words ⇒ **~3:45 runtime** at the pinned voice. Marks below are
given as clock times AND as fractions so the builder can re-anchor them against the
real `timing.json` without re-designing anything.

**Method note:** this pitch was designed accumulation-first — the ladder below was
written before the object was named. The object is whatever could plausibly do that
gaining.

---

## The accumulation ladder

What the frame has physically GAINED at each mark. Nothing here is a heading swap;
every row is more material, denser material, or material re-sorted.

| Mark | ~% | The frame has GAINED | Script it reads |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Ground under it stays whole. One of four outlines is now occupied. | "The Pivot… still builds on your past… your experience carries forward… reframe the ones you already have." |

Two things about the shape of this ladder. First, density is **monotone** — nothing
is ever cleared or reset between the four builds; each wall stays standing while the
next goes up, so a viewer can tell 2:10 from 2:45 from 3:10 by mass alone. Second,
the last two rungs add **no mass at all** — they re-sort what is already there,
which is where the payoff lives.

## The carrying object

**A build site: one standing wall of blocks (what you've built so far) and four
staked plots beside it, where that same stock gets re-laid — each plot mixing
carried blocks with newly quarried ones until all four stand at the same height.**

Drawn in flat shapes, no skeuomorphism: blocks are small rounded rectangles in a
regular course grid; **solid fill = carried forward**, **outline-only = newly
quarried**; the ground is a single horizontal rule that is either continuous or
carries a jagged break; a transfer span is a thin measured run from the standing
wall to a plot, its length the only thing that varies; bracing is two diagonal
strokes. Nothing on screen ever numbers the blocks, and no count is spoken or
labelled — the reading is mass and proportion, never arithmetic. The standing
original wall is **never depleted**: a transfer traces a band of blocks along the
span while the source stays whole, because the script says experience *carries
forward*, not that it is spent. Once a block lands it is pixel-static — the site
grows, it never idles (`check_motion.py`).

---

## Four frames

**25% — ~0:56.** One dense solid wall standing on the left third. To its right, one
long ground line with four dashed empty rectangles staked along it. Above the ground,
two small drawn measures: a short run and a long run. One of the four dashed
footprints sits on a ground line that already carries a fracture. Right two-thirds
is open ground — structured, staked, and completely unbuilt.

**50% — ~1:52.** The standing wall, plus plot one finished (short span, thick solid
band, thin outlined cap) and plot two mid-build (long span drawn, slim solid base
landed, outlined courses climbing). Two footprints still dashed and empty. The eye
can already compare two finished recipes side by side; grounds all still whole
except the staked fracture.

**75% — ~2:48.** Four occupied or occupying plots. Three complete walls with three
visibly different solid/outline recipes, plot three sitting on visibly broken
ground, and plot four's fractured ground already staked with its long span drawn
and its first props set. Busiest, most uneven frame of the video — four walls at
four different heights mid-build. No level line yet.

**100% — ~3:45.** The full site: four completed walls under a single level rule at
equal height, two whole grounds and two fractured, plot four still braced, three
surveyor's stakes in the foreground, one solid marker set on the open ground in
front, committed to nothing. This frame could not have been drawn at 0:10 — at 0:10
the frame is a single line.

## The payoff beat

**~3:10–3:25, on "None of these four is 'better' than the others."** The level line.
Every wall on the site has been going up at its own pace and stopping at what looked
like its own height; when one long rule draws across all four tops, they resolve to
the *same* height. In one stroke the accumulation re-reads: the four walls were never
four outcomes of different quality, they were four **recipes** — different amounts
carried, different amounts newly made, different ground underneath — for the same
standing height. The two questions get their re-read in the same beat without any
new drawing: "did you choose it" is already visible as whole vs fractured ground,
and "how big is the leap" is already visible as the span lengths, so the closing
question pair points back at marks the viewer watched arrive.

A second, quieter re-read follows at the very end: the unplaced marker. The site is
complete and the viewer's own position is the one thing still missing from it —
which is exactly what the script's last line asks them to supply.

## Why this is not lazy

**A static prop cannot produce these frames.** The 25% frame has four *empty* dashed
footprints; the 75% frame has three finished walls of three different compositions
plus one broken ground; the 100% frame has four walls under a level line. There is
no single prop that reads correctly at all three with the headings changed — the
carried band, the outlined band, the span length, and the ground state each differ
per plot and each is a drawn quantity, not a label. A builder who swaps headings
produces four identical walls, and the 3:10 level-line payoff then says nothing,
because equal height is only meaningful once the four recipes visibly differ.

**Checkable milestone contract for `CONCEPT.md`.** Occupancy is monotone (1 → 2 → 3
→ 4 walls, nothing ever cleared); fractured grounds go 0 → 0 → 1 → 2; solid-to-
outline proportion is high, low, high, low across plots one to four; span length is
short, long, short, long; bracing appears on plot four only; the level rule exists
only after all four walls do. Any of those failing is visible on a contact sheet
without reading a word of copy — which is the shuffle test passing by construction.

**Why this is a different picture, not a recolour of the approved map cut.** The
approved `m1` reference carried "four positions on ONE map, built once, never left" —
its subject is **position in empty space**: two perpendicular axes, four quadrants,
the object complete the moment it is drawn, and the runtime spent re-reading it. This
object has no axes, no quadrants and no coordinates. Its subject is **material and
ground** — what transfers, what has to be newly made, and whether the ground under
you held. The four plots sit in a row on one horizontal line in the script's own
spoken order, and the two questions appear as *properties of a built thing* (span
length, fracture) rather than as directions to travel. The time-shape is inverted
too: the map is dense at 20% and re-read thereafter, whereas this site is deliberately
**empty at 25%** and only reaches full density at ~84%, because the lens here is
accumulation. And it ends on a claim the map cannot make — a map says "four
positions"; a level line across four differently-built walls says "four recipes, one
standing height," which is the script's "none of these four is better" drawn rather
than captioned. It is not the 48-mark field cut either: that field only ever
re-*groups* fixed marks, while here material physically moves and new material is
quarried.

**Fidelity check — every visual property traces to narration.** Solid vs outlined
proportion ← "builds on your past" / "your experience carries forward" vs "genuinely
new". Span length ← "adjacent direction" vs "reinventing into something new" ("how
big is the leap"). Fracture ← "a change you did not choose" / "you were pushed out".
Bracing ← "extra care, extra support". Equal height ← "none of these four is
'better'". Order ← the script's own first / second / third / fourth. No counts, no
percentages, no numbered blocks, no timeline, and no claim about which transition the
viewer is in — the closing marker stays unplaced because the script never places it.

**Pace headroom.** The object supplies ~37 discrete, genuinely different gains across
~3:45 (5 opening + 5 ground-and-stakes + 5 per plot + 7 closing) — roughly 10
beats/min without splitting a single beat into a twin, since every beat lands new
material or a new drawn measure.
