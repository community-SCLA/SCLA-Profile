# PITCH A — m2_four-kinds-of-career-transition_mid-career-momentum

Lens: metaphor-first.

## The carrying object

**One long workbench holding four open packs, loaded from a single supply rack that drains overhead as they fill.**

The bench is a full-width horizontal slab across the lower third; above it hangs a three-bin supply rack whose stock is drawable and countable-by-eye: flat **skill plates** (thin stacked bars), dark **ballast weights** (solid rounded blocks), and **probes** (thin stems with small flags). Each pack is a wide trapezoid with a handle bar, a cutaway window so its load line is legible from outside, and a front pocket. Two physical marks — never axes, never coordinates — say which kind it is: a **tag** on the handle (hand-tied loop tag = you chose it; flat plate riveted to the shoulder, no slack = it chose you) and a **feed line**, a rail running from the pack back to the worn, already-full pack at the far left that opens the video (your delivered projects, supported teams, managed stakeholders). The feed line is intact, deliberately unhooked and coiled, or snapped and capped.

## Four frames

- **25% — one pack, nothing new added.** The rack is full. The worn pack sits at the left, plates packed tight. The first empty pack stands open beside it with a hand-tied tag; its feed line runs back intact and taut. Nothing comes down from the rack: instead the plates already inside the worn pack are being flipped face-out along the line and set into the pack in their new orientation — reframing what is already there. Three ghost pack outlines wait further down the bench, unopened.
- **50% — the rack starts to drain.** Pack one is closed and standing, its load line low. Pack two is open under the rack taking its first real delivery: a tall column of new skill plates plus a handful of probes, load line already above pack one's. Its tag is hand-tied; its feed line is unhooked and coiled neatly on the bench beside it, by hand. Two ghost outlines left. The rack's plate bin has a visible notch out of it.
- **75% — weight, not height.** Three packs stand. Pack three carries a riveted tag, an intact feed line, and one ballast weight set at the very bottom of its cutaway with a modest load resting on top — the trip is short, the thing underneath it is heavy. Pack four is open and taking the largest delivery of the run: plates, probes and ballast together, a second carry strap being clipped to its side for another pair of hands. Its tag is riveted; its feed line lies snapped and capped. The rack is nearly bare, its bins showing floor.
- **100% — the row reads itself.** The rack is empty and pushed back. Four closed packs stand in one level row — no pedestal, no pack raised above another — each with its cutaway load, its tag and its feed line legible at a glance. One pack has stepped forward off the row with three slips in its front pocket: a reflection slip, an evidence slip from the AI activity, and the Next Move Statement.

## What accumulates

Mass moves top-to-bottom across the runtime and never returns. The rack starts full and ends bare; the four packs start as outlines and end as four distinct, closed, differently-loaded objects standing on a level bench. Each pack's load line, ballast placement, tag type and feed-line state is set once and then held, so by the end the bench carries four completed load profiles that did not exist at 0:10 — and the contact sheet can be re-sorted from the rack's remaining stock alone. Nothing is highlighted; things are carried.

## The payoff beat

The closing row. Four packs, level, side by side — and with the headings covered you can still name every one of them from its two physical marks: tag type says whether you chose it, feed line says how far the leap is. The object that spent the whole runtime being loaded one pack at a time becomes, in the last beat, a single readable row — and then the viewer's own slips go into exactly one front pocket, which is why the closing frame could not have been drawn at the start.

## Why this is not lazy

A heading swap against a static bench fails immediately on four independent counts. The rack's stock must decrease monotonically, so every milestone frame pins a different rack state that a static prop cannot produce. The first pack is loaded by a **different verb** from the other three — existing plates flipped and re-seated, nothing delivered — so a builder reusing one load animation is visibly wrong. Ballast placement is positional, not decorative: pack three's weight sits at the bottom under a light load, pack four's sits inside the heaviest load, and the two must not look alike. And the payoff requires four cutaway loads legible **simultaneously** in one frame, which no per-beat heading swap can assemble. Motion stays legal: only the currently loading pack and the draining bin move; a closed pack is pixel-static for the rest of the run.

**Why this is not the approved map, recoloured.** A map answers *where am I standing*; the two questions become axes and the four kinds become positions. This object never draws a coordinate plane — position on the bench is just narration order, and the four kinds are told apart by **what is inside them and what they are still tied to**. That is the answer to the script's own payoff line: knowing which one you are in tells you what to plan for — how much identity work, how much grief, how much experimenting. A bench of packed loads is a picture of what you carry, not of where you sit.
