# CONCEPT — m2_four-kinds-of-career-transition_mid-career-momentum

Judged against the two APPROVED rows of `design-system/docs/taste.md` only.
The 2026-08-05 `career-building-is-a-repeatable-process` cut is
SHIPPED-NOT-A-REFERENCE and was not used to anchor anything below.

---

## Concept angle

**The graft row** — one mature standing stock on the left and four beds staked
along a single ground rail, where cuttings taken from that stock are planted and
grown into four visibly different forms that finally resolve to one height; the
stock and the rail arrive in the opening beats and the row is on screen
continuously from **beat 2 through the final beat (~95% of runtime)**.

---

## The carrying object

A flat, two-tone growth diagram — no naturalism, no gradients, no ornament.
Everything is drawn from four primitives:

- **The ground rail.** One horizontal rule across the lower third, full frame
  width. It is either **continuous** or carries a **jagged breach** directly
  beneath a bed. That is the only thing it varies, and it is the answer to the
  script's first question: whole rail = you chose it; breached rail = it chose
  you. Nothing ever travels along the rail — it is ground, not a route.
- **The standing stock** (left fifth of the frame). A mature form drawn
  entirely in **solid** strokes: a thick trunk with **three limbs**, each limb
  carrying paired branch strokes ending in small flat **leaf-tabs** (rounded
  rectangles, never leaf shapes). The three limbs arrive one per phrase in the
  opening — projects delivered, teams supported, stakeholders managed. The stock
  is **never cut down and never loses a limb**: a graft takes a *cutting*, and
  the parent stays whole, because the script says experience carries forward.
- **A form in a bed.** Same vocabulary as the stock: a vertical stem stroke with
  paired branch strokes and leaf-tabs at regular intervals. Two fills and only
  two: **solid = wood carried across from the cutting**, **outline-only = new
  growth made on site**. The proportion of solid to outline across the whole
  silhouette is the form's **recipe**, and it is the single most legible signal
  on the frame at contact-sheet scale. Height is read off the topmost branch
  pair. Branch-tabs give each form real area, so it fills its column rather than
  hanging as a thin line in empty space.
- **The carry run.** A thin measured line from the stock to a bed, drawn once
  and then left on screen. Its **length** is the only thing that varies, and it
  is the second question: short run = adjacent, long run = genuinely new.

Two further marks, each used exactly once: **support ties** (two diagonal
strokes staked against form four only) and the **canopy rule** (one long
horizontal line drawn across the tops of all four forms, and only after all four
exist). Beds before planting are **dashed empty rectangles** at the rail.

Nothing is ever numbered, counted, or captioned with a quantity — the reading is
proportion and length, never arithmetic. Once a stroke has landed it is
pixel-static for the rest of the run; growth only ever ADDS a new stroke
(`check_motion.py`).

## Milestone frames — the builder's ACCUMULATION CONTRACT

**25% — structured ground, nothing planted.** The standing stock complete on the
left, all solid, three limbs. The ground rail runs the full width. Four **empty
dashed beds** staked along it to the right of the stock. Above the rail, the two
questions exist as four small sample marks: a **short run** and a **long run**, a
**whole rail stub** and a **breached rail stub**. Right two-thirds of the frame:
staked, measured, and completely unplanted.

**50% — two recipes, side by side.** Stock unchanged. **Bed one complete**:
short carry run, almost entirely solid carried wood, one thin outlined pair
capping it, rail beneath it whole. **Bed two mid-growth**: long carry run drawn,
a slim solid base at the bottom, outlined new growth climbing above it in
several separate passes, rail whole. Beds three and four still dashed and empty.
The eye can compare two finished recipes without reading a word.

**75% — the busiest, most uneven frame in the video.** Four beds occupied or
occupying. **Bed three complete**: the rail beneath it was **breached first,
before any wood arrived**; short carry run; mostly solid carried wood.
**Bed four just started**: rail breached, long carry run drawn, first thin
outlined shoots up, and its two **support ties** already staked. Three complete
forms at three visibly different apparent heights plus one under construction.
**No canopy rule yet.**

**100% — the row reads itself.** Four complete forms under a **single horizontal
canopy rule at equal height**. Two whole rail sections, two breached. Form four
still tied to its supports. Three small **stakes planted in the open
foreground**, deliberately in no bed. One solid **unplanted cutting** lying on
the open ground in front, committed to nothing. At 0:10 this frame is one rail
and a bare stock; it could not have been drawn there.

## What accumulates

Occupancy is monotone and nothing is ever cleared: **0 → 1 → 3 (+1 building) →
4** planted beds. Breaches in the rail go **0 → 0 → 1 → 2**. Carried-to-new
proportion runs **high, low, high, low** across forms one to four. Carry-run
length runs **short, long, short, long**. Support ties appear on form four only.
The canopy rule exists only after all four forms do. The standing stock accrues
its three limbs early and then never changes — it is the fixed reference the
whole row is measured against.

So the contact sheet can be re-sorted into time order from **planted-bed count
alone**, with every word of copy covered: the shuffle test passes by
construction. And the last two gains — the canopy rule and the unplanted cutting
— add **no new material at all**; they re-read what is already standing.

---

## The payoff beat

**The canopy rule, on "None of these four is 'better' than the others."** Every
form has been growing at its own rate and stopping at what looked like its own
height. One stroke draws across all four crowns and they resolve to the **same**
height. In that instant the whole runtime re-reads: these were never four
outcomes of different quality, they were four **recipes** — different amounts
carried, different amounts newly grown, different ground underneath — reaching
one standing height. Both questions get their re-read in the same beat with no
new drawing, because whole-vs-breached rail and short-vs-long run are already on
screen and were watched arriving.

A quieter second re-read closes it: the **unplanted cutting** in the foreground.
The row is complete and the viewer's own position is the one thing still missing
from it — which is exactly what the script's last line asks them to supply.

---

## Grafted from the runner-up

**The first bed is made by a different verb.** Pitch A's strongest single idea
was that its first pack is loaded by *re-orienting material already inside it*
rather than by taking any new delivery. Carried across: at bed one, the cutting
arrives along its short carry run with its leaf-tabs folded flat, and the beat's
action is the tabs **turning face-out as the piece seats** — same wood, new
orientation, nothing new delivered — with only one thin outlined pair capping it
afterwards.

Why: it is the script's own sentence drawn rather than captioned ("your job is
less to prove brand-new skills than to reframe the ones you already have"), and
it structurally forbids the laziest available build — reusing one growth routine
four times — because form one's arrival is a different motion from the other
three. The turn happens while the piece is entering, not after it has settled,
so it stays clear of the in-place re-animation ban.

## Batch-diversity note

Nothing here is laid, stacked, or coursed. The material is **grown**, and the
forms **branch** rather than stack — so this is not the task-bricks doorway, not
the course-by-course foundation wall, not the brick skyline traced into a wave.
There is no sheet, no filled form and no ruled zones, so it is not either
worksheet carrier. No pegs, no threads, no tensioned lines between points. No
field of scraps re-sorting into columns and no single statement strip. No marker
built on stacked experience — the one marker in this concept is deliberately
*unplanted* and stands on open ground.

Closest neighbour is the **career track with a checkpoint node** (the approved m1
reference), and the separation is deliberate: the ground rail is not a route,
carries no node, is never travelled or progressively filled left-to-right, and
its only variable is whether it is whole or breached beneath a bed. The four
kinds are told apart by **what each form is made of and what it stands on**, not
by position along a line. It is also not the map picture: no axes, no quadrants,
no coordinates — the two questions are properties of a built thing (run length,
breach), not directions to travel.

## Judge's note

**Most likely slide: four look-alike forms.** This is the thin cut's failure in
a new costume — the carrier is present the whole time, but a builder reuses one
growth routine in four beds, the recipes come out identical, and the canopy rule
then says nothing, because equal height only means something once the four
compositions visibly differ. Everything else in this concept survives a
mediocre build; that one does not.

**Guard:** the recipe contract above is checkable off the 75% and 100% contact
sheets with all copy covered — proportion high/low/high/low, run
short/long/short/long, breach 0/0/1/2, ties on form four only, and form one
built by the re-orientation verb rather than by delivery. If forms one and two
cannot be told apart with the headings hidden, that is a build defect and the
beats get re-authored; it is not a matter of taste. Secondary guard, cheap to
hold: the drawing vocabulary is two fills and flat strokes only, so there is
nothing decorative to sway — no landed stroke ever moves again.

## Scores

**Pitch A — the bench of four packs under a draining supply rack.** Genuinely
rich and genuinely not lazy: the rack's monotone drain is a second, independent
time key, the "different verb" for the first pack is the best single idea in
either pitch, and the closing level row is a real payoff. It loses on
**legibility of the discriminating marks**. The two marks that tell the four
kinds apart — a hand-tied loop tag versus a riveted plate, and a feed line that
is intact, coiled, or snapped — are small, fiddly, near-identical at
contact-sheet scale, and they sit on props rendered in a skeuomorphic vocabulary
(handles, straps, rivets, worn packs) that the approved references never use.
Its 25% and 50% frames are also the closest pair of the four in either pitch:
packs on a bench under a partly-full rack, twice. The most likely real build is
four labelled boxes in a row — a card comparison wearing a metaphor. **Strong
concept, fragile drawing.**

**Pitch B — the build site of one standing wall and four staked plots.** Wins
on every one of the five questions. The object is nameable and drawable; the
four frames are the most distinct pair-wise set of anything pitched (structured
empty ground → two recipes → four uneven builds → one level line); accumulation
is monotone in mass *and* re-sorted at the end without new mass; the payoff is a
single stroke that re-reads the entire runtime and pays off both questions for
free; and it ships its own anti-lazy contract in numbers a contact sheet can be
graded against. Its one disqualifying problem was **material**, not structure:
blocks, courses and walls collide head-on with three carriers already committed
in this batch. **Verdict: PITCH B WINS**, re-cast from masonry into the graft
row — the accumulation logic, the recipe contract, the level-line payoff and the
unplaced marker are kept intact, the material is changed from laid block to
grown wood, and Pitch A's different-verb opening is grafted onto bed one.
