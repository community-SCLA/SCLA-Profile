# Pitch B — m3_discover-experiences-that-support-your-next-move

**Lens:** accumulation-first. The ladder below was decided before the object was
named; the object was then chosen as the cheapest concrete thing that could
plausibly do that gaining.

**Runtime basis:** the script is ~306 narration words at the pinned Oxana 1.0
voice, plus inter-beat air and the 1.8s final hold — call it **~2:20 (140s)**.
Marks below are stated in seconds and as percentages so they survive the real
measured duration.

---

## The accumulation ladder

What the frame has physically GAINED at each mark. Nothing on this ladder is a
highlight moving; every item is a countable thing that is on screen at the mark
and was not on screen before it.

**By 0:30 (~21%) — the store exists, and it is untouched.**
Gained: a lamp at the left, lit; a rack of plain, unmarked glass discs, drawn
deliberately uncountable (the script never counts your past work, so neither do
we); a barrel standing at centre with its slots visibly EMPTY; a formless pale
smear on the wall at the right. Plus the first gauge ring clamped onto the
barrel's throat. Net: the whole inventory has arrived and not one piece has been
chosen. The empty slots are the picture.

**By 1:00 (~43%) — the throat is fully specified and the first load is seated.**
Gained: the second and third gauge rings (scope, then impact — the impact ring
carrying its four notches for improved outcomes, better processes, stronger
relationships, growth); and the first discs lifted out of the rack and seated in
the barrel. A disc is blank in the rack and gains its etched rim only as it
passes the rings — so selection is visible as marking, not as motion. The smear
on the wall has taken its first hard edge.

**By 1:30 (~64%) — the wall starts collecting.**
Gained: two pinned projection prints on the wall — the leadership/strategic load
and the lateral-move load, each with its own three discs drawn edge-on beneath
it. Both stay pinned, dimmed, for the rest of the runtime. The barrel is then
reloaded with the viewer's own set, within the script's own three to five. The
rack behind it is still full, and that is the point: you did not display every
piece, and you did not throw the rest away.

**By 2:00 (~86%) — each chosen disc gets heavier.**
Gained: a four-line collar grown beneath every seated disc — Responsibility,
then Action, then Measurable outcome, then Scope — engraved one line per beat as
each is defined. The beam through the barrel closes to unbroken and the patch on
the wall hardens from a shape into a legible figure. Nothing new is added to the
rack or the wall here; the same few discs simply become denser objects.

**Close, ~2:20 (100%) — the bench is seen whole for the first time.**
Gained: the wide view itself. Full rack, few chosen discs each fully collared,
two dim prints and one bright resolved patch, all in one frame.

---

## The carrying object

**A cutaway lantern bench: a lamp at one end, a slotted brass barrel at centre
that gains glass experience discs one at a time, and a wall patch at the far end
where the light lands.**

Drawn flat and side-on in SVG, left to right, as one continuous bench that never
cuts away: the lamp is a squat housing whose filament plate is the learner's Next
Move Statement (already in hand — it is the source, never the output); the rack
is a slotted tray of thin ellipses, blank; the barrel is a horizontal body with a
visible ladder of empty slots and a threaded throat where the gauge rings clamp;
the beam is a single tapered polygon from lamp to wall, redrawn only at the
instant a disc seats or a collar completes — never shimmering, never idling, so
`check_motion.py` has nothing to catch. Every state change is a discrete,
countable event: a disc leaves the rack, a ring clamps, a collar line engraves, a
print pins.

## Four frames

**25% — 0:35.** Left-heavy and lopsided. The rack dominates: rows of blank
discs. The barrel sits centre-frame with an obviously empty slot ladder and one
gauge ring on its throat. The wall at right is a pale, edgeless smear. Reads as:
everything you have ever done, and nothing chosen yet.

**50% — 1:10.** Centre-heavy. Three gauge rings on the throat, the impact ring
notched four ways. Three discs are seated and etched; the first print has just
pinned to the wall; the lateral set is mid-lift out of the rack, caught between
tray and barrel. Two different loads are visible in the same picture — one
projected, one in transit.

**75% — 1:45.** Right-heavy. Two dim prints hang pinned on the wall with their
edge-on stacks beneath. The barrel holds the viewer's own three-to-five, and the
collars beneath them are half engraved — the first two lines cut, the last two
still blank rules. The beam is bright but the wall patch is still soft.

**100% — 2:20.** Wide. The whole bench at once, at a scale used nowhere else in
the video: full rack still full, few discs each carrying a complete four-line
collar, two dim prints, one bright resolved patch. Four different centres of
gravity — rack, barrel, wall, whole.

## The payoff beat

The final pull-back. For 2:20 the frame has only ever shown a portion of the
bench; the closing beat shows all of it in one shot, and the sentence it lands
on is "you make every bullet work harder for you" — which is legible in the
picture because each disc that stayed is the one with the heaviest collar, and
the rack behind it is still full of the pieces that did not need to be displayed.
That frame cannot be drawn at 0:10: at 0:10 the barrel is empty, every disc is
blank, the wall is a smear, and there is no collar anywhere on the bench.

---

## Why this is not lazy

**It cannot be built with heading swaps.** Three separate quantities change and
all three are countable straight off a contact sheet: seated discs (0 → 3 → 3-5),
collar lines per disc (0 → 2 → 4), pinned prints on the wall (0 → 1 → 2). A
builder who swapped headings against a static bench would produce a sheet where
those counts never move, which is visible without reading a word.

**The shuffle test passes by construction.** Disc count only rises, collar lines
only fill, prints only pin. Time has a direction you can reconstruct from any two
stills.

**It is not the thin cut.** The rejected thin carrier was six dots on a circle
that never gained detail. These are not dots on a ring: they are edge-on plates
that start blank, gain an etched rim on selection, then grow a four-line collar
underneath. The object gets denser, not busier — the rack, the rings, the collars
and the prints are all detail the viewer can go back and read a second time.

**It is not the slow cut either.** The ladder yields roughly twenty-two discrete
gain events across ~140s — lamp, rack, barrel, three rings, four notches, six
branch discs, two prints, the reload, four collar lines, four definitions, the
resolve, the pull-back — so pace comes from things arriving, not from beats being
split or the picture churning.

**It stays inside the narration.** Every count on screen is one the script says
out loud: three criteria, four kinds of impact, three clauses per branch example,
three to five experiences, four parts to the bullet formula. The rack is
deliberately uncountable because the script never says how much experience you
have. The Next Move Statement sits at the lamp, upstream, because the script
treats it as something you already point with — not something this lesson
produces.
