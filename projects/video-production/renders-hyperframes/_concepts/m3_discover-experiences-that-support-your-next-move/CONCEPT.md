# CONCEPT — m3_discover-experiences-that-support-your-next-move

Judged 2026-08-05 against the two APPROVED rows of
`design-system/docs/taste.md` only. Assigned carrier family (binding):
**the gallery wall and its hanging rail, fed by a storage rack.**

## Concept angle

A storage rack of backs-turned canvases stands at the frame's edge and a few of
them — only a few — turn face-forward, cross to a single gallery rail, and get
framed, tested, and captioned; the rack-and-rail stands on frame from the title
card to the final beat (**100% of runtime**, no beat without it).

## The carrying object

One picture, flat and geometric, in the brand palette, spanning the full canvas
from the first beat to the last. It has five parts and nothing else:

- **The wall** — a wide panel, slightly lighter than the background, occupying
  roughly the right two-thirds of the canvas at the open and the full width by
  the close.
- **The rail** — ONE thin horizontal hairline crossing the wall at about 55%
  frame height, spanning ~80% of the wall's width. It is a picture rail: things
  hang *from* it, below it. It has no nodes, no checkpoint, no travelling
  marker, and no segment of it ever fills with colour or changes weight. Its
  only job is to be the line the hangers touch.
- **The storage rack** — at the far left, a dense overlapping row of plain
  rectangles tilted 6–8° off vertical, blank, backs turned, drawn deep enough
  that the eye reads "many" and cannot count them. **No number is ever printed
  on frame.**
- **A chosen piece** — a canvas that leaves the rack turns face-forward (its
  face carries a few simple abstract marks: two short rules and a bar, or one
  small block — never an icon, never a picture of a thing), gains a 3px double
  border, and hangs on the rail. Three aspect ratios only — tall, wide, square
  — so the rail reads as a rhythm, not a row of clones.
- **A placard** — under each hung piece, a small rectangle ruled into four short
  lines, which fill in one at a time: Responsibility, Action, Measurable
  outcome, Scope.

Three pieces of rail furniture arrive once each and are never removed: a **head
card** at the rail's left end carrying the Next Move Statement (the wall's own
label — it is the source the wall is hung against, in hand before the lesson
starts); and **three ruled test tags** standing along the rail — strengths,
scope, impact — with the impact tag notched four ways for improved outcomes,
better processes, stronger relationships, and growth.

Above the rail, in a band used for nothing else, hang up to **two pinned sample
plates**: the leadership/strategic example and the lateral-move example, each a
small card with three edge-on mini-pieces beneath it. Once a plate pins, it
dims and stays pinned for the rest of the runtime.

## Milestone frames — the ACCUMULATION CONTRACT

The builder owes these four pictures. Every beat must increase at least one of
the four counters in *What accumulates*; a beat that moves none of them is a
dead beat and must be re-authored.

**25% — the storage room.** Left-heavy and lopsided. The rack owns the left
two-thirds: a deep overlapping stack of blank, backs-turned rectangles. The
wall behind is bare except for the empty rail hairline. One canvas has been
pulled a hand's width proud of the stack and stopped there — the question,
held, not yet answered. Nothing hangs. No head card, no tags, no placards, no
plates. *Counters: face-forward 0 · rail furniture 0 · plates 0 · placard rules
0.*

**50% — the wall gets a label and a test.** Weight shifts to centre. The head
card is installed at the rail's left end. Three ruled test tags now stand along
the rail — strengths, scope, impact — the impact tag carrying its four notches.
Two canvases have turned face-forward, crossed, and hung with their double
borders; the marks on their faces are readable for the first time. The rack is
still deep. No placards yet, and the band above the rail is still empty.
*Counters: face-forward 2 · rail furniture 4 · plates 0 · placard rules 0.*

**75% — three registers, and the captions begin.** The frame reads in three
horizontal bands for the first time. ABOVE the rail: both sample plates are now
pinned and dimmed, leadership/strategic at the left, lateral move at the right,
each with its three edge-on mini-pieces. ON the rail: four pieces hang evenly
spaced — the previous two have stepped along to make room, still framed, still
face-forward. BELOW: under each hung piece a placard has appeared with its
first rule written and three rules still blank. The rack is still visibly deep.
*Counters: face-forward 4 · rail furniture 4 · plates 2 · placard rules 1 of 4.*

**100% — the room.** Pull back to the whole wall at once, at a scale used
nowhere else in the video. Head card at the rail's head; two dim plates above;
four framed pieces spaced along the rail; four placards below, every one of
their four rules now written; and the rack still standing deep at the edge —
faces forward now, but unframed and unhung: kept, not displayed. **No new
element enters on this beat.** The wall is simply legible end to end for the
first time. *Counters: face-forward 4 · rail furniture 4 · plates 2 · placard
rules 4 of 4.*

## What accumulates

Four ladders, each strictly one-directional, so the sheet can be put back in
order from the pictures alone:

1. **Face-away → face-forward.** Once a canvas turns, it never turns back.
2. **Rail furniture.** Bare hairline → head card → three test tags (impact
   notched four ways). Nothing is ever removed or replaced.
3. **Pinned sample plates.** 0 → 1 → 2. Once pinned, a plate dims and stays —
   so the second worked example ARRIVES BESIDE the first instead of replacing
   it.
4. **Placard rules.** No placards → placards with one rule → placards with all
   four. This is the finest-grain accumulator and it deliberately runs through
   the final third, so the closing stretch still has something to earn.

One counter runs the other way and must be bounded: **rack depth is
non-increasing, but at the close it must still read as clearly deep — no
thinner than two-thirds of its opening depth.** The script's point is that you
do not need to display every piece; a rack emptied onto the rail says the
opposite. Only the few that hang ever leave it.

## The payoff beat

The final beat, landing on "you make every bullet work harder for you": the
wide hold on the entire wall. This frame could not have been drawn at 0:10 —
the rail was a bare line, no placard existed, no plate was pinned, and every
canvas was blank-backed in the stack. The payoff is **recognition, not
addition**: the viewer sees that the gallery was assembled out of the same
pieces that were in the storage room the whole time, which is the script's own
closing point — this isn't about inventing a new story.

## Grafted from the runner-up

Three things from Pitch B, all of which repair real holes in Pitch A:

- **The two worked examples become permanent artifacts, not swapped content.**
  B's "pinned prints that dim and stay" is the single best idea in either pitch.
  In A, the leadership and lateral examples were handled by changing the head
  card and re-sorting the rail — a beat whose only visible change is text, which
  is the thin cut's failure exactly. Pinning them above the rail turns that
  stretch into two GAIN events.
- **The four notches on the impact tag** (B's notched impact ring), so the
  script's four kinds of impact are a countable feature of the object rather
  than a bullet list beside it.
- **The rack must stay full.** B was right and A was wrong: A ran the rack down
  to "a thin leaning remainder," which contradicts "you don't need to display
  every piece." The bounded rack-depth invariant above is B's correction.

## Batch-diversity note

The carrier is a **wall, a rail, and a rack of physical pieces** — no light
source anywhere in it. Nothing in this concept emits, focuses, projects, or
lands light: no lamp, no beam, no lens, no glass, no illuminated case, no
under-lit surface. That keeps it clear of the darkroom light table, the
lighthouse lens, and the lit museum exhibit case assigned to the sibling M3
lessons. It is also not a brick wall or skyline (the wall is a plain flat panel
and never gains courses, windows, or a horizon), not a ruled worksheet, not a
peg-and-thread board (nothing is threaded between two points), not a scrap
field feeding a statement strip, not a trail marker, not plant cuttings, not a
potted tree, and not a truss.

**The one collision to police is the rail itself.** A career track with a
checkpoint node is assigned elsewhere in the batch, and the APPROVED m1 cut
also carries a horizontal line. This rail must never read as either: it gains
no nodes, no filled segment, no dashed future, no travelling marker, and no
label sitting *on* it. Everything hangs BELOW it or pins ABOVE it; the line
itself is inert hardware from the first beat to the last.

## Judge's note

**Likeliest failure mode:** the middle stretch collapsing into "same wall, new
heading." The script's two branch examples (leadership/strategic, lateral move)
are the natural place for a builder to change the head card, re-word the rail,
and call that the re-arrangement — which is the rejected thin cut's "the
highlight moves around," dressed as a gallery.

**Concrete guard:** the four counters in the accumulation contract are the
build's acceptance test, not prose. Every beat must raise at least one of
*face-forward canvases / rail-furniture items / pinned plates / placard rules
written*, and none may ever fall. In particular the branch stretch is only
valid if plate 1 pins on the leadership beat, plate 2 pins on the lateral beat,
and both are still on frame at the close. A contact sheet where two beats show
the same counter tuple has a dead beat in it, and that is visible without
reading a word of copy.

**Second guard, for the collision above:** if any snapshot shows the rail with a
coloured or thickened segment, a node, or a marker riding along it, the carrier
has drifted into the sibling career-track family and the beat is re-authored.

## Scores

**Pitch A — metaphor-first (gallery wall, rail, storage rack).** Names a
concrete drawable object taken from the script's own sentence, so the picture is
a second reading of the narration rather than a claim laid on top of it (Q1:
strong). Its four frames are genuinely four pictures — blank rack, labelled and
tested rail, populated rail with placards starting, whole room — and it is the
only pitch that offers the lazy-build test a real answer, in the form of
numeric invariants a contact sheet can be graded against (Q2, Q5: strong). Its
accumulation is three-laddered and one-directional, with the finest grain
deliberately running late so the closing third still earns something (Q3:
strong). Two genuine defects: the mid-run re-hang leans on a text change and
would pass as a heading swap, and running the rack down to a thin remainder
argues against the script's own "you don't need to display every piece."

**Pitch B — accumulation-first (lantern bench: lamp, disc rack, gauge barrel,
wall patch).** The strongest *reasoning* of the two — it counts its gain events,
ties them to a runtime estimate, and shows its work on why it is neither the
slow cut nor the thin one, and its persistent dimmed prints are the best single
idea on the table. But the object is a five-part apparatus (lamp, tray, barrel,
beam, patch) invented for the lesson rather than drawn from it, which makes it
harder to draw as one thing and easier to render as small props in empty space
— the thin cut's actual visual failure. Decisively, its lamp-and-beam core sits
inside the darkroom light table and lighthouse lens families assigned to the
sibling M3 lessons and is forbidden here.

**Verdict: Pitch A wins**, with B's pinned-and-dimmed worked examples, its
notched impact criterion, and its full-rack discipline grafted in. The result is
expressed in the assigned family: the gallery wall and its hanging rail, fed by
a storage rack that never empties.
