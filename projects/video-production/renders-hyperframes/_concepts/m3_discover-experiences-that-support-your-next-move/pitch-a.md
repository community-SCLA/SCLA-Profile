# Pitch A — m3_discover-experiences-that-support-your-next-move

Lens: **metaphor-first**. The carrier is taken straight from the script's own
line — "Think of your resume and LinkedIn profile as a curated gallery, not a
storage room" — so the picture is a second reading of the narration, not a new
claim laid on top of it.

## The carrying object

**A gallery wall with one hanging rail, fed by a storage rack of face-away
canvases leaning at the frame's left edge.**

Drawn flat and geometric: the wall is a wide light panel crossed by one thin
horizontal rail line at eye height; the rack is a dense overlapping row of
plain rectangles tilted 6–8° off vertical, blank grey, backs turned. A piece
that gets chosen leaves the rack, turns face-forward (its face carries a few
simple marks — rules, a bar, a small block), gains a 3px double border, and
sits on the rail. Under each hung piece hangs a **placard**: a small rectangle
with four short ruled lines that fill in one at a time. Three aspect ratios
only — tall, wide, square — so the rail reads as a rhythm rather than a row of
clones. **No count is ever printed on frame**; the rack is a dense stack, not a
tally.

## Four frames

**25% — the storage room.** The rack owns two-thirds of the canvas: a deep
overlapping stack of blank, backs-turned rectangles. The wall behind is bare
except for the empty rail line. One canvas has been pulled a hand's width
proud of the stack and stopped there — the question, held, not yet answered.
Nothing is hung. Nothing has a placard.

**50% — the wall gets a head and a test.** A title card is installed at the
left end of the rail (the Next Move Statement — the wall's own label). Three
short ruled tags stand along the rail as the test every piece is put to:
strengths, scope, impact. The rack has begun to turn: several canvases now
face forward showing their marks, two have crossed to the rail and hung. The
stack is visibly shallower and the wall is no longer empty.

**75% — the re-hang, and the placards start.** The title card at the head
changes, and the rail re-sorts under it: a different set comes up, the
previous pieces step back along the rail — but every canvas that has turned
face-forward *stays* face-forward. Four pieces sit hung and evenly spaced.
Below each, a placard has appeared with its first rule written
(Responsibility), the remaining three still blank rules waiting. The rack is
down to a thin leaning remainder.

**100% — the room.** Pull back to the whole wall at once: title card at the
head, the hung pieces framed and spaced along the rail, and every placard's
four rules now complete — Responsibility, Action, Measurable outcome, Scope.
The leftover rack is a thin stack at the edge, faces forward, unframed and
unhung: kept, not displayed. No new element enters on this beat; the room is
simply legible end to end for the first time.

## What accumulates

Three things, each strictly one-directional across the whole runtime, so the
frames can be put back in order from the pictures alone:

1. **Face-away → face-forward.** Once a canvas turns, it never turns back. The
   rack's blank-backed depth only ever decreases.
2. **Rail occupancy and framing.** The rail goes bare → titled → tested →
   populated → complete. Hung pieces carry borders that unhung ones don't.
3. **Placard rules.** Zero placards → placards with one rule → placards with
   all four. This is the finest-grain accumulator and it runs through the last
   third, so the closing stretch still has something to earn.

The mid-run re-hang re-arranges the rail *without* resetting any of the three
— that is deliberate. It is the approved-cut signature: a set that arrives
once and is thereafter only re-grouped, not swapped out for new material.

## The payoff beat

The final beat, on "you make every bullet work harder for you": the camera
holds the entire wall for the first time — head card, full rail, four complete
placards, exhausted rack. **This frame could not have been drawn at 0:10**, when
the rail was empty, no placard existed, and every canvas was blank-backed in
the stack. The payoff is recognition, not addition: the viewer sees that the
gallery was assembled out of the same pieces that were in the storage room the
whole time. Nothing was invented — which is exactly the script's closing point
("This isn't about inventing a new story").

## Why this is not lazy

A builder cannot satisfy this with heading swaps against a static prop,
because the prop's **physical state** is the contract, and it differs at every
milestone. The milestone frames in CONCEPT.md can be written as checkable
invariants:

- face-away canvases in the rack: strictly non-increasing, beat to beat;
- face-forward canvases: strictly non-decreasing, and reaching >0 before the
  midpoint;
- pieces on the rail: 0 at the open, >0 by 50%, unchanged in count across the
  re-hang, full at the close;
- placard rules written: 0 → 1 → 4, never regressing.

Any beat that leaves all four of those numbers untouched is a dead beat by
definition, visible on the contact sheet, and caught by the shuffle test. The
object also earns its whole frame rather than sitting as a small prop in empty
space: rack plus wall plus rail spans the canvas from the first beat to the
last, and the detail lives at two scales — the wall's arrangement read at a
glance, the placard rules read on a second look.

**Fidelity note.** Every word that reaches the frame is the script's own:
gallery / storage room; strengths, scope, impact; improved outcomes, better
processes, stronger relationships, growth; led teams, influenced decisions,
owned results; learned quickly, translated skills, collaborated;
Responsibility, Action, Measurable outcome, Scope. The hung set matches the
script's stated "three to five." No count, ordering, or claim appears on
screen that the narration does not itself say.
