# PITCH A — what-energizes-me_early-career-boost

**Lens: metaphor-first.**

## The carrying object

**A single wide sheet of paper — the Energy Ledger — ruled down the middle into two columns, that fills with small written slips torn from your own past, and is finally re-grouped into two or three circled clusters.**

It is drawn as one landscape sheet occupying roughly 80% of the frame: a soft-cornered page with a hairline border, a 2px vertical rule at its centre, and two short small-caps column heads sitting above the rule ("energizes" left, "drains" right). The experiences are slips — squat rounded rectangles, roughly 3:2, each carrying two or three short ruled strokes standing in for handwriting plus a thick coloured left edge that codes the kind of experience (class, job, project, volunteer role, group work). A loose stack of unplaced slips waits at the sheet's right edge; drained slips render outline-only at reduced weight so the two columns read at a glance by ink density, not by colour alone. Clusters, when they form, are enclosed by a single hand-drawn ellipse stroke — one continuous line, visibly drawn rather than snapped to a shape.

Nothing that has settled ever moves again. Slips are placed, they stay; the only motion is new ink arriving or a placed group being lifted deliberately for the re-sort.

## Four frames

**25% — the empty ledger and the pile.**
The sheet is almost bare: centre rule drawn, both column heads set, no slips placed. At the right edge sits the stack of unplaced slips, tall and squared-off, three of them fanned face-up but sitting *across* the rule, unassigned. The frame reads as "you already have the material and none of it is sorted yet." Ink is concentrated at one edge; the page is mostly white.

**50% — both columns populated, the pile shrunken.**
The sheet is genuinely two-sided. The left column carries a dense run of filled slips stacked down the page; the right column carries fewer, outline-only slips with more air between them. The stack at the edge is visibly shorter than at 25%. Off the sheet's top-left corner, one oversized slip lies face-down and set aside — the single answer nobody is looking for. Ink now runs the full width of the frame and the vertical rule is doing real work.

**75% — the columns break up.**
The left column's slips lift off their rows and drift into three loose constellations across the sheet's lower two-thirds; the ruled column lines show faintly beneath them. One slip is held up alone, larger than the rest, and has gained a second and third ruled line of writing — the follow-up detail a question pulled out of it. One hand-drawn ellipse is partway around the nearest constellation, the stroke stopping mid-air. The right column stays exactly where it was and dims. This is the only frame where anything is in the air.

**100% — the whole sheet, re-read.**
The full page sits centred and static: three closed ellipses, each with a short written label beside it, the column rule now the faintest line on the page and the column heads reduced to whispers. Every slip is inside a cluster or in the dimmed drain column; the edge stack is empty, its outline the only thing left where it stood. The picture is no longer a sorting table — it is a pattern map, and it can be taken in as one image.

## What accumulates

Three things, all countable off the pixels:

1. **Ink arrives and never leaves.** The sheet goes from bare rule to two populated columns to a labelled map. Every beat that places a slip leaves it placed; total ink on the page only rises.
2. **The stack empties as the sheet fills.** There is a visible conservation: what leaves the right-edge pile lands on the page. By the closing frame the pile is gone and its absence is drawn.
3. **The page changes topology once, late.** Rows become clusters. That is a re-arrangement of material already on screen — the same slips, a different structure — which is why the 75% and 100% frames could not have been drawn at 0:10: there was nothing placed to re-group.

Individual slips also gain detail (a second and third line of writing) during the coach stretch, so "does anything gain detail?" has an answer at slip scale as well as page scale.

## The payoff beat

The final beat — "two or three patterns, that's all you need to start pointing yourself somewhere." The camera holds the whole sheet at once for the first time since 25%, the last ellipse closes, and the column rule fades under the clusters. The object completes by **re-reading**: the two-column intake form the lesson opened with turns out to have been a pattern map the whole time. The closing frame contains every slip the video ever placed, in a structure that did not exist until the last stretch.

## Why this is not lazy

A builder cannot serve this with heading swaps against a static prop, because **the prop is never the same twice**: slip population, column balance, stack height, and — at the end — the grouping structure are all different pixel states, and each one is pinned by a milestone frame. The shuffle test passes by construction: you can restore beat order from ink density and stack height alone, without reading a single word.

The topology change is the specific defence. A build that faked this with headings would have to show the *same* arrangement of slips under a new title, and the 75% and 100% milestones make that visibly wrong — rows and clusters are not the same picture. And because the closing frame is defined as containing everything placed earlier, the builder cannot skip the middle: an unpopulated page at 50% makes the payoff frame impossible to draw.

**Fidelity note.** Everything on the page comes from the narration: the two columns and the worksheet's name, the experience kinds (classes, jobs, projects, volunteer roles, group work), the contribution kinds (organising, creating, helping, solving, analysing) as cluster labels, the one big set-aside slip for the lightning-bolt answer that is not the goal, and "two or three" as the cluster count. No totals are shown, no slip is numbered, and the slips carry ruled strokes rather than invented sentences — so the picture never asserts a count, a sequence, or an experience the script does not say.
