# CONCEPT — what-energizes-me_early-career-boost

## Concept angle

The Energizing Work Worksheet drawn at wall size — a two-column board fed by a
visibly emptying reserve of experience slips, which threads, inks, and finally
re-sorts onto a horizontal axis of named ingredients — on frame from the second
beat (~0:08) to the closing frame, roughly 95% of runtime.

## The carrying object

**A pinned board, wall size, holding ~80% of the safe area at all times.**

- **The board.** A ruled surface with a hairline border and one hairline
  vertical gutter down the centre, two column heads set above it in the brand
  display weight (energizes / drains). Tokens come from the workspace's
  `config/tokens.yml` — no hexes are named here.
- **The reserve.** A squared-off stack of unplaced slips standing against the
  right edge, drawn at full height before the board carries anything. It is the
  experience the viewer already has — classes, jobs, projects, volunteer roles,
  group work — and it is finite. Every slip that lands on the board comes out of
  it, so the stack's height falls monotonically across the whole runtime and its
  empty outline is still drawn at the close.
- **A slip.** A 3:2 rounded rectangle, hairline border. Its **bottom edge
  profile** carries the charge — serrated for the ones that left you buzzing,
  dead-flat for the ones that left you flat — so polarity survives greyscale and
  never rests on colour alone. Its **interior** is 0–4 short ruled ink strokes
  standing in for how specific the example is; strokes are only ever added.
- **Threads.** Single-weight straight chords pinned corner-to-corner between
  slips that share something. Several cross the gutter.
- **The tag rail.** Five short word-tags — organizing, creating, helping,
  solving, analyzing — hung along the top edge, unpinned, with nowhere to go,
  from the moment they arrive until the final beat.
- **The direction edge.** A hairline arrow-rule in the right margin, faint from
  the moment the gutter exists, inking solid only at the close.
- **The bands.** Three full-width tinted lanes that *replace* the gutter at the
  end; they never sit beside it.

Nothing that has settled ever moves again. Every change is an entrance, a
migration of not-yet-settled material, or the one authored re-sort.

## Milestone frames — the accumulation contract

- **25% (~0:48) — bottom-heavy, nothing sorted.** The reserve stands at full
  height against the right edge. A loose heap of plain, uninked, unsided slips
  lies along the bottom rail. The gutter is drawn only partway down from the
  top. The previous lesson's five conditions sit demoted to a small corner card,
  top-left. All the ink is in the bottom-right of the frame; the board above is
  open. *Composition: horizontal weight at the floor.*
- **50% (~1:35) — vertical, every slip has a side.** The gutter runs full height
  and is doing real work. The heap has migrated up and split into two populated
  columns; the left is denser, but the right is genuinely populated and stays —
  the drain side is data, never swept away. Every slip now shows a serrated or
  flat bottom edge. The reserve is visibly about half its 25% height. Two struck
  attractors sit crossed out in the top margin, left visible rather than erased:
  the single lightning-bolt answer, and a job title. No threads anywhere, every
  slip interior still blank. *Composition: two tall stacks either side of a
  dominant vertical.*
- **75% (~2:23) — a network with an uneven ink map.** Slip population is
  unchanged since ~2:00 — this stretch adds no new slips at all. Threads are
  pinned between slips that share something, several crossing the gutter, and
  two thread-clusters bulge visibly out of the even scatter. A question-pin sits
  partway down the left margin; the slips it has already passed carry three or
  four interior strokes while their untouched neighbours are still blank, so the
  board's ink density is conspicuously patchy. The five-word tag rail hangs
  across the top, still unpinned. The reserve is a stub. *Composition: a web,
  with density inside the marks rather than in their count.*
- **100% (~3:10) — horizontal, re-read.** The gutter is struck through. Three
  full-width bands are ruled across the board and every slip has slid into one.
  Three tags come off the rail and pin to the band heads; the remaining two grey
  out in place. The reserve is gone — only its empty outline remains where it
  stood. The direction edge inks solid off the right margin. *Composition: three
  horizontal lanes pointing right; the vertical is gone.*

No two of those four could be mistaken for one another: the dominant axis is
floor, then vertical, then web, then horizontal.

## What accumulates

Five quantities, all countable off a contact sheet without reading a word:

1. **Slips placed** — only rises.
2. **Reserve height** — only falls, and its total is conserved against (1).
   Growth on the board is drawn as a trade, not as material appearing from
   nowhere, and the pile's absence is drawn at the end.
3. **Interior ink strokes per slip** — only rises, and rises unevenly, so the
   board gains texture rather than just population.
4. **Threads** — only rises, over a population that has stopped growing.
5. **Tags left on the rail** — only falls, and only at the very end.

The board also changes its sort axis exactly once, late: a left/right sort of
your past becomes a top-to-bottom set of ingredients. That is a re-arrangement
of material already on screen, which is why the closing frame is undrawable at
0:10 — at 0:10 there are five faint marks, no slips, no gutter, no threads, no
bands and no rail.

## The payoff beat

The final beat — "Two or three patterns. That's all you need to start pointing
yourself somewhere." The gutter is struck, the three bands rule across, and the
tag rail that has hung unused for roughly ninety seconds is finally spent: three
tags drop onto the band heads. The whole board is taken in at once for the first
time since 25%, and it re-reads — the intake form the lesson opened with turns
out to have been a pattern map. The empty reserve outline sits beside it as the
receipt: everything on the board came out of the viewer's own past.

## Grafted from the runner-up

**Pitch A's conservation mechanic: a finite reserve stack that visibly empties
as the board fills, with its absence drawn at the close.** Pitch B already
opened on a heap of loose slips, but consumed it by 1:00 and then let new slips
simply arrive — which is the cheapest legal pass wearing a costume ("keep adding
cards"). Making the reserve persist across the whole runtime as a depleting
source does three things B could not do alone: it gives the accumulation a
source, a cost and an end point; it adds a monotone quantity legible at thumbnail
size from the frame's right edge, which strengthens the shuffle test; and it ties
the growth to the script's own claim that you already have the data.

## Judge's note

**The failure mode this will slide into: a mid-runtime coast.** The script's
long middle — the "genuinely hard to name" stretch and the AI-coach walkthrough
— is discursive prose with no new nouns in it, and the board at that point is
already built. The obvious cheat is to hold the 50% picture and swap headings
across eight or ten beats. That is precisely the "beats 12–19 are the same
picture with new headings" checkout, and the thin rejected cut passed every
numeric gate doing exactly this.

**The guard, concretely:** every beat between the 50% and 100% milestones must
move at least one of the five counters above, and the builder must be able to
name which one for each beat — reserve height, threads, or interior strokes are
the only three available in that stretch, because slip population is frozen
after ~2:00 by design. A beat that moves none of them is not a beat; merge it or
re-author it. Second guard, against the thin cut: the board holds ~80% of the
safe area at every beat, and after the 25% milestone its *occupied* region can
never be small enough to cover with one hand — a large ruled rectangle with a
few small marks in it is the 6-dot circle again.

## Scores

**Pitch A — Energy Ledger sheet.** (1) Names a genuinely drawable object: a
ruled sheet, slips with a coded left edge, hand-drawn ellipses — no abstraction
wearing a shape. (2) Its four frames differ, but 75% and 100% are both
"clusters", one mid-flight and one closed, so the last quarter risks reading as
one picture settling. (3) Accumulation is real and elegant — ink only rises, the
pile empties into the page, and the topology changes once — with the
conservation idea the single best mechanic in either pitch. (4) The payoff
re-reads the sheet, but it arrives unplanted: nothing has been held in reserve
for it, so the close resolves rather than pays off. (5) The lazy-build defence
rests on "the prop is never the same twice", which is true but softer than a
prop that is structurally incompatible with its earlier self. Strong, slightly
under-specified in the back half. **Runner-up.**

**Pitch B — the Energizing Work Worksheet at wall size.** (1) Same class of
object and better drawn: the slip's bottom-edge profile carries polarity without
colour, interior strokes carry specificity, and the object is the one the
narration itself names. (2) The strongest four-frame answer available — the
dominant compositional axis changes at every milestone (floor, vertical, web,
horizontal), so no two frames can be confused even at thumbnail size. (3) Five
independent monotone counters rather than one, and keeping the drain column
populated is both faithful to the script and a real defence against the frame
emptying out. (4) The payoff is planted: the tag rail hangs unused for ninety
seconds and the final beat spends it, and the axis flip is a structural re-read
rather than a fade. (5) The sharpest anti-lazy argument of the two — a
two-column board and a three-band board cannot be the same picture, and three
rungs deliberately add *nothing new* so "keep adding cards" is foreclosed. Its
one risk is density, which the judge's note above fences. **WINNER — Pitch B,
with Pitch A's depleting reserve grafted in.**
