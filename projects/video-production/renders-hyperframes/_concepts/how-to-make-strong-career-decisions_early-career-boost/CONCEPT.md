# CONCEPT — how-to-make-strong-career-decisions_early-career-boost

Winner: **PITCH B** (peg-and-thread decision board), with one idea grafted from
Pitch A. Judged against the two APPROVED rows in
`design-system/docs/taste.md` only.

## Concept angle

A dark ruled **decision board** that gains pins, punched strips and one taut
thread as the process runs, then strips back to five holes in its left rail —
the same object is on screen from the first beat to the last held frame
(**100% of runtime**, carrier never leaves the canvas).

## The carrying object

A full-bleed workshop **board** in the deep brand ground, carrying a faint
square rule, with a narrow vertical **rail** down the left edge. Drawn as flat
geometry with one light source — no photographic texture, no wood grain, no
paper.

- **A pin** = a filled circle in the brand accent with a short offset ellipse
  beneath it for the shaft's shadow. Every option the person is considering is
  one pin. Pins are identical and bare when they arrive — they have not been
  rated yet, and that is the point.
- **A punched strip** = a small rounded-rectangle outline under a pin holding
  between three and seven square cells, each filled or hollow. Present/absent
  only. **No number is printed anywhere on frame.**
- **A thread** = a 3–4px stroke with one soft bend. The committed thread is
  drawn at double weight — the heaviest line in the video, once.
- **The boundary** = a scored inset rectangle with corner ticks, appearing when
  the decision is clarified. A small **tag** (rectangle with a notched corner
  and an empty ruled date line) clips to its top-right corner.
- **Uncertainty** = a stipple field of irregular dots at varying opacity
  creeping in from the right edge. "Clearing" it means replacing stipple with
  clean rule inside a hard-edged window — a measurable ink change, never a glow.
- **The rail** gains one small dark **hole** at each of the five steps, punched
  on the way past and never remarked on.
- **The tally columns** (grafted, see below) = two short columns of flat marks,
  one mark grossly oversized, drawn once near the start and never erased.

Ground, accent and neutral come from the workspace's copied `tokens.yml`. No
hex is fixed here. Pins enter once and never move again except in the single
authored re-sort transit; nothing wobbles, breathes or re-marks after it
settles.

## Milestone frames — the ACCUMULATION CONTRACT

Percentages are of total runtime; the script anchor is given so the builder can
place them exactly. Each milestone must introduce **a kind of mark not present
at the previous milestone** — not merely more of the same mark.

**25% — "a reality check" / entering "most people do not use any process."**
A large, mostly empty ruled board. Left of centre: two short columns of flat
tally marks, small in the frame, one mark in the left column at twice the
stroke weight and height of its neighbours, shouldering the others out of
alignment. The right third of the board is grey stipple, densest at the edge.
Three or four short marks float outside both columns at wrong angles, anchored
to nothing. **No pins, no boundary, no threads, no strips.**

**50% — step one done, step two ("widen your options") starting.**
A scored boundary now frames the working area with a dated tag clipped to its
top-right corner. The drifting marks are swept. The tally columns have been
pushed *outside* the boundary to the bottom-left and stay there. Inside the
boundary stand **exactly three pins**, well spaced, with clean board around
them; a fourth is entering at the margin. **Two holes** in the rail. Stipple
unchanged. Reads as: order arrived before material did.

**75% — mid step four, the cheap experiment running.**
The board is crowded. Pins in ranked rows across most of the surface, **a
punched strip under every pin**, the filled/hollow cells stepping down row by
row so the sort order is readable across the room. Several pins sit outside the
boundary's inner margin — the overrun from widening, never tidied away. One
thread arcs from a high row out into the stipple and back; inside the loop it
made, one small window of grey has resolved into crisp ruled surface, hard-edged
against the stipple around it. **Four holes** in the rail.

**100% — closing loop.** Two-part frame. First, pulled back: every layer at
once — boundary, dated tag, the full overrun population, the ranked strips, the
cleared window, and one **heavy inked thread** from the rail's start peg to a
single pin with a review tag hanging off it. Then the strip-back: threads lift,
pins withdraw, and the held final image is the scored boundary, **five holes in
a clean vertical column** down the left rail, and — still outside the boundary,
unchanged since 30% — the two flat tally columns with their one oversized mark.

Any build reaching the gate with the same pin count at 50% and 75%, or with no
strips at 75%, has failed the **concept**, not the gate.

## What accumulates

1. **Population** — 0 pins → 3 pins → a field arriving in three authored waves,
   the last two overrunning the boundary and staying overrun.
2. **Internal detail** — bare pins gain a punched strip each; the strips are the
   only place factors are recorded.
3. **A wholesale re-sort** — pins travel exactly once, into ranked rows. The
   arrangement a viewer memorised at 50% is gone at 75%. This is the single
   hardest thing here to fake and the easiest to verify.
4. **Legibility bought back, partially** — one window of stipple resolves; the
   rest stays grey permanently, because the script says you rarely need
   certainty.
5. **Structure on the rail** — one hole per step, five by the end, never
   explained while they appear.
6. **Then subtraction** — the final move removes almost everything. A concept
   whose last beat is a removal cannot be satisfied by adding a heading.

## The payoff beat

The closing loop paragraph, in two moves. **First**, the pull-back is the only
frame in the video that holds every accumulated layer at once — unreachable at
0:10 by construction, because at 0:10 the board holds one column of tally
marks. **Then** the object re-reads: threads lift, pins withdraw, and the five
rail holes — punched one at a time on the way past, never named — resolve into
the process itself, empty and ready to be strung again for which internship,
which major, which offer, which next move. The re-read is earned, not announced;
the viewer watched every hole get punched without being told what it was for.

## Grafted from the runner-up

**Pitch A's third accumulation: the opening artifact is outmatched, not
deleted.** Pitch B swept the tally columns away by its 1:30 rung, which quietly
erases the script's own opening argument. Grafted in: the two tally columns and
their one grossly oversized mark are **never erased**. At step one they are
pushed outside the scored boundary; they sit there, flat and small, for the rest
of the runtime, and they are still on the final held frame — the only marks on
the board that never gained anything. That demotion-by-persistence *draws* "you
can do much better" instead of asserting it, and it gives the stripped-back
closing frame a second thing to read against.

Pitch A's paper-fold silhouette was not carried: a warm off-white sheet on a
desk fights the brand ground, and the concept spent its first quarter on a
single unchanging panel.

## Judge's note

**Most likely slide: population growth mistaken for accumulation.** The stretch
from step two to step three is "more pins appear," which is the thin-cut failure
in a denser costume — a viewer's eyes have nothing new to *do* while identical
marks multiply, exactly as the six-dot circle offered nothing but a moving
highlight.

**Guard:** every milestone must introduce a *new kind of mark*, and the builder
checks this literally — column → pin → strip → thread and cleared window →
inked line plus the rail read as a column. The punched strips must begin
arriving before the pin field is finished filling, so the dense stretch is
gaining detail and population at the same time, and the re-sort must land as one
authored positional change at a scene boundary (never idle motion on settled
content). If the 50% and 75% frames differ only by how many pins are on screen,
re-author the 75% frame before rendering.

**Second guard, fidelity:** nothing on this board weighs, tips, balances or
compares two sides — the sibling lesson already shipped that device. The strip
is a present/absent punch card and what it produces is spatial ranking, not a
beam finding level. Every element traces to spoken narration; no counts, scores
or dates are printed on frame, and the tag's date line stays empty.

## Scores

**Pitch A — the folding sheet.** (1) Names its object cleanly; paper is the most
drawable thing either pitch offers. (2) Its four frames differ in *silhouette*,
which is a real answer to the four-frame test — but the first quarter is one
narrow panel with a lifting crease, a dead stretch of roughly 25% of runtime
built in at the concept stage. (3) Accumulation is genuine and monotone (panels,
ink, the taped stub) and idea 3 — the opening artifact demoted rather than
deleted — is the single best line in either document. (4) The payoff is strong:
the full spread seen once, plus the return crease. (5) It survives the lazy-build
test on silhouette change alone. Its costs are real, though: skeuomorphic
off-white paper on a desk fights the brand ground and the pixel-ink bands, the
sustained camera pull-back is a motion risk, and four of five panels are
line-and-slip variations that could quietly collapse into text columns. **Strong
second.**

**Pitch B — the decision board.** (1) Names a concrete object and, better, names
what each *part* depicts — a pin is an option, a punch is a factor, a thread is
the decision — so the drawing instructions are unambiguous. (2) The four frames
are four unmistakably different pictures: sparse and adrift, structured and
under-populated, dense and sorted with a hole cut in the fog, then everything
and then almost nothing. (3) Accumulation is the richest of the two — count,
per-element detail, sort order, legibility, and structure on the rail — and it
closes with subtraction. (4) The payoff is the best available: a whole-run frame
plus a re-read that was seeded invisibly across the entire runtime, which is
precisely the approved cuts' move (a rail that is built early, then only ever
re-read). (5) It ships its own milestone contract with countable states, closing
the lazy-build hole. Its risk is complexity — several sub-systems to draw, and a
middle stretch that could read as identical marks multiplying. That risk is
addressable by a guard; Pitch A's dead first quarter is structural.

**Verdict: PITCH B wins**, with Pitch A's outmatched-not-deleted tally columns
grafted in and the "new kind of mark per milestone" guard armed against the
thin-cut relapse.
