# CONCEPT — `skills-for-the-ai-era-future_early-career-boost`

Judged against the two APPROVED rows in `design-system/docs/taste.md` only
(`m1_mini-syllabus` freeform trial; `build-direction-before-you-build-a-plan`
Jul 30–31). The 2026-08-05 `career-building-is-a-repeatable-process` cut is a
SHIPPED-NOT-A-REFERENCE row and anchors nothing here.

**Winner: PITCH B**, with one element grafted from PITCH A.

---

## Concept angle

A brick skyline of skills that rises, sinks and is finally traced into one
wave — the strip arrives on the first content beat and persists, unbroken, to
the last frame (**beat 2 → final beat, ≈95% of runtime**, well over the 60%
floor).

---

## The carrying object

**A left-to-right strip of stacked tiles standing on a single horizon rule,
walked by one small figure.**

- **The rule.** One horizontal stroke across the lower third, full frame
  width. It is the only line in the composition that never moves.
- **The columns.** Above the rule, columns built from small rounded
  rectangles — masonry, not measurement. Tiles are roughly a fifth of a
  column wide and stack with slightly uneven left edges so the profile reads
  as hand-laid brick. Column widths vary a little between neighbours. **Tile
  faces stay blank.** Naming happens in a label group set in brand type at
  legal size in the frame's clear upper-left area, joined to its column by
  one short leader line — never lettered inside a tile, which is how this
  concept stays above the 40px floor.
- **The bed.** Below the rule, the same rectangles lying flat in horizontal
  courses. The bed is the tasks AI is very good at. It only ever thickens,
  and it thickens partly by receiving bricks that fall out of columns above.
- **The ghost stratum.** The identical vocabulary drawn at low opacity with a
  dashed outline, offset down and to the left, sitting behind the live strip.
  It carries exactly one tall ghost column — the learn-to-code advice, the
  only falling thing the script names. Every other sunk or blank column stays
  **unlabelled**, because the narration names no other loser.
- **The walker.** A stick-and-circle figure about one column tall, so it
  reads as scale rather than as a character. Behind it, dotted footstep marks
  across the column tops it has already stood on. The trail only lengthens.
- **The rack.** In the final third, a small open frame slung at the walker's
  side that fills one lettered tile at a time. Rack tiles are copies pulled
  from the strip — the strip loses nothing.

No axis, no gridline, no tick, no baseline, no legend, no number anywhere.
The strip has a top edge and a bottom edge and nothing else a chart would
have.

## Milestone frames — THE ACCUMULATION CONTRACT

Each must be a genuinely different **silhouette**, readable at contact-sheet
thumbnail scale with every label unreadable. A build whose sheet is missing
any one of these is a failed build, not a matter of opinion.

**M1 — 25% · "Two strata, one flat shelf."**
Silhouette: *a low level plateau, with one tall faint spike behind it at the
left.* A near-level shelf of similar-height columns standing on a thin bed.
Four of them carry the hardest-to-automate skills the voice has just named —
judgment and decision-making, communication, relationships, and the ability
to keep learning — labelled in the clear upper area on leaders. Behind and
below, the dashed ghost layer with its single tall column for the old advice.
Two postures exist in the frame: lying down (the bed) and standing up (the
columns). Nothing has moved twice.

**M2 — 50% · "The shelf has tilted."**
Silhouette: *a sawtooth climbing left to right, over a bed twice as thick.*
The level shelf is gone. Some columns have visibly lost bricks downward into
the bed; others have gained bricks upward. The bed has thickened by two full
courses. The walker now stands mid-strip with a short dotted trail behind it,
and the first translucent category band is washed lightly across. The ghost
stratum has begun receding left.

**M3 — 75% · "Sorted, notched, carrying."**
Silhouette: *the same sawtooth, plus one solid block of equal-height columns
pushed together ahead of the walker, plus a small rectangle hanging at the
walker's side.* All four category bands are drawn. Each tile carries a small
corner notch for every band it falls inside, so a tile in two bands wears
two. Then the strip performs its **single re-arrangement of the whole
runtime**: the two-notch columns slide together into one cluster ahead of the
walker. The rack is half-filled with lettered tiles for the transferable
skills as the voice names them. This is the densest frame, and it is the only
beat in the video where anything already placed moves again.

**M4 — 100% · "The whole tide-line, crested."**
Silhouette: *one continuous curve running the full frame width, with a single
detached spike standing above it and untouched.* Pulled back so the entire
strip fits at once, wider than any earlier frame showed. One stroke is traced
across every column top, left edge to right edge, and the accumulated profile
resolves into a wave: ghost stratum in the trough at the far left, the thick
bed as the body of the water, the walker at the crest with a full rack of
six, the dotted trail running the whole width as the route up the face. Set
apart from the strip, with a clear gap and no trail reaching it, stands one
tall blank column the crest stroke does not touch.

## What accumulates

Seven counters, each monotonic, each legible from a still with the text
unreadable:

1. **Brick count.** Only up. The bed thickens by a course at the wealth line
   and again at the "until then" line. M1 and M4 differ by visible mass.
2. **Column heights.** Load-bearing in both directions — some sink into the
   bed, some grow. Freeze them and the closing stroke has nothing to trace,
   so the concept fails visibly rather than quietly.
3. **Trail length.** The dotted footsteps only lengthen. Any two stills can
   be ordered by trail alone — the shuffle test passed by construction.
4. **Band count.** Zero → one → four, then held.
5. **Notch count.** Tiles gain corner notches and never lose them.
6. **Rack fill.** Empty → half → full: three distinct silhouettes hanging off
   the walker across the last third, exactly where the rejected thin cut had
   nothing left to give.
7. **Strip width.** New columns land to the RIGHT of everything that existed
   at M2, so M4 is not a re-lettering of M2 — it is physically wider.

Bricks that leave a column are never deleted; they arrive in the bed. The
drawing keeps its own past, so "these skills lost value" is shown rather than
asserted.

## The payoff beat

**The final beat, on the script's own closing line about riding the wave.**
One continuous stroke is drawn across the top of every column that has
accumulated over the whole runtime, and the silhouette resolves into the wave
the narration names in its last sentence. Nothing new is invented at the
payoff — the stroke only connects what is already on screen, which is exactly
why it cannot exist earlier: at 0:10 there is one horizon rule and no tops to
connect, and at M2 the profile is too jagged and too short to read as one
curve. The closing frame is un-drawable at 0:10.

Secondary re-read at M3: the one-time re-sort into the two-notch cluster. It
lands as an event, not as churn, because it is the only re-arrangement in the
video.

## Grafted from the runner-up

**Pitch A's lone rock stack, carried across as the one detached, blank column
the crest stroke does not touch.**

Pitch B's ladder covers every line of the script except one: the penultimate
sentence, where the narration explicitly rejects the goal of finding a single
job that will never be automated. Pitch A had the only concrete shape for a
*rejected* idea — an isolated stack, unlabelled, drawn once, with no path
leading to it, that the trail simply passes by. Grafted in, it costs one
shape and earns three things: it visualises a sentence B would otherwise have
narrated over unchanged pixels; it gives the closing frame a second readable
element so the payoff is a comparison rather than a single gesture; and it
strengthens the crest stroke by giving it one top it pointedly excludes. It
stays unlabelled and is drawn only because the script rejects it by name.

## Judge's note

**The failure mode this concept will slide into is congestion, not
thinness.** Seven counters plus bands, notches, a ghost stratum, a walker, a
trail and a rack is a lot of vocabulary arriving in one frame. The M3 stretch
is where a builder will keep adding dressing until nothing individual is
legible at thumbnail scale — and the recovery move a builder reaches for is
to shrink everything, which pushes labels under the 40px floor and turns the
frame into texture. That is the rejected *slow* cut's churn problem in static
form.

**The guard, concretely:**

- **At most ONE new element class per milestone segment.** Bands are M2→M3.
  Notches are M3. The rack is M3→M4. Nothing else joins the vocabulary.
- **One lettered label group on frame at a time**, always in the clear
  upper-left area at legal size on a leader — never inside a tile. The 40px
  floor is never negotiated; if a label does not fit, the label count drops,
  never the type size.
- **The silhouette test.** If at one-third scale you cannot tell M3 from M2
  by outline alone, cut dressing — do not shrink elements.
- **Motion discipline.** Every tile, band, notch, rack tile and footstep
  arrives once and then holds pixel-static. The single declared exception is
  the M3 re-sort, and it happens once. No idle wobble, drift or shimmer on
  settled content.
- **Anti-chart guard.** Uneven tile edges, varying column widths, no axis, no
  gridline, no tick, no baseline label, no legend, no number. Heights are a
  re-reading of the script's own "drives down the value / drives up the
  value" and must never imply a measured quantity the narration does not
  state.

## Scores

**Pitch A — the shoreline.** *Name the object:* strong — a sloping shore with
labelled stakes and a rising tide is concrete, nameable and drawable. *Four
frames:* the strongest of the two on picture-variety, because the locus of
density genuinely migrates around the frame (bottom-left knot → upper-right
plots → dense middle → pulled-back whole coast). *What accumulates:* three
clean monotonic counters plus a re-read, elegantly minimal. *Payoff:* a scale
change no earlier beat could contain — legitimate and well-argued. *Lazy
test:* passes; state genuinely carries between beats. It loses on two things
that the reference sheets make decisive. First, its own M1 reserves "the
entire upper two-thirds of the frame" as empty drawn ground — that is the
rejected thin cut's exact signature (a small prop beside large empty space)
held for the first quarter of runtime. Second, and worse, its central
mechanism — submerged stakes surviving as pale washes under a translucent
tide — is a low-contrast opacity effect on a dark navy ground. The thin cut's
sheets show faint blue marks on navy already reading as near-empty at
thumbnail scale, and the shuffle test is a thumbnail-scale test. A's
accumulation is real but half of it is invisible where it has to be seen.
**Verdict: runner-up — right instincts, wrong physics for this palette.**

**Pitch B — the strip.** *Name the object:* concrete and drawable, and
structurally the closest relative to the approved `m1_mini-syllabus` cut — a
full-width spine with things standing on it — while adding a vertical
dimension that spine lacked. Its one weakness is chart-adjacency, which the
Judge's note arms a guard against. *Four frames:* four distinct silhouettes,
still → tilting → sorted → resolved, and unlike A the frame is populated from
the first content beat onward. *What accumulates:* the richest of the two,
and critically **mass-based** — more bricks, thicker courses, taller and
shorter columns, longer trail, wider strip. Mass survives thumbnail scale and
survives a dark palette, which opacity does not. *Payoff:* the crest stroke
is the better-motivated of the two, because it connects what is already on
screen rather than adding anything, and it lands on the script's own last
words. *Lazy test:* the sharpest formulation either pitch offers — every beat
owes a specific physical delta legible from a still with the text unreadable.
Its risk is congestion, which is a fixable authoring problem; A's risk is
invisibility, which is not. **Verdict: WINNER.**
