# PITCH B — `skills-for-the-ai-era-future_early-career-boost`

**Lens: accumulation-first.** The build-up was designed before the object was
named. Script is 504 words → expect ~195–210s of narration (~3:15–3:30) at the
pinned voice, so the marks below are stated in seconds AND as a share of
runtime; if the synth comes back shorter, slide the marks proportionally, do
not drop rungs.

**Fidelity note up front.** Every height, sink, band and label below is a
re-reading of a sentence the narration actually says. No number, axis, tick,
percentage or date appears anywhere on frame. Only two counts exist in this
concept — **four** categories and **six** transferable skills — and the voice
says both aloud. Only ONE column is ever labelled as falling (the
learn-to-code advice, which the script calls "much more ambiguous"); every
other sinking column stays blank, because the script names no other loser.

---

## The accumulation ladder

What is physically MORE, denser, re-sorted or newly connected at each mark.
The frame opens with a single horizon rule and nothing on it.

**By ~30s (≈15%) — the bed arrives, and the first things stand up.**
Gained: a dense low bed of small blank tiles laid flat along the rule (the
"tasks" AI is very good at), and then four hand-lettered tiles that stand
UPRIGHT out of that bed — judgment and decision-making, communication,
relationships, and the ability to keep learning. The frame has gone from one
line to a populated strip with two postures in it: lying down and standing up.
Nothing has moved twice; every tile arrived once and stayed.

**By ~60s (≈30%) — the strip gains a past, and its first unevenness.**
Gained: a second stratum, drawn in dashed ghost line BEHIND the live strip and
sitting lower — the "about ten years ago" layer — carrying one tall ghost
column for the learn-to-code advice. Gained on the live strip: more tiles, and
the first genuine height difference between neighbouring columns. Gained
below: the bed has visibly thickened by a course of tiles (automation creating
wealth, wages up on average). The picture now has depth it did not have at 30s
— a foreground strip and a buried history.

**By ~90s (≈45%) — the flat shelf becomes a slope, and a trail begins.**
Gained: the profile tilts. Some columns have lost bricks downward into the bed
(value driven down), others have gained bricks upward (value driven up), so
the strip reads as a jagged rising skyline instead of a shelf. Gained: a small
walking figure and, behind it, the first dotted footstep marks across the
column tops it has already stood on. The trail is cumulative — it only ever
gets longer, never redraws.

**By ~120s (≈60%) — the strip is partitioned, then re-sorted once.**
Gained: four translucent vertical bands drawn across the strip, one per spoken
category. Gained: each tile picks up a small corner notch for every band it
falls inside — a tile sitting in two bands carries two notches. Then the strip
performs its single re-arrangement of the whole runtime: the two-notch columns
slide together into a cluster ahead of the walker ("valuable today and
satisfies at least two"). This is the one time the field re-orders; everything
else in the video is addition.

**By ~150s (≈73%) — a second, portable object hangs off the first.**
Gained: a rack at the walker's side that fills, one hand-lettered tile at a
time, with the six transferable skills — using AI to get real work done,
taking care of yourself, personal effectiveness and agency, prioritization and
forecasting and decision-making, social skills, and learning how to learn. The
rack is pulled FROM the strip, so the strip does not lose them; they are copies
that travel. Once a rack tile lands it stays for the rest of the runtime.

**By ~180s (≈88%) — the strip outgrows the frame it started in.**
Gained: new columns drawn to the RIGHT of everything that existed at 60s, past
the walker, while the ghost stratum and the tall code column have receded to
the far left edge. Gained: the bed underneath is now several courses thick
across the whole width. The strip is physically wider and heavier than the one
that existed at the first mark — that is the "until then" of the automation
history line made visible.

**By ~200s / end (100%) — the crest line, drawn once.**
Gained: one continuous stroke traced across every column top, left edge to
right edge. It is the last mark added and it re-reads everything under it.

## The carrying object

**A left-to-right strip of stacked, hand-lettered skill tiles — a growing
tide-line whose column heights are the value the narration assigns them, walked
by one small figure, which at the last beat is traced into a single wave.**

Drawn as: a horizon rule across the lower third; above it, columns built of
small rounded rectangles, each about a fifth of a column wide, hand-lettered in
brand type only where the script names the skill and left blank where it does
not. Below the rule, the same rectangles lying flat in courses — the bed. The
past stratum is the identical vocabulary at 40% opacity with a dashed outline,
offset down and left. The walker is a simple stick-and-circle figure roughly
one column tall, so it reads as scale rather than character. No axis, no
gridline, no baseline number, no legend — the strip has a top edge and a
bottom edge and nothing else that a chart would have.

---

## Four frames

**25% (~50s) — "Two Strata, One Flat Shelf."**
A near-level shelf of columns, all similar height, four of them lettered and
standing proud of a thin bed. Behind and below, a dashed ghost layer with one
tall column carrying the old advice. The frame reads as archaeology: a present
and a past, both still.

**50% (~100s) — "The Shelf Has Tilted."**
No longer level. A jagged skyline climbing left to right, several columns
half-collapsed with their bricks visibly added to the thickened bed below, the
walker mid-strip with a short dotted trail behind it, and the first category
band lightly washed across. The frame reads as weather: things are moving
against each other.

**75% (~155s) — "Sorted, Notched, Carrying."**
The densest picture. Four bands fully drawn, tiles wearing one or two corner
notches, the two-notch columns clustered together ahead of the walker, and a
half-filled rack of lettered tiles slung at the walker's side. The frame reads
as inventory: everything has been counted, grouped and picked over.

**100% (~200s) — "The Whole Tide-Line, Crested."**
Pulled back so the entire strip fits at once. One stroke runs across every
column top and the silhouette is unmistakably a wave: ghost stratum in the
trough at the far left, the thick bed as the body of the water, the walker
standing at the crest with a full rack of six. The dotted trail behind it is
the route up the face.

Four different pictures: still → tilting → sorted → resolved. None of them is
another one with new headings.

---

## The payoff beat

**The final beat — "The goal is to ride the wave."** One continuous stroke is
drawn across the top of every column that has accumulated over the whole
runtime, and the accumulated silhouette resolves into the wave the script
names in its own last sentence. Nothing new is invented at the payoff; the
stroke only connects what is already on screen, which is exactly why it cannot
exist earlier — at 0:10 there is one horizon rule and no tops to connect, and
even at 50% the profile is too jagged and too short to read as one curve.

Secondary re-read at ~120s: the single re-sort into the two-notch cluster. It
is the only moment the field re-orders, so it lands as an event rather than as
churn.

---

## Why this is not lazy

A builder cannot satisfy this with heading swaps against a static prop,
because **every beat owes a specific physical delta on one object**, and each
delta is legible from a contact-sheet still with the text unreadable:

- **Brick count only goes up.** The bed thickens by a course at the wealth
  line, again at the "until then" line. A still from 30s and a still from 180s
  differ by visible mass, not by wording.
- **Column heights are load-bearing.** The tilt at ~90s, the sinking of
  unlabelled columns, and the crest line at the end are the same measurement
  read three times. Freeze the heights and the closing stroke has nothing to
  trace — the concept fails visibly, not quietly.
- **The trail is monotonic.** The dotted footsteps only ever get longer. Two
  stills can always be ordered by trail length alone, which is the shuffle
  test passed by construction.
- **The rack is a second object with its own fill state.** Empty, half, full —
  three distinct silhouettes hanging off the walker in the last third, where
  the thin rejected cut had nothing new to give.
- **The strip physically outgrows its own starting width.** New columns land
  to the right of everything that existed at 60s, so the frame at 180s is not
  a re-lettering of the frame at 60s; it is wider.
- **The one re-sort is a one-time event.** It happens once, at the four
  categories, and the arrangement it produces persists to the end — so a
  builder cannot pad by shuffling the field repeatedly, and the motion rule
  (settled content never re-animates) is satisfied by design: every tile
  arrives once, and only the one declared re-sort moves anything twice.

The cheapest legal pass this concept allows is still a strip that gains bricks,
tilts, grows a history layer, grows bands and notches, grows a trail, grows a
rack, grows past its own edge, and closes on a stroke that only the
accumulation makes drawable.
