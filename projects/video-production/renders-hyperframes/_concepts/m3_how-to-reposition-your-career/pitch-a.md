# PITCH A — m3_how-to-reposition-your-career

Lens: **metaphor-first**

## The carrying object

**A lit museum exhibit case — the same objects you already owned, moved onto the lit shelf, given written label cards, and topped with a re-lettered title strip.**

It is drawn as one wide glass rectangle that occupies most of the frame: a thin-rule border, a solid title strip running the full width across the top, three horizontal shelf lines inside it, and a dim holding shelf below the case floor where everything starts. The exhibits are flat geometric silhouettes — a stack of three bars, a ringed disc, a slab, a folded sheet — each sitting on a shelf line with a small blank rectangle (its label card) directly beneath it. Three states do all the work and are visible at a glance: an object is **dim on the holding shelf**, **lit on a shelf with a blank card**, or **lit with a written card**; the title strip is **blank**, **struck through**, or **lettered**. The case is the single hero illustration on every frame — nothing else is decorated.

## Four frames

**25% — SELECT.** The case is lit for the first time. A crowded dim holding shelf sits below, still full; four of its silhouettes have been lifted onto the top shelf, and one card under them is written out in full — "Led a multi-department initiative that reduced processing time by 25% and improved customer satisfaction" — while its neighbours' cards are still blank rectangles. The title strip reads "Operations & Strategy Leader." Beside the case, a second, completely empty case stands unlit and pushed half out of frame: the "start completely over" picture, drawn and set aside.

**50% — RELABEL.** Nothing moves shelves. The middle shelf's objects are the classroom ones, and they stay exactly where they are while their label cards are struck through and re-written underneath in the target field's words — "Designed and implemented multi-modal learning experiences for diverse learners" — and a row of small method chips (platforms, data analysis, collaborative design) lights along the shelf's front rail. The title strip is mid-change: half struck, half re-lettered to "Learning Experience Designer." The top shelf is now a settled block of written cards behind it; the empty case is gone from frame.

**75% — SURFACE FROM THE BACK.** The bottom shelf's small, previously overlooked items are pulled forward from the back of the shelf to the front rail — the moments already led informally — and get their own cards, one reading "Mentored 3 new analysts, accelerating their ramp-up time by 30%." All three shelves are now lit and written. The case *frame itself* takes its first annotation: four thin callouts pin to the four structural parts — the title strip above (direction), the shelf selection (curate), the label cards (translate), the front glass (public signals) — so the object is being read as a method, not just filled.

**100% — READ FROM OUTSIDE.** The camera crosses to the corridor side of the glass. We see the whole case front-on at once: three lit shelves, every card written, the four callouts resolved into the case's own architecture, and the title strip and front placard reading forward. The holding shelf is still there, still full, still visible through the glass — nothing was thrown out. A single sight-line runs from the oldest object on the bottom shelf up to the title strip: the through-line, now drawn.

## What accumulates

Three things, all countable off the picture: **lit objects** (holding shelf → shelves, and the holding shelf is never emptied), **written label cards** (blank rectangles → set copy, one per selected object, monotonically), and **lettering on the title strip and front placard** (blank → struck → set). Add the one structural gain: the case frame picks up its four callouts only in the last quarter. Nothing that has been lit ever goes dim again, and nothing that has been written is re-written after its beat — the case only ever gains.

## The payoff beat

The final beat, the crossing to the outside of the glass. Every earlier frame is drawn from the curator's side — reaching in, moving, writing. The last one is the visitor's view: the whole exhibit legible in one look, which is exactly the script's closing claim that the story "makes your next move feel obvious to others." That frame is undrawable at 0:10 — at 0:10 the shelves are empty, the cards are blank rectangles and the title strip has no letters, so there is literally nothing to read from outside.

## Why this is not lazy

Each quarter is a *different verb performed on the same object* — select, relabel-in-place, surface-from-the-back, read-from-outside — so no two frames are the same picture with a new heading, and the shuffle test resolves on object state alone (how many cards are written, how many strips are lettered) without reading a word of copy. A builder cannot satisfy this with a static prop: the milestone contract is the case's state, not its headings — holding shelf full and one shelf lit at 25%, a shelf whose objects do not move while its cards change at 50%, all three shelves lit plus the four frame callouts at 75%, exterior view at 100%. The 50% frame is the concept's own anti-laziness clause: it forbids adding anything, which is also the lesson's point — the classroom did not disappear, only its label changed. And every string on screen is quoted from the narration; the case introduces no counts, steps, or claims the script does not make.
