# PITCH B — m3_how-to-reposition-your-career

**Lens: accumulation-first.** The build-up was designed before the object was named.
Script: 531 words → estimated runtime **~3:40 (≈220s)**. Quarter marks land at
**0:55 / 1:50 / 2:45 / 3:40**, which by word position fall inside Alex's three
moves, inside Jordan's translation, on "Clarify your future direction", and on
the close — four different zones of the script, so four different pictures come
for free.

---

## The accumulation ladder

What the frame has physically GAINED at each rung. Nothing on this ladder is
ever taken away — the script's own guardrail is "you're not erasing your past",
so the concept is built so that removal is *impossible*.

**By 0:45** — the surface is lit, and one complete record lies on it.
The frame opened on a sealed, blank roll (the "start completely over" picture:
new degree, new industry, new identity). That blank roll is set down to one side
and never picked up again. In its place a full strip is laid out end to end,
already exposed, every frame on it filled. **Gained: light, and one whole fixed
record that arrived complete.** From here the record itself never changes — only
what is read off it and made from it accumulates.

**By 1:30** — the record has gained readings, and the air above it has gained an
output. Grease-pencil rings sit on scattered frames of Alex's strip (the
cross-functional projects, the redesigned systems, the shaped decisions the
script names). Above the table, a line that was bare now carries its first
hanging print. **Gained: marks on a record that did not move, plus the first
thing anyone outside the room can actually see.**

**By 2:15** — the surface has gained a second complete record, and the line a
second print. Jordan's strip lies parallel to Alex's, its own rings on its own
frames. Critically, Alex's unringed frames are still lit and still there — the
script is explicit that the classroom "didn't disappear". **Gained: density on
the table (two full-length records where there was one) and a second output
above it.**

**By 3:00** — three records fill the table edge to edge; three prints hang in a
row. Priya's strip completes the bed. The table is now full: there is no more
room for a fourth, and the eye reads the three as one block rather than three
items. **Gained: the surface is saturated — this is the most material the frame
will ever hold.**

**By 3:20** — the three separate records become one connected thing. A band
draws left-to-right *across* all three strips at once: aim, mark, grade, print —
the four operations the script names as clarify / curate / translate / update.
Drawn once, touching all three. **Gained: connection. Three records that were
side by side are now demonstrably the same procedure run three times.**

**By 3:40** — the outputs multiply and the record is re-read.
One print rises off the line and enlarges until it dominates the frame, and the
strip that made it stays lit underneath, whole and unmarked-up. Along the bottom
rail the same image now exists in the four formats the script names — resume,
LinkedIn headline, summary, 60-second career story. **Gained: four public
outputs from one unchanged record.**

## The carrying object

**A lit photographer's light table that gains one full negative strip per
professional — fixed, un-reorderable records — with a drying line above it that
gains the prints pulled from them.**

It is drawn as flat brand vector, not photo-realism: the table is one large
rounded slab of pale glow filling the lower two-thirds of the canvas; each strip
is a charcoal band with sprocket perforations along both edges and a run of
lighter windows inside it, laid perfectly horizontal so three of them stack into
a legible bed. Grease-pencil rings are single-stroke brand-accent ovals dropped
onto individual windows — a handful, never numbered on screen, never labelled
with a count the script does not make. The drying line is one thin rule across
the upper third with small clips; prints are clean white cards, and **all
readable copy in the video lives on those cards** (headings Title Case, the
quoted bullets and summaries in sentence case exactly as the script writes
them), so nothing on the negative strips is ever asked to carry small type. The
negative windows carry shape only.

## Four frames

**25% — 0:55 (inside Alex's three moves).**
One horizontal band. A single lit strip runs the full width of a glowing table,
a few accent rings on scattered windows, the enlarger head angled down at it
from the upper left, the drying line above still bare except for one empty clip.
The blank sealed roll sits in the lower corner, closed. Composition: one
dominant horizontal, mostly empty air above it. The frame reads as *a record
being read*.

**50% — 1:50 (inside Jordan's translation).**
Two registers now compete. Two strips lie parallel on the table, both ringed;
two prints hang above with their copy facing the viewer, and the enlarger head
has swung to the second strip. The picture has split into a lower bed and an
upper row, and the eye travels vertically between them for the first time. The
frame reads as *a record being turned into something others hold*.

**75% — 2:45 (on "Clarify your future direction").**
The table is full: three parallel strips, edge to edge, three prints above them.
The rings across all three strips are visible at once as a scatter, and the
first stroke of the four-operation band is starting to draw across the bed,
left to right. Composition: a dense filled rectangle under a populated line —
the busiest frame in the video. The frame reads as *three separate stories that
are about to be shown to be one procedure*.

**100% — 3:40 (the close).**
Inverted composition. One print, enlarged, occupies the centre and upper half of
the canvas — it is now the biggest object in the video, where at 0:55 the
biggest object was a strip. Beneath it the three strips remain lit and complete,
compressed into a stack. Along the bottom rail the same image sits in four
formats. Nothing horizontal dominates any more; the frame is top-weighted. The
frame reads as *the thing other people see, standing on a record that never
changed*.

Four different pictures: one horizontal band → two registers → a saturated bed →
a top-weighted enlargement over a compressed bed.

## The payoff beat

**~3:25, on "revealing the through-line in what you've already done."**

The enlarged print is laid back down flat against the strips it came from, and
every mark on the print lines up with a window that was already lit at 0:45.
The re-read is the sentence *you cannot print what is not on the negative* —
which is the script's own guardrail, "Alex didn't claim a title that wasn't
earned — just surfaced the strategic work already inside those operations
roles." The closing frame could not have been drawn at 0:10: at 0:10 there is a
sealed blank roll and an unlit table; at 3:25 there are three complete records,
a row of prints, a connecting band, and one enlargement that provably contains
nothing new.

## Why this is not lazy

**The record is fixed, and that is the whole point.** Every other carrier in
this batch moves its material — scraps re-sort into columns, bricks re-lay into
a doorway, a field is re-grouped. A negative strip physically *cannot* be
re-ordered: the frames are welded into their sequence in the order they
happened. So this concept cannot cheat by shuffling. The only things that can
accumulate are readings (rings), outputs (prints), and connection (the band) —
which is exactly the script's claim that you change the telling, not the past.
It also visually forbids the failure mode the script warns about: you cannot add
a frame to a strip, so no unearned title can appear.

**It fails the heading-swap test on purpose.** A builder cannot satisfy this
with new headings on a static prop, because the prop changes state five times in
ways headings can't fake: the table goes from unlit to lit, gains a strip, gains
a second, gains a third until saturated, gains a spanning band, and finally
gives up an enlargement that re-composes the whole canvas. The drying line goes
from bare to one to two to three to four formats. Three sheets pulled from any
point in the runtime can be ordered by counting strips and prints alone — the
shuffle test passes by construction.

**It is not the thin cut.** The rejected thin cut was a six-dot circle on empty
frames. Here the object owns the full canvas from 0:45 (the table is the floor
of every frame) and gains detail continuously: sprocket edges, window runs,
rings, clips, prints, the band. And it is not the slow cut either — nothing
churns. The strips, once laid, never move again for the rest of the video;
change is always addition, never re-animation of settled content.

**Nothing is invented.** Every word printed on a card is the script's own:
Alex's rewritten bullet with its 25%, "Operations & Strategy Leader", Jordan's
multi-modal learning experiences line, "Learning Experience Designer", Priya's
"Mentored 3 new analysts…" with its 30%, her emerging-people-leader summary, and
the four public signals the script names by name. The four-operation band uses
the script's four verbs verbatim. No count, sequence claim or fact appears that
the narration does not say.
