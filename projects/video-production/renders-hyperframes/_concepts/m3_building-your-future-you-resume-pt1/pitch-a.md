# PITCH A — m3_building-your-future-you-resume

**Lens: metaphor-first.**

## The carrying object

**A museum display case, standing empty beneath a crammed archive shelf, that gets curated and lit one bay at a time.**

Drawn flat and orthographic, not in perspective: the case is a wide rounded-rect outline in SCLA ink occupying the lower two-thirds of the frame, ruled into bays by thin vertical mullions, with a blank title plate (a wide, empty rounded bar) mounted above it. Behind and above sits the archive: four to five horizontal shelf rules, each crammed edge to edge with **box spines** — flat vertical bars of varying width, uniform fill, closed, each carrying one short label. Three drawn states do all the work and must stay visually distinct: a **closed spine** (solid narrow bar, label only), an **opened exhibit** (the bar rotated flat into a bay and expanded into a visible interior — a small object silhouette plus its plate), and an **engraved plate** (a rounded bar that fills with ruled lines as narration names what goes on it). Light is one pale wedge per lit bay, dropped from the case's top edge — never a glow, always a hard-edged triangle.

## Four frames

- **25% — "the archive that works against us."** The case is empty, unlit, its title plate blank. The archive wall above is at maximum density: spines packed across every shelf, nothing readable but a task verb per spine. Three spines are pulled proud of the rank, one per culprit the narration names — one whose label is a bare activity, one drawn in a visibly older/heavier label style than its neighbours, one sitting with no line of any kind reaching the case below it. The picture's weight is entirely top-of-frame; the case is dead space, and that is the point.
- **50% — "curating, not documenting."** The title plate above the case is filled for the first time with the direction the lesson names, and the case's glass goes light. The archive wall drops to a faint outline, its density inverted. One spine has been lifted down and opened into the leftmost bay — the first time anything in the frame has an *interior*: an object silhouette instead of a closed bar, sitting beside a still-blank plate. The remaining bays stand open and empty with blank plates, so the frame reads as a case mid-curation, not a finished one.
- **75% — "measured against the north star."** A statement panel is fixed across the top of the case and a single plumb rule drops from it through every bay, so each bay is now visibly *checked against* something. The bays the narration's own "three to five" allows are open and occupied; their plates are mid-engraving, filling line by line as responsibility, action, measurable outcome and scope are named — some plates fuller than others. Spines that don't serve the direction are pushed back flush into the wall and greyed to outline. The frame's centre of gravity has moved from the shelf to the case.
- **100% — "the case, read in one look."** Full light across every bay at once. Every plate is engraved to its last line, the fullest one carrying the rewritten example the narration reads aloud. The title plate above names the role. The archive wall survives only as a faint ruled ghost behind — visibly the source, no longer the subject — and the case reads left to right as one continuous exhibit rather than a row of separate items.

## What accumulates

Closed identical spines become opened, plated, individually lit exhibits. Three things move in one direction and never move back: the **plates** go blank → partially ruled → fully engraved; the **title plate** goes blank → named, which retroactively changes what every bay below it means; and the **density inverts** — the archive starts crammed and dominant and ends a faint outline, while the case starts empty and ends full and lit. A viewer handed the four frames shuffled can restore their order from the plate fill alone.

## The payoff beat

The final beat: the light comes up across the whole case at once and, for the first and only time, the case is readable as a **single exhibit under one title** — every engraved plate legible in one view, with the archive wall still ghosted behind it as the thing it was all drawn from. That frame is impossible at 0:10, where there is no title, no interior, no plate and no light.

## Why this is not lazy

A static prop with swapped headings cannot produce these frames, because the prop itself is in a different *state* at every one of them. Each bay carries its own independent lifecycle — closed spine, lifted, opened, plated, engraved, lit — and those transitions are staggered across the runtime rather than shared, so no two beats can reuse the same picture. The plates fill incrementally with named lines, so even neighbouring beats differ in ruled-line count. The backdrop's density inverts once, irreversibly, which no heading swap can fake. And the title plate's arrival is a single one-time event that re-reads everything already on screen. A builder who leaves the case unchanged and only edits text will fail the milestone frames on sight.

**Fidelity note:** the case shows only what the narration says. Counts come from the script alone — the three culprits, the "three to five" experiences, and the example bullet's own figures, which appear only on the beat that speaks them. No file/part number reaches the frame or the voice.
