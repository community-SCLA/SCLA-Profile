# CONCEPT — m3_building-your-future-you-resume

**Judged:** 2026-08-05, concept-judge lane (`/render-lessons` B2). Graded against
the two APPROVED rows of `design-system/docs/taste.md` only. The 2026-08-05
`career-building-is-a-repeatable-process` cut is SHIPPED-NOT-A-REFERENCE and was
not used as an anchor.

**Assigned carrier family (binding):** darkroom / light table — contact frames
edited down into large prints.

---

## Concept angle

A darkroom light table, seen top-down, on which a whole career's worth of tiny
illegible contact frames is edited down **in place** into three-to-five large,
captioned prints all squared to one card at the head of the table — the table is
on screen from the first content beat to the final frame, roughly **95% of
runtime** (every beat after the title card).

---

## The carrying object

**A lit working surface, drawn flat and top-down (plan view), filling ~85–90% of
the canvas.** It is a *horizontal working surface*, never a wall and never a
vitrine: things lie ON it, viewed from above. Its own faint etched grid rules the
lit panel; a thin ink bezel closes it. Seven drawn parts, and nothing else:

1. **Contact frames** — dim rounded rectangles at contact size (~1/40 of frame
   width), laid in strips of six with a pair of tiny sprocket notches on one
   short edge so they read as film. Each holds one illegible blob silhouette.
   **No text at contact size** — illegibility is the point, so that enlargement
   is a real information gain rather than a zoom.
2. **Wax-pencil marks** — freehand accent-colour crosses and rings, drawn onto
   frames already on the table. Drawn once, never re-drawn, never animated after
   they land.
3. **An enlarged print** — the *same* rectangle at ~4.5×, now carrying readable
   interior art: a seated figure at a desk, a bar that rises, a cluster of
   figures. An enlargement is one-way; a print never shrinks back.
4. **A caption plate** — a rounded bar directly beneath each print that fills
   with ruled lines, **one line per element the narration names** (responsibility,
   action, measurable outcome, and scope). Blank → part-ruled → full. This is the
   fine-grain accumulation register: neighbouring beats differ by ruled-line count.
5. **The sleeve stack** — closed thin sleeves piling at the LEFT margin, holding
   strips retired off the field. It only ever grows.
6. **The card mount and the registration rule** — a shallow bracket at the HEAD
   (top edge) of the table, empty for the first ~70% of runtime. When the card
   seats, a single hairline straight edge is etched out from it across the
   surface. A ruled straight edge on a table — not a thread, not a track, and it
   carries no nodes.
7. **The developing tray** — a shallow pan at the NEAR (bottom) edge, appearing
   once and late, holding exactly one print: the re-made bullet.

**Backdrop law (one-way, irreversible):** the field starts crammed and dominant
and ends a thin stack at one margin. Density falls monotonically; legibility
rises monotonically. Neither ever reverses.

## Milestone frames — ACCUMULATION CONTRACT

The builder owes these four pictures. A build whose 25% and 75% frames could be
swapped without anyone noticing has failed this contract, not merely
under-decorated it. (Marks are percentages of final runtime; the builder pins the
exact seconds from the synthesized duration — the script is ~630 spoken words, so
expect roughly four minutes at the pinned voice.)

- **25% — the archive that works against us.** The table is packed edge to edge
  with strips of small, dim, near-identical frames, all at slightly different
  angles, squared to nothing. Three frames have been pulled proud of their rank,
  one per culprit the narration names, each showing its own defect: one wax-crossed
  for showing activity and no result, one visibly older and heavier in style than
  its neighbours, one sitting with no line of any kind reaching the head of the
  table. The mount at the head is bare. *Reading: cluttered, uniform, dim, nothing
  large, nothing aligned. The weight of the frame is the mess.*
- **50% — curating, not documenting.** The field has been swept into a stacked
  sleeve pile occupying the left third. The right two-thirds is clear lit surface
  carrying exactly ONE enlargement — the first legible thing in the entire video —
  with its caption plate below it still blank. The mount is still empty. *Reading:
  one big legible object against cleared space; the compositional inverse of the
  25% frame.*
- **75% — measured against the north star.** The card is seated in the mount, its
  registration rule runs across the surface, and three-to-five large prints stand
  squared to it. Their caption plates are mid-engraving and **unevenly so** — some
  carry two ruled lines, one carries all four. Two dim strips are mid-slide into
  the sleeves. *Reading: ordered, aligned, mid-density, visibly mid-edit.*
- **100% — the surface read as one document.** The card at the head, one aligned
  block of prints beneath it, every caption plate engraved to its last line, one
  print visibly re-made at full legibility (the script's own before/after
  sentence), the tray idle at the near edge, the archive surviving only as a thin
  sleeve stack at the far margin. *Reading: sparse in count, heaviest in ink,
  obviously a finished document rather than a pile.*

## What accumulates

Four registers move in one direction and never move back:

- **Legibility.** Illegible blobs → one readable print → several → a full block.
  This is detail gained, not position moved — the exact axis the thin rejected cut
  had nothing on.
- **Caption ink.** Plates go blank → part-ruled → fully engraved. Because lines
  land one at a time as the narration names them, no two adjacent beats hold the
  same plate state.
- **The sleeve stack.** Monotonic growth at the left margin. It is the physical
  record of every de-emphasis decision, and it can never shrink.
- **Alignment.** Nothing on the table is squared to anything until the card seats;
  afterwards everything is. This is a property of the *whole surface* that flips
  exactly once.

Element **count** rises then falls; total **ink** rises throughout. The 0:10 frame
(many tiny identical things, no head, no alignment) and the closing frame (a few
large legible things under one card) cannot be mistaken for each other, and any two
contact sheets can be put back in order from density and legibility alone.

---

## The payoff beat

**The registration event, at ~72–76% of runtime — the beat where the narration
names the Next Move Statement as the north star.** The card seats into the empty
mount, a hairline rule etches out from its edge across the whole surface, and
**every print already on the table rotates once to square against it.** Nothing
new arrives; everything already on screen gains a relationship it did not have.
That single move answers the script's third and heaviest culprit — no through-line
from history to readiness — in the picture rather than in the heading, and it is
what makes the closing frame re-readable as one document instead of a group of
items. The closing frame is undrawable at 0:10: there is nothing legible on the
table and no head to square to.

## Grafted from the runner-up

Three devices lifted from Pitch A into the light-table family:

1. **The caption plate that engraves line by line** (A's "plate"). Pitch B gained
   interior *bands* on each print; A's incrementally-ruled plate is finer-grained
   and gives every beat in the 50–100% stretch a guaranteed visible delta. Adopted
   wholesale — this is the graft that most improves the winner.
2. **The three culprits pulled proud of the rank** at 25%, each carrying its own
   distinct visible defect rather than a shared highlight. A's version was sharper
   than B's caption-beside-frame treatment.
3. **A's stated density-inversion law** — the backdrop inverts once and
   irreversibly — promoted here into the explicit "backdrop law" above so the
   builder treats it as a contract, not an atmosphere note.

Pitch A's display case, title plate, bays, mullions, plumb rule and lighting
wedges are **not** grafted: the case is another lesson's assigned carrier, and
lighting-as-accumulation is the weakest axis in the bracket (it is a highlight
moving, which is what the thin cut was rejected for).

## Batch-diversity note

- **Not the museum display case** (sibling): that is a front elevation of an
  enclosed vitrine with bays and a title plate. This is a top-down plan view of an
  open horizontal working surface with no enclosure, no bays and no mullions.
- **Not the gallery wall with a hanging rail** (sibling): nothing here hangs, and
  there is no rail. Objects lie flat, are slid, stacked and rotated in plane.
- **Not the lighthouse lens** (sibling): the table's light is a flat even ground,
  never a beam, never a source that sweeps. **No light wedges, no glow, no
  spotlight anywhere in this build** — brightness never carries meaning here;
  legibility and ink do.
- **Not a scrap field feeding a statement strip** (taken elsewhere): material never
  travels into the card. Material moves *field → enlarged in place* or *field →
  sleeve stack at the margin*. The card is a governing reference at the head that
  *aligns* what is already down; it receives nothing.
- **Not a peg-and-thread board, career track, or checkpoint node** (taken
  elsewhere): the registration rule is a single hairline straight edge etched on
  the surface, carrying no nodes, no pegs, no stations, and never branching.
- **No brick, skyline, ruled worksheet, trail marker, plant, tree or truss.**
- **No timeline of any kind, and no years or dates** — the script argues *away*
  from a resume as timeline, and dates would be fabricated besides.

## Judge's note

**Likeliest failure mode: the 50–75% stretch collapsing into "enlarge another
print, enlarge another print" — three consecutive beats that read as one picture
with new headings.** This is where a builder will meet pace pressure with
repetition, and it is exactly what the shuffle test and `twin-share` catch late
and expensively.

**The guard, stated as a build rule:** *no two consecutive beats may change the
same single register.* The five registers are (a) a new print enlarged, (b) a
caption plate gaining a named line, (c) the sleeve stack gaining a sleeve as
strips leave the field, (d) a wax mark landing, (e) the card / registration rule /
tray. Every beat changes at least one; consecutive beats must change *different*
ones. A beat that only adds a print is legal exactly once in a row.

**Secondary guard — the 50% frame must not read as empty.** Cleared surface is
never more than ~40% of the canvas: the sleeve stack and remaining strips hold the
left third dense, and the table's own etched grid carries the cleared area so the
ink gate sees a lit surface rather than background.

**Motion guard.** The registration rotation is a one-time, one-way transform on a
declared beat. A print that has registered never moves again — no settle, no
drift, no re-mark. Same for wax marks and ruled caption lines: they land and hold.
(`check_motion.py` bans in-place keep-alive; this concept must not give it a
reason to fire.)

**Fidelity guard.** Everything countable on screen is a count the script speaks:
three culprits; three to five experiences; the four named elements on a caption
plate; promotion, lateral move, or role redesign; 6 to 12 months; and the example
bullet's own team of 6 / 30% / 20%, which appear only on the beat that speaks
them. Figure clusters elsewhere are drawn unnumbered. The `-pt1` suffix reaches
neither the frame nor the narration.

## Scores

**Pitch A — museum display case.** Names a genuinely drawable object and defends
it well: the case, its bays, the box-spine archive and the three drawn states are
concrete enough that two builders would draw the same thing, and the
blank→ruled→engraved plate is the single best accumulation device either pitch
produced. It answers Q1, Q3 and Q5 strongly. It is weaker on Q2 and Q4: its 75%
and 100% frames differ mainly by *how lit* the case is and how full the plates
are, and its named payoff is "the light comes up across the whole case" — a
brightness change, which is the closest thing in either pitch to the highlight-
moves failure the thin cut was rejected for. Its plumb-rule-from-a-statement-panel
also edges toward two carriers already claimed in this batch. Decisive, however, is
that the display case is another M3 lesson's assigned carrier and is forbidden
here. **Verdict: RUNNER-UP — best device grafted (the engraving caption plate,
the three proud culprits, the density-inversion law), carrier retired.**

**Pitch B — photographer's light table.** Designed accumulation-first, and it
shows: it is the only one of the two whose four frames are *compositionally
inverted* from each other rather than progressively fuller — packed-and-uniform,
then one-large-thing-against-clear-space, then aligned-and-mid-density, then a
sparse heavy block. That is a clean pass on Q2, and its density-falls /
legibility-rises law makes the shuffle test pass by construction (Q3). Its payoff
(Q4) is the strongest element in either pitch: the registration event changes the
meaning of every object already on screen without adding one, which is precisely
the taste doc's "the field re-sorted into its final grouping" and mirrors the
approved `build-direction` cut's signature. Q1 is a pass — table, strips, sleeves,
mount, tray are all nameable — and Q5 is the strongest of the two, since element
count itself rises and falls, which no heading swap can fake. Its real weakness is
a repetitive middle stretch, addressed by the register rule in the judge's note,
and thin per-beat granularity, addressed by grafting A's caption plate. It is also
natively inside the assigned darkroom / light-table family. **Verdict: WINNER —
build this, with the three grafts and the register rule binding.**
