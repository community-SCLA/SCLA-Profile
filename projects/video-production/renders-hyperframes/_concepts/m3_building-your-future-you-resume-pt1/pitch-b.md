## The accumulation ladder

What the surface has GAINED at each mark — decided before any object was named.

| Mark | What is physically more / denser / re-sorted / newly connected |
| --- | --- |
| **0–30s** | **Count.** The surface starts nearly bare and fills edge to edge with small, near-identical, dim rectangles arriving in long strips. Gain = sheer density and uniformity. Nothing is labelled, nothing is bigger than anything else. |
| **30–60s** | **A second layer over the same stuff.** No new rectangles — the surface gains an annotation layer: wax-pencil crosses on the ones that show motion but no result. Gain = marks on top of what is already there (the archive is not re-drawn, it is re-read). |
| **60–90s** | **Visible unevenness.** Gains faded/wrong-gauge strips that don't match their neighbours (culprit two), and gains the *absence* of a line — every rectangle sits at its own slight angle, squared to nothing. Gain = the eye can now see the mess as a mess. |
| **90–120s** | **A cleared field and an empty mount.** The clutter is pushed into stacked sleeves at the left margin; the surface gains open working space and a bare holder at its head. First true re-sort: same material, radically different distribution. |
| **120–150s** | **Scale and internal detail.** The first rectangle is enlarged in place to ~4× and, for the first time in the video, has *contents you can read* — a figure, a rising bar, a group. Gain = detail, not position. Then a second enlargement. |
| **150–180s** | **Parts inside the parts.** Each large print gains labelled internal bands, and dim rectangles slide off the working field into the sleeve stack. Gain = interior structure, plus a shrinking archive. |
| **180–210s** | **A connection that reaches everything already on screen.** A card drops into the empty mount at the head; a registration line runs from its edge across the whole surface and every print already placed rotates once to square to it. Gain = alignment — the newest element changes the meaning of the oldest. |
| **210–254s** | **Resolution and finality.** One print is re-made from the same small rectangle at full legibility (the script's own before/after sentence), two more come back sharper from the tray at the surface's edge, and the finished set closes into one aligned block under one heading. Gain = the surface can be read as a single document. |

At 0:10 the frame is many tiny identical things. At 4:10 it is a few large legible things, aligned, under one card, with the archive reduced to a thin stack at the margin. Net ink is *higher*, net element count is *lower*, and neither state can be mistaken for the other.

## The carrying object

**A photographer's light table: a lit working surface where a whole career's worth of tiny contact frames is edited down, in place, into a few large prints.**

Drawn flat and brand-native — one glowing panel filling the canvas, not a prop in a corner. The small frames are dim rounded rectangles in strips, deliberately illegible at contact size (a silhouette-blob, no text). An enlarged print is the *same* rectangle scaled up and given readable interior art: a seated figure, a bar that rises, a cluster of people, a labelled band. The only other elements are wax-pencil marks (accent stroke), a sleeve stack that grows at the left margin, a card mount at the head of the table, and a small developing tray at the near edge that appears once, late. Every enlargement is one-way and holds — nothing wobbles, pulses or re-marks after it settles.

## Four frames

**~63s (25%) — "overloaded with tasks but light on impact."**
The table is packed edge to edge with strips of small, dim, near-identical frames. Wax-pencil crosses have started landing on the ones that show activity only; the script's own examples sit as small captions beside three of them. The head of the table is bare. Reading: *cluttered, uniform, grey, nothing large, nothing aligned.*

**~127s (50%) — "curating evidence, not documenting everything."**
The archive has been swept into a stacked sleeve pile occupying the left third. The right two-thirds is clear lit surface with an empty mount at its head and exactly ONE enlargement sitting alone on it — the first thing in the video you can actually read. Reading: *one big legible object against clear space; the opposite composition to frame 1.*

**~190s (75%) — "does this help make the case?"**
Three to five large prints stand in the working field, each squared to the card now seated in the mount, each carrying its labelled interior bands (responsibility, action, measurable outcome, scope — the script's four). A registration line runs from the card's edge through all of them. Two dim frames are mid-slide back into the sleeves. Reading: *ordered, aligned, mid-density, clearly mid-edit.*

**~254s (100%) — the finished surface.**
The card at the head, one aligned block of large prints beneath it, the whole archive reduced to a thin sleeve stack at the far margin, the tray idle. The heading sits above the block. Reading: *sparse in count, heavy in ink, obviously a finished document rather than a pile.*

## The payoff beat

**~180–190s, the registration event — and its echo in the closing frame.** When the Next Move Statement card seats into the mount, a line runs out from it and every print already on the table turns once to square against it. Nothing new arrives; everything already on screen gains a relationship it did not have. That single move *is* the script's "no through-line" culprit being answered, and it is what makes the last frame re-readable as one document instead of a group of items. The closing frame could not have been drawn at 0:10 — at 0:10 there is not one legible thing on the table and no head to square to.

## Why this is not lazy

- **It cannot be built with heading swaps against a static prop.** The prop is destroyed and rebuilt: element count rises then falls, every enlargement is irreversible, the sleeve stack only grows, and the 180s registration event mutates every element already placed. A builder who left the table static would be visibly wrong at four separate milestones, not just under-decorated.
- **It gains detail, not position** — the exact failure of the rejected thin cut. The small frames are *drawn illegible on purpose* so that enlargement is a real information gain: the same rectangle acquires interior art and labelled bands. "The highlight moves around" is structurally impossible here, because the highlight leaves a permanent enlarged object behind.
- **It supports a high idea-rate with low churn** — the approved-cut signature. One surface, established in the first 20s, then only ever added to or re-sorted: a strip arrives, a cross lands, a sleeve fills, a print grows, a band labels, the line runs. Each is a ~5–7s increment on an object that never gets replaced, so pace comes from the object earning more, never from swapping pictures.
- **It is a re-reading of the narration, not an addition to it.** Every count on screen is one the script speaks (three culprits; three to five experiences; responsibility/action/measurable outcome/scope; the team-of-6 / 30% / 20% example sentence verbatim; promotion, lateral move, or role redesign; 6 to 12 months). Impacted-people clusters are drawn **unnumbered** because the script gives no number. **No years, no dates, no timeline ticks anywhere on the surface** — the script explicitly moves away from a timeline, and inventing dates would be a fabricated sequence claim. The part number appears nowhere on frame or in narration.
- **The shuffle test passes by construction.** Density falls monotonically, legibility rises monotonically, and the sleeve stack only ever grows — any two contact sheets can be put back in order from the pictures alone.
