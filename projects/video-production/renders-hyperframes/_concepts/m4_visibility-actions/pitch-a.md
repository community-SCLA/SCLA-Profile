# PITCH A — m4_visibility-actions (metaphor-first lens)

## The carrying object

**A potter's kiln shelf holding five finished-but-unglazed pieces** — the work
you have already made, sitting in plain sight and completely unreadable.

It is drawn as one wide horizontal slab (a flat rectangle on two short posts)
spanning most of the canvas, with five deliberately different silhouettes
standing on it, each built from simple geometry: a wide bowl (half-ellipse +
rim ellipse), a straight-sided mug (rounded rect + arc handle), a tall bottle
(trapezoid body, narrow neck, ellipse lip), a plate stood on edge (circle on a
small triangular stand), and a low lidded jar (rounded rect + dome). Every
piece starts in the same flat neutral with a dry chalk-edge stroke and **no
highlight and no cast shadow**, so at a glance the whole row collapses into one
gray block — that flatness is the lesson's problem, drawn. The only other
objects are a glaze tub at the shelf's right edge (a cylinder with a lid) and,
later, the kiln: a dark rectangular chamber with a hinged door and three
horizontal coil lines. Glaze is drawn as a clipped color fill (brand palette
from `tokens.yml`) with a wavy lower edge and two or three teardrop drips;
"fired" is one white sliver highlight arc plus a pooled dark edge where the
glaze ran thick.

## Four frames

**25% — The shelf as it stands.** Wide, straight-on: shelf edge to edge, five
silhouettes, every one the same matte neutral, no highlights anywhere. The
glaze tub is still lidded and the brush is dry on the bench. Nothing in the
picture distinguishes the best piece from the plainest one. (Reads the opening:
work that exists, and the right people cannot see it.)

**50% — Five surface acts.** Pushed in close on the row: the tub is open and
each of the five pieces now carries a visibly *different* surface act — one
dipped to the shoulder (flat color to a straight waterline, three drips below
it), one poured with a wide diagonal stripe, one brushed with a single band
around the rim, one carrying a bare resist stripe cut through its color, one
dipped only around its foot. The glaze is thin enough that the thrown ridges
and a thumb-dent still show through every one. Five acts for the script's five
examples — no more, no fewer.

**75% — Three forward, one loaded.** The camera steps back and the frame now
holds **two places**, which frame 1 did not: the shelf on the left with three
of the five pieces moved out to its front lip, and on the right the kiln with
its door swung open — dark chamber, three coil lines, a small shelf on three
posts — with exactly ONE of the three being set inside. The two that stay
behind sit dry and waiting. Dried glaze splashes have collected along the
shelf's front edge.

**100% — The shelf, re-read.** The exact framing of frame 1 returns: same
shelf, same five forms, same positions. One piece now carries a gloss highlight
arc, a pooled dark rim where the glaze ran thick, and a bare unglazed foot ring
where it stood in the fire. The other four are still flat. The row no longer
reads as one gray block — your eye lands on the fired piece from across the
room, and its form is identical to what it was at 0:10.

## What accumulates

Four things, all permanent, none of them a moving highlight:

1. **Treated pieces:** 0 → 5. Surface, never form — no piece is ever replaced,
   re-thrown, or made better.
2. **Glaze splashes on the shelf's front lip:** one small mark per act, and
   they never wipe off. Any later frame carries the physical record of every
   earlier one, so the contact sheet can be re-ordered from the pictures alone.
3. **Re-grouping:** five on the shelf → three at the front lip → one in the
   chamber. The set is re-arranged, not enlarged.
4. **One irreversible state change:** matte → fired. That single change is the
   only thing in the whole runtime that cannot be undone, and it lands last.

## The payoff beat

The closing beat, on "easier to see — and easier for others to support": the
frame returns to the opening composition exactly, and the row **re-reads**. One
piece holds the light; its thumb-dent is still visible under the glaze; its
foot ring is bare where it touched the shelf. The final picture is literally
the first picture with one surface changed — the entire argument is the
difference between two shots the viewer has already seen. This frame could not
have been drawn at 0:10: at 0:10 there is no gloss, no drip, no splash on the
lip, and no bare foot ring anywhere on the shelf.

## Why this is not lazy

- **Heading swaps against a static prop cannot fake it.** Each milestone
  demands a materially different drawing — a highlight-free bisque row; five
  distinct drip/pour/resist geometries; an open kiln chamber with posts and a
  piece inside it; a fired surface with gloss arc, pooled rim and bare foot. A
  builder who reuses one shelf drawing and changes the heading produces four
  identical pictures and fails the shuffle test on sight.
- **It answers "boring" with substance, not churn.** The shelf arrives whole in
  the first beats and is then only ever re-read — the shape of both APPROVED
  reference cuts. Nothing wobbles, drifts or re-marks: every addition is a
  permanent mark on settled content, so `check_motion.py` has nothing to catch.
- **The carrier fills the canvas.** The shelf spans the frame at every
  milestone; there is no small prop marooned in empty space, which is what sank
  the thin-carrier rejection.
- **It reads the narration and adds nothing.** Five treatments = the script's
  five examples. Three at the front lip = "design three visibility actions."
  One in the chamber = "choose one to test in the next 30 days." The return to
  the opening framing = "reflect on what you learn." No counts, no sequence
  claims, no facts the script does not speak. On-frame copy stays the script's
  own words.
- **The metaphor is the lesson, not decoration.** Glaze changes no pot's form,
  only whether it catches light and whether anyone else can pick it up and use
  it — which is exactly "visibility isn't about being perfect," and exactly
  "easier for others to support." The pieces stay imperfect on purpose: the
  thumb-dent survives the fire.
- **Batch-distinct.** Nothing here is a wall, a sheet, a board, a track, a
  plant, a span, a light table, a hung canvas, a lens or a core sample; the
  mechanism is surface state on one shelf, not storing, hanging, or stacking.
