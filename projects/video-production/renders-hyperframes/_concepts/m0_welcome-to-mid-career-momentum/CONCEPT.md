# CONCEPT — m0_welcome-to-mid-career-momentum

**Judged 2026-08-05.** Winner: **Pitch B** (accumulation-first). Runner-up:
Pitch A (metaphor-first), one idea grafted in.

Graded against the two APPROVED rows in `design-system/docs/taste.md` only.
The 2026-08-05 `career-building-is-a-repeatable-process` cut was not consulted
as a reference and nothing here is anchored on it.

---

## Concept angle

**A trail marker the viewer already owns the foundation of — a stack of flat
stones with a post driven through it — is built up on screen from bare pole to
finished, loaded, resolved marker, and it is the only hero object in every beat
from beat 1 to the final beat (100% of runtime).**

---

## The carrying object

**A trail marker under construction, standing on the viewer's existing
experience.**

Drawn in flat SCLA-token shapes — no texture, no paper conceit, no wood grain,
no scenery. Every part is a stroke or a filled primitive the composition can
actually draw:

- **The stack (the foundation).** Roughly 24–40 unlabelled horizontal lozenges
  of varying width, stacked slightly off-centre so the column has an irregular
  real silhouette rather than a neat tower. Sits lower-left of centre. The
  count is a density choice for the builder — it is **never numbered on screen
  and never spoken**, because the script gives no number for the experience
  already built.
- **The post.** One tall rectangle driven down through the top of the stack,
  bare at the start. Near its top it later gains a short perpendicular
  **crossarm**.
- **The band (the progress bar).** One horizontal line running through the
  post's base. Solid to the **left** of the post. To the right of the post
  there is, at first, **nothing at all** — not a hollow outline, genuinely
  undrawn frame. (See "Grafted from the runner-up".)
- **The fittings.** Four small rounded **tabs** hooked over the post's shaft at
  different heights (the four platform tools). Three plain **trapezoids** hung
  from the crossarm on short strokes (the three pressures). Two thin **tether
  lines** running from the post down to a small driven stake at the lower-right
  frame edge (the two support routes).
- **The tracks.** 2–6px strokes fanning out of the post into the upper-right
  (the three questions). Later: two thinned to hairline ghosts, one thickened,
  ticked at even month intervals, carrying a span bracket across its sixth-to-
  twelfth tick and a short run of small even dashes leaving the post.
- **The plate.** A rectangle bolted across the post's face carrying the 90-day
  commitment sentence with a ruled blank in it, plus a stay-line from the
  plate's foot down into the base of the stack.

**Canvas discipline:** the assembly occupies the left-of-centre third early and
grows to roughly three-quarters of the canvas by the end. After the 50% mark
nothing on screen is a small prop floating beside large empty space — the
rejected thin cut's exact signature.

---

## Milestone frames — THE ACCUMULATION CONTRACT

Percentages are of total runtime (~2:00 at the pinned voice; the seconds are
indicative and the builder re-anchors them to real beat boundaries after
synthesis). Each milestone is a **different picture because the object changed**,
and each carries a stated negative constraint the contact sheet can be checked
against.

**25% (~0:30) — "A bare post on a settled stack."**
Lower-left: the stone column, freshly settled from a loose scatter, dense and
irregular. Through its top, a bare vertical post — **nothing hung on it**.
Through the post's base, the band, solid and running left to the frame edge and
**stopping dead at the post**. Right of the post and the whole upper-right:
empty. Silhouette: *one heap, one pole, one line that stops.*
→ *Constraint:* the post is bare here and **cannot** be bare at 50%.

**50% (~1:00) — "A loaded post under tension."**
Same stack, same stopped band. The post is now busy: four hooked tabs down the
shaft, a crossarm fixed near the top, three weights hanging from the crossarm
and pulling it to a slight one-time settled tilt, two tether lines angling
down-right to the small stake at the frame edge. Silhouette: *a pole carrying
more than it looks able to.* The upper-right is still empty, and that emptiness
is load-bearing for the next frame.
→ *Constraint:* the upper-right is empty here and **cannot** be empty at 75%.

**75% (~1:35) — "A three-way fan filling the sky."**
The densest frame in the video. Three equal-weight, equal-length tracks spray
out of the post into the upper-right and run off the frame edges, unresolved
and unranked. The weights still hang. The stones beneath have begun to turn.
Silhouette: *a crowded, undecided crossroads* — the only frame in which nothing
on screen is resolved.
→ *Constraint:* the three tracks are equal weight here and **cannot** be equal
at 100%; the resolution must be a geometry change, never a colour or a caption.

**100% (~2:00) — "One road, one plate, one blank."**
Pulled back one step. Two hairline ghosts where two tracks were. The third is
thick, ticked at even month intervals, carrying the six-to-twelve span bracket
and a short run of even dashes leaving the post — **and it now lands back onto
the band's line and carries it past the post to the right frame edge**, so the
band reads for the first time as one continuous route running *through* the
marker. The stones have finished re-facing and all angle toward the surviving
track. The plate is bolted across the post's face with the commitment sentence
and its ruled blank. A stay-line runs from the plate's foot into the base of
the stack. Silhouette: *a finished marker with exactly one empty space left in
it.*
→ *Constraint:* no two of these four frames share a silhouette. The sheet order
must be recoverable from the pictures alone.

## What accumulates

Four registers, three additive and one **re-arranging** — the re-arrangement is
the approved-reference signature and is the register a lazy build cannot fake:

1. **The stack:** loose scatter → settled irregular column → stones begin to
   turn → every stone faced toward the chosen track. *Same material throughout,
   re-sorted.* This is "reposition your experience" drawn literally, using
   nothing new.
2. **The post:** bare shaft → four hooked tabs → crossarm and three hanging
   weights → two tethers → a bolted plate across its face.
3. **The empty right of frame:** genuinely undrawn → three equal tracks
   consuming it → two ghosts and one ticked, bracketed, dashed road.
4. **The band:** stops dead at the post → still stops at the post → still stops
   → continues through and past it to the frame edge.

At 0:10 there is a scatter of stones: no post, no band, no track, no plate, and
nothing to pull back from. The closing frame is undrawable at 0:10, which is
the target sentence in `taste.md` met literally.

---

## The payoff beat

**The final beat — "When you're ready, click Next to begin."** The camera pulls
back one step and, for the first time, the whole marker reads as a single
finished object rather than a post being worked on. Four things re-read at once:

1. The heap the video opened with was never scenery — it is the **foundation
   the whole marker stands on**. The experience the viewer already has turns
   out to be what holds the assembly up.
2. The long horizontal line from 0:30 was not a rule; it is the **progress bar,
   with the post standing in it** — solid behind, and now drawn ahead.
3. The route **does not stop at the checkpoint.** For ninety percent of the
   runtime the band ended at the post and looked like a wall. In the last beat
   it runs through it. A checkpoint is a place you pause, not a place you stop.
4. Every shape on screen is filled, resolved, or chosen **except one**: the
   ruled blank on the plate. The single empty space left in a frame that spent
   two minutes filling up is precisely the thing the viewer is being asked to
   write.

## Grafted from the runner-up

**Pitch A's strongest single idea: the ground ahead starts genuinely blank, and
the closing pull-back is the first moment the route reads as one continuous
line *through* the checkpoint rather than a road that ends at it.**

Carried across because it repairs the one weak element in the winning pitch. In
Pitch B the band was drawn from 0:30 as "solid left, hollow outline right" and
then never changed again for ninety seconds — a static register sitting in the
frame doing nothing, which is exactly the rejected thin cut's failure at small
scale. Deleting the hollow outline and letting the surviving track *complete*
the band at 100% converts a dead element into a fourth accumulating register
and adds the video's sharpest re-read. It also puts a deliberate echo of the
APPROVED `m1_mini-syllabus` signature — a horizontal route with a mid-route
checkpoint that fills as the argument advances — underneath a richer primary
object, rather than making that line the whole carrier as it was there.

Pitch A's survey-sheet / inked-paper / wooden-plank rendering and its
camera-slide at 75% were **not** taken. The rendering is an illustration style
this pipeline cannot draw in flat brand tokens, and the 75% slide moves the
carrier off frame, which breaks carrier persistence and reads to `carrier-drift`
as an abandoned object.

## Judge's note — the failure mode, and the guard

**Most likely slide: the 50%–75% stretch degenerates into an attachment
checklist.** This is a course welcome, and the thin shape the owner rejects is
"here's what's coming" as a list. The disguised version of that list here is
one fitting hung per beat — tool tab, tool tab, tool tab, tool tab, support
tether, pressure, pressure, pressure — which is a bulleted list drawn
vertically down a pole. From a contact sheet it reads as one picture with new
headings, which is the thin cut exactly.

**Concrete guards, checkable from the snapshot grid:**

- **The four tool tabs enter as ONE group in ONE beat.** They are the sentence
  "you'll use simple tools in this platform", not four sentences. Same for the
  two support tethers: one beat, one gesture. The three pressures may land in
  one beat or as a single settling group — never one beat each.
- **No beat between 25% and 75% may leave BOTH the post's silhouette AND the
  right of frame unchanged from the previous beat.** Every beat in that stretch
  either changes the assembly's outline or consumes a named region of the
  previously undrawn right side.
- **Nothing on the marker is captioned with a label row.** The tabs, weights
  and tethers are never annotated with a matching text list beside them — that
  turns the hero illustration back into the icons-beside-bullets shape the rule
  set bans and gives a lazy build a way to differentiate beats with copy alone.
- **Every addition enters once and then holds pixel-static.** The crossarm tilt
  is a single settle at the moment the third weight hangs, never a loop; the
  stones' re-facing is one transition, not a drift. Idle keep-alive motion is
  the repo's most-violated rule and `check_motion.py` grades it.
- **Introduce no fact, count or sequence the script does not speak.** Every
  count on screen is one the narration says: four tools, two support routes,
  three pressures, three questions, the six-to-twelve span, 90 days. The stones
  are the deliberate exception — unlabelled and unnumbered, because the script
  gives no figure for experience already built.

## Scores

**Pitch A — metaphor-first (survey sheet, trail, post, tape).** *Name the
object:* strong in words, weak in buildable terms — "heavy inked line-work",
"footfall ticks", "a plain wooden plank", "untouched paper" describe an
illustration style rather than shapes this composition can draw in flat brand
tokens, so two builders would draw two different videos. *Four-frame test:*
four genuinely different frames, but three of the four differences are supplied
by the **camera** (wide → close → slid right → pull-back) rather than by the
object, and at 75% the camera slides off the post entirely — the carrier leaves
frame, which is a persistence break, not richness. *What accumulates:* very
good — blank ground → pressed ruts → inked road → tied road with stake and
measured tape, plus the bundles re-arranging from scattered to stacked.
*Payoff:* excellent, and the best single idea in either pitch — the route that
stopped at the post reads at the end as one continuous line through it.
*Lazy-build test:* partially open, because "the framing changes" can be
satisfied by scaling and panning one static drawing under new headings. Verdict:
**runner-up — the strongest idea in the competition inside the weaker
specification.**

**Pitch B — accumulation-first (stone stack, post, fittings, tracks, plate).**
*Name the object:* strongest of the two — a trail marker on a stack of stones,
specified in primitives, stroke weights and canvas share, so a builder draws
what the judge imagined. *Four-frame test:* four different silhouettes produced
by the object's own state, with the carrier on screen continuously from beat 1;
no camera trick is doing the work. *What accumulates:* four registers with one
genuine re-sort — the stones re-facing is "reposition your experience" drawn
using only material already on screen, which is precisely the signature the two
approved cuts share. Its one weakness was the band, static from 0:30 onward,
now repaired by the graft. *Payoff:* three simultaneous re-reads before the
graft, four after; the last unfilled shape in a filling frame being the thing
the viewer is asked to write is a genuinely earned close. *Lazy-build test:*
closed hardest of the two — it states negative constraints (the post cannot be
bare at 50%, the upper-right cannot be empty at 75%, the tracks cannot be equal
at 100%) that a contact sheet can be graded against directly. Verdict:
**WINNER.**
