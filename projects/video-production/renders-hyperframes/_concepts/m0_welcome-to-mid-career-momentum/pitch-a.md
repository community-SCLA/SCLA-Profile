# PITCH A — m0_welcome-to-mid-career-momentum

**Lens:** metaphor-first (what single concrete object carries the whole lesson and visibly accumulates).

---

## The carrying object

**One inked trail running left-to-right across a single survey sheet, with a
wooden checkpoint post standing mid-route where the walker has stopped.**

It is drawn as one continuous heavy line across the full width of the frame:
behind the post the line is thick, dark and crowded with small footfall ticks,
so the left half reads as ground already covered; ahead of the post it is bare
paper with nothing on it at all. The post is a plain vertical plank with a
crossbar, planted where the line stops. A measuring tape runs along the bottom
edge of the sheet, its marked section ending exactly at the post. Everything is
flat, inked line-work on one paper plane — no depth, no scenery, no scenic
landscape: a route, a post, a tape.

The whole lesson is one act — **the empty right half of the sheet gets
surveyed, chosen and inked** — and every beat is a mark being made on it.

---

## Four frames

**25% — Arrival at a bare post.**
Wide, sheet-flat. The left two-thirds of the frame is the dense inked trail with
its footfall ticks; the post stands just right of centre; the right third is
untouched paper. The measuring tape below is marked up to the post and blank
past it. Nobody has drawn anything ahead yet. The read: a long way already
walked, and a hard stop.

**50% — The post is busy; the ground ahead splits three ways.**
Camera in closer, base of the post crowded. The post now wears its checkpoint
band. Three bundles are set down at its foot — the loads being carried. A short
side-track breaks off from the post and runs to a small station drawn at the
sheet's edge, joined by a line so the post is never isolated. Marking
instruments lie open on the ground, mid-use. Ahead, three faint scored ruts fan
out from the post into the blank paper — pressed in, not inked. Crowded base,
three-way fan: a completely different composition from frame 1.

**75% — Two ruts fade; one becomes a road.**
Camera slides right, off the post, onto the ground ahead. Two of the three ruts
have dropped to pale grey. The third is now inked at full weight, and its near
stretch has been laid with short even cross-ties, evenly spaced, marching away
from the post. The bundles are no longer scattered — they are stacked in one
pile against the post, out of the way. Clean, ordered, right-weighted frame,
where frame 2 was cluttered and centred.

**100% — The whole sheet at once.**
Full pull-back to the entire survey sheet. One unbroken route runs corner to
corner *through* the post: dense footfall ticks behind, checkpoint post at
centre, inked and cross-tied road ahead. A stake is driven into the near
stretch of the new road carrying a written plaque — the commitment line, with
its blank still open. The side-track and station sit quietly off to one side.
The measuring tape is now marked past the post as well, so the road ahead has
distance on it rather than being unmeasured space.

---

## What accumulates

At 0:10 the right half of the frame is **blank paper**. At the end it carries
an inked route, evenly laid cross-ties, a driven stake with a written plaque,
and measured distance on the tape. That is new material on ground that did not
exist as a picture at the start — not a highlight moving.

Three separate registers accumulate, and one *re-arranges*:

- **The ground ahead:** blank → three pressed ruts → one inked road → a road
  with ties, a stake and a distance mark.
- **The post:** a bare plank → a banded checkpoint → a checkpoint with a
  written plaque staked beside it and a track running to help.
- **The tape:** marked only behind the post → marked through and past it.
- **The bundles (re-arrangement, not addition):** carried, then set down
  scattered, then stacked tidily against the post — the same objects, resolved.

The shuffle test passes on the ground-ahead register alone: you can restore the
order of any three sheets from how much of the right half is drawn.

---

## The payoff beat

**The final pull-back**, on the closing line. For the whole lesson the frame
has been cropped in on one half of the sheet at a time — first the walked half,
then the post, then the new ground. The last beat is the first and only moment
both halves are in frame together, and the route reads as **one continuous line
through the post** rather than a road that stops. The picture literally could
not have been drawn at 0:10: half of it was empty paper, and the stake carrying
the written commitment is the last mark made.

---

## Why this is not lazy

A builder cannot satisfy this with heading swaps against a static prop, because
**the prop is half-missing at the start and must be constructed on screen**:

1. **The same ground is drawn three times in three incompatible states** —
   blank paper, three pressed ruts fanning out, one inked road with cross-ties.
   No heading swap produces that; each state is different line-work in the same
   region of the frame.
2. **The framing itself changes on a fixed rule** — wide sheet (frame 1),
   close on the post (frame 2), slid right onto the new ground (frame 3), full
   pull-back (frame 4). A static prop with new text over it fails this on
   sight.
3. **Two-thirds of the marks are subtractive or re-arranging, not additive** —
   two ruts must FADE, the bundles must MOVE from scattered to stacked. A lazy
   build only ever adds; this concept requires the frame to resolve.
4. **The milestone contract is checkable from the contact sheet**: right half
   blank at 25%, three ruts at 50%, one inked tied road at 75%, stake plus
   full-sheet pull-back at 100%. Any beat where the right half of the sheet is
   unchanged from the previous beat's state is the defect.

**Anti-list guard.** This is a welcome, so the failure mode is a "here's what's
coming" list frame. The platform tools are drawn as **instruments in use making
the marks** at the post — never a labelled row of icons or cards. The support
channel is a **drawn side-track to a station**, not a bullet. The three
questions are **three ruts in the ground**, not three cards.

**Fidelity.** Every element is a re-reading of the narration and nothing else:
the career map and the mid-career checkpoint, the progress bar (the measuring
tape), the tools and the support channel (instruments and the side-track), the
three loads and the three questions (bundles and ruts), the one realistic next
move over the coming six to twelve months (one rut inked), the small consistent
steps (the even cross-ties), and the 90-day commitment written and submitted
(the staked plaque with its blank). No counts, facts or sequence claims beyond
what is spoken.
