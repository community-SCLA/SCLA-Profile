# PITCH B — m0_welcome-to-mid-career-momentum

**Lens: accumulation-first.** The build-up was designed before the object was
named. Runtime estimate: 278 narration words at the pinned voice ≈ **2:00**,
so the 30 / 60 / 90 / 120s marks land almost exactly on 25 / 50 / 75 / 100%.
Marks below are stated in seconds; a builder re-anchors them to real beat
boundaries after synthesis.

**Fidelity rule applied throughout:** every count on screen is a count the
narration itself speaks (4 platform tools, 2 support routes, 3 pressures,
3 questions, the six-to-twelve span, 90 days). The one uncounted element —
the stones — is deliberately never labelled and never spoken, because the
script gives no number for the experience already built.

---

## The accumulation ladder

The welcome opens **already loaded**, which is the whole trick. The script's
first real claim is *"you've built meaningful experience"* — so the frame does
not start empty and does not start with a list of what's coming. It starts
with a heap of material the viewer already owns, and everything after that is
weight, fittings, and sorting added to that heap.

**By ~30s — the heap gains a coordinate.**
What is physically more: a loose scatter of flat stones has settled into one
stacked column with a real height, and a bare post is now driven through its
top. A single horizontal band runs the full frame width through the post's
base — solid on the left of the post, hollow outline on the right.
*Gained:* structure where there was scatter; a position where there was none;
one long line that did not exist at 0:10.
*Reads:* "the mid-career checkpoint in your career map… you always know
exactly where you are and what's ahead" — the band **is** the progress bar,
and the post is the notch on it.

**By ~60s — the post gains fittings and a lifeline.**
What is physically more: four small hooked shapes now hang off the post's
shaft, a short crossarm has been fixed near its top, three weights hang from
that crossarm, and two thin tether lines run from the post down to a small
driven stake at the lower-right frame edge.
*Gained:* density on a previously bare pole; the post is now visibly carrying
things and is visibly held by something.
*Reads:* the four platform tools, the two support routes, and the three
pressures the script names — family responsibilities, leadership
expectations, a full calendar.

**By ~90s — the empty half of the frame gains structure.**
What is physically more: the upper-right, empty since 0:00, is now crossed by
three tracks fanning out of the post to the top and right edges, all three the
same weight, all three running to the frame edge unresolved. The stones under
the post have started to re-face.
*Gained:* the frame's dead space is consumed; this is the densest, most
loaded picture in the video, and the only one with visible tension in it.
*Reads:* "Should I push for promotion? Redesign my role to fit better? Or
change lanes entirely?"

**By ~120s — everything re-sorts and resolves.**
What is *not* more, but re-arranged: two of the three tracks thin to
hairlines; the third thickens, gains evenly-spaced month ticks with a span
bracket drawn across the sixth-to-twelfth, and takes a short run of small even
dashes leaving the post. The stones re-group so their faces all angle toward
the surviving track. A plate is bolted across the post's face carrying the
90-day commitment with a ruled blank in it, and a stay-line runs from the
plate's foot down into the base of the stack.
*Gained:* resolution, and one deliberately unfilled blank.
*Reads:* "focus on your next, realistic move over the next six to twelve
months… define that move, reposition your experience, and build momentum
through small, consistent steps" — *reposition* is drawn literally as the
already-present stones re-grouping, not as new material arriving. Then: "it
becomes your anchor for the rest of the track."

## The carrying object

**A trail marker being built on the viewer's own experience: a stack of flat
stones with a post driven through it, which gains fittings, load, a fan of
tracks, and finally a bolted plate.**

Drawn in flat SCLA-token shapes, no texture: the stones are ~24–40 unlabelled
horizontal lozenges of varying width, stacked slightly off-centre so the
column has a real silhouette (the count is a density choice for the builder,
never spoken, never numbered on screen). The post is a single tall rectangle
with a short perpendicular crossarm near the top; the four tools are small
rounded tabs hooked over the shaft at different heights, the three pressures
are plain trapezoids hung from the crossarm on short strokes, the tethers and
tracks are 2–6px strokes. The whole assembly occupies the left-of-centre
third early and grows to fill roughly three-quarters of the canvas by the end;
nothing is a small prop floating beside empty space after the 60s mark.

---

## Four frames

**25% (~0:30) — "A bare post on a settled stack."**
Lower-left: the stone column, freshly settled, dense and irregular. Through
its top, a bare vertical post, nothing on it. Across the full frame width at
the post's base, one long horizontal band — solid to the left of the post,
hollow to the right. Upper-right: empty. The picture is *one heap, one pole,
one line*.

**50% (~1:00) — "A loaded post under tension."**
Same stack, but the post is now busy: four hooked tabs down its shaft, a
crossarm, three weights hanging and pulling the crossarm to a slight
one-time-settled tilt, two tether lines angling down-right to a small stake at
the frame edge. The picture is *a pole carrying more than it looks able to*.
Upper-right still empty — that emptiness is load-bearing for the next frame.

**75% (~1:35) — "A three-way fan filling the sky."**
The busiest frame in the video. Three equal-weight tracks spray out of the
post into the upper-right and run off the frame edges, unresolved and
unranked. The weights still hang. The stones beneath have begun to turn. The
picture is *a crowded, undecided crossroads* — and it is the only frame where
nothing on screen has been resolved.

**100% (~2:00) — "One road, one plate, one blank."**
Pulled back a step. Two hairline ghosts where two tracks were; one thick
track with month ticks and a span bracket across the six-to-twelve, with a
short run of even dashes leaving the post. A plate bolted across the post's
face holding the 90-day commitment sentence and its ruled blank. A stay-line
from the plate's foot into the base of the stack. The stones now all face the
surviving track. The picture is *a finished marker with exactly one empty
space left in it*.

No two of those four share a silhouette. The order is recoverable from the
pictures alone.

## The payoff beat

**The final beat — "When you're ready, click Next to begin."** The camera
pulls back one step and, for the first time, the whole marker is visible at
once as a single object rather than as a post being worked on. Three things
re-read simultaneously:

1. The heap the video opened with was never scenery — it was the **foundation
   the marker is standing on**. The viewer's existing experience turns out to
   be the thing holding the whole assembly up.
2. The long horizontal band from 0:30, which read as a rule, is now obviously
   a **progress bar with the post standing in it** — solid behind, open ahead.
3. Every element on screen is filled, resolved, or chosen **except one**: the
   ruled blank on the plate. The last empty shape in a frame that spent two
   minutes filling up is the thing the viewer is being asked to write.

This frame is undrawable at 0:10 — at 0:10 there is a scatter of stones, no
post, no band, no track, and nothing to pull back from.

## Why this is not lazy

**The prop is not static — it is different hardware at every mark, and the
concept states the hardware, not the headings.** A builder cannot hold one
illustration and swap text over it, because the contract fixes the object's
physical state at named times:

- The post is **bare** at 25% and **cannot be bare** at 50% — four tabs, a
  crossarm and three weights must be attached, and each attachment lands on
  its own narration line.
- The upper-right is **empty** at 50% and **cannot be empty** at 75% — three
  tracks must consume it. A build that fills it early has nowhere left to go
  for forty seconds and the contact sheet shows it immediately.
- The three tracks are **equal weight** at 75% and **cannot be equal** at 100%
  — the resolution is a geometry change (two thin, one thick and ticked), not
  a colour or a caption.
- The stones **re-face** between 75% and 100%. That is a re-sort of material
  already on screen, which is exactly the approved-reference signature and is
  impossible to fake with copy.

Every beat's job in this concept is a named physical addition or re-sort with
a specific attachment point on a specific part of the object; not one beat is
differentiated by its heading alone. A heading-swap build would leave the
tether, crossarm and track geometry unattached to anything, which reads as
broken rather than as thin — the failure is visible, not merely dull.

**Guards this concept respects by construction:** the object is on screen
continuously from beat 1 to the last beat (carrier persistence); every
addition enters once and then holds pixel-static, including the crossarm tilt,
which is a single settle at the moment the third weight hangs and never a loop
(no in-place keep-alive motion); it is ONE hero illustration per frame with no
icons beside rows; and it introduces no fact, count, or sequence the narration
does not itself speak.
