# Visibility Actions — Concept Selection

## Lens A — The Contribution Spotlight (selected)

One oversized contribution occupies the full stage. At first, the work exists but sits outside the view of the people who could recognize or support it. Each visibility action changes the relationship: presenting places the work in a shared room, sharing carries evidence to a wider audience, asking creates a useful exchange, a stretch role expands demonstrated scope, and mentoring transfers capability. The spotlight is never about the person performing; it illuminates the useful contribution and makes its value legible to a named audience.

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Fidelity | 5/5 | Directly demonstrates work, action, audience understanding, reputation, and support. |
| Evolution | 5/5 | One contribution moves from unseen to shared, recognized, tested, and supported. |
| Attention | 5/5 | One large action and one visible consequence dominate each frame. |
| Feasibility | 5/5 | Full-stage HTML geometry and GSAP transforms fit the existing 17-beat timing cleanly. |
| **Total** | **20/20** | **Selected: clearest cause-and-effect story with no progress device.** |

## Lens B — Open the Door

The work begins behind a large closed architectural panel. Each action opens a different aperture toward decision-makers, sponsors, or peers; over time, the openings combine into a clear passage toward the Next Move Statement.

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Fidelity | 4/5 | Strong for access and visibility, weaker for specific actions such as mentoring or asking questions. |
| Evolution | 4/5 | Closed-to-open transformation is clear but risks repeating the same reveal. |
| Attention | 4/5 | Large apertures use the stage well, though multiple audience openings could become busy. |
| Feasibility | 5/5 | Straightforward deterministic panels and masks. |
| **Total** | **17/20** | **Not selected: less flexible for the full range of script examples.** |

## Selected thesis

**Visibility is a focused action that puts useful work in view, links it to your name, and gives others something concrete to understand and support.**

## Explanatory hierarchy

1. A short heading names the current principle.
2. One full-stage contribution/action demonstration carries the meaning.
3. A named audience or consequence changes visibly because of the action.
4. Secondary labels clarify the relationship without repeating the heading.

## Milestone frames

1. **Exists but unseen:** the contribution is substantial, but decision-makers cannot yet read its value.
2. **Useful context:** the spotlight shifts away from self-promotion and onto work, growth, and value for three named audiences.
3. **Visibility action:** a hand places the contribution into shared view, a name attaches to it, and repeated evidence establishes reputation.
4. **Examples in consequence:** Present, Share, Ask, Stretch, Mentor, and Teach each produce a different visible audience response.
5. **Focused, not loud:** a narrow spotlight illuminates one useful contribution while visual noise recedes.
6. **Design three:** Share, Ask, and Offer become three large action rehearsals tailored to a Next Move Statement.
7. **Test one:** one action enters a 30-day experiment and returns with a visible learning response.
8. **Support:** small repeated actions clarify future direction until another person can confidently extend support.

## Motion language

Motion depicts meaning only: place the work, open an audience view, attach a name, transfer knowledge, widen demonstrated scope, select an experiment, receive a response, and extend support. No sliding bars, progress rails, signal traces, decorative dots, counters, scene numbers, tiny canvases, or inert card decks.
