The Career Map Tool builds a personalized roadmap out of your goals and your preferences. We will walk through the tool first. Then we will take the career ideas it gives you and put them to work.

Open the Career Map Tool from your course or your dashboard. On the landing screen, look for the section titled Map Your Career. Click the button to begin. Depending on your setup, it might read Start or Get started.

Next comes the screen for choosing your career paths. Select up to three approaches that fit your aspirations and your values. The options may include Traditional path, Fast track, Entrepreneurial, Work-life balance, Location independent, and Social impact. Click the tiles that resonate most, then continue.

Now fill in the profile section so the tool can tailor your map. It asks for things like your name, your preferred industry, and your general background. In the experience area, upload or select a resume if it asks for one. That lines your map up with the skills you already have.

For each approach you selected, you can add more detail, like target industries and specific goals. Then click the button to generate or update your career map.

You can regenerate the map later. Choosing to regenerate brings up a confirmation first. It warns you that regenerating removes your old career approaches and jobs. Click Regenerate if you are sure, or Cancel if you are not.

When your map is ready, you get a summary. It tells you how many paths and how many jobs it generated, such as one path holding three jobs. Click Show more to expand the details on any path or job.

Then the tool lists your generated paths with quick stats. Those stats cover the number of roles, the industry, or the focus, like goal-oriented or work-life balance. Confirm with the button that selects your path.

On the Career Map overview page, review your personalized roadmap. Check the time frame, your preferred industry, and your five-year goal. Then do the same for every job in your path. You will see how responsibilities and expectations grow over time.

Now you have real ideas in front of you. The Career Map is a simple way to see your options side by side, instead of agonizing over them one at a time. Plot each option you are considering on two axes.

The horizontal axis is personal fit and energy. How well does this option match what you are good at and what energizes you? Use the themes you named in the previous activity. The vertical axis is long-term value, or career capital. How much does this build skills, connections, credentials, and options you will value later?

Four zones come out of that. High fit and high value is your Prioritize zone, so pursue those first. High value with uncertain fit lands in Explore, promising but unproven, and worth a cheap test. High fit and lower value is your Enjoy or Side zone, so keep that energy as a backup or a side pursuit. Low fit and low value goes to Park, and you set those aside without guilt.

The value is not the score itself. It is what you notice while you score. You might catch yourself thinking that the safe option is weak on everything you said mattered most. That noticing is the point.

There is a quadrant worksheet attached to this lesson, so use it while you plot. Seeing your options mapped does two things at once. It widens your frame, because you consider more than one path. It also applies the same criteria to every option you are weighing.
